WORKFORCE DEMOGRAPHICS RETURN
Placement Services Division — quarter ending 30 September 2016
Prepared by the Data and Reporting Officer for the Regional Operations Board

This return covers all individuals placed into temporary or fixed-term assignments by the Division during the quarter. The population of interest is 4,812 workers, each counted once regardless of the number of assignments held. Figures are drawn from the placement system extract taken on the first working day following the quarter end and reconciled against the payroll run for the same period. The fields tabulated are Age Band, Sex, Ethnicity, First Language, Gross Weekly Earnings Band and Postal District of Residence, all of which are captured at registration and refreshed at annual review.

By Age Band, the largest group is 25 to 34, at 1,491 workers or 31.0 per cent. The 16 to 24 band accounts for 1,082 or 22.5 per cent, and the 35 to 44 band for 1,006 or 20.9 per cent. Numbers fall away thereafter: 764 or 15.9 per cent in the 45 to 54 band, 402 or 8.4 per cent in the 55 to 64 band, and 67 or 1.4 per cent aged 65 and over. The age profile is unchanged in shape from the preceding quarter, with a shift of roughly one percentage point from the youngest band into the 25 to 34 band.

By Sex, 2,738 workers or 56.9 per cent are recorded as male and 2,067 or 43.0 per cent as female. A residual category of Sex Not Stated falls below the publication threshold and is therefore withheld, which is why the two published categories sum to 99.9 rather than 100 per cent.

By Ethnicity, the distribution is: White British 2,614 (54.3 per cent); Asian or Asian British 748 (15.5 per cent); White Other 691 (14.4 per cent); Black, African, Caribbean or Black British 486 (10.1 per cent); Mixed or Multiple ethnic groups 149 (3.1 per cent); Other ethnic group 87 (1.8 per cent); and Not Stated 37 (0.8 per cent).

By First Language, English is recorded for 3,908 workers or 81.2 per cent. The next most frequent are Polish at 341 (7.1 per cent), Romanian at 178 (3.7 per cent), Urdu at 121 (2.5 per cent), Portuguese at 96 (2.0 per cent) and Lithuanian at 61 (1.3 per cent). All remaining languages are combined into a single residual of 107 workers or 2.2 per cent, since several would otherwise fall below the threshold when reported separately.

By Gross Weekly Earnings Band, calculated on average earnings across weeks worked in the quarter, 604 workers or 12.6 per cent fall under £200; 1,438 or 29.9 per cent in the £200 to £299 band; 1,502 or 31.2 per cent in the £300 to £399 band; 809 or 16.8 per cent in the £400 to £499 band; and 459 or 9.5 per cent at £500 and above.

By Postal District of Residence, the six most frequently occurring districts account for 2,550 workers or 53.0 per cent of the population, distributed as LS9 612 (12.7 per cent), LS11 548 (11.4 per cent), LS10 431 (9.0 per cent), WF1 397 (8.2 per cent), WF2 288 (6.0 per cent) and BD3 274 (5.7 per cent). The remaining 2,262 workers or 47.0 per cent are grouped as All Other Districts.

Small-cell suppression applies throughout. Any cell with a count of fewer than ten has been withheld and, where withholding a single cell would allow it to be recovered by subtraction, the adjacent smallest cell has also been withheld or the categories combined. Only district-level geography is published; no finer geographic breakdown is released in this return or supplied on request. Cross-tabulations of two or more of the above fields are not published at any level.
