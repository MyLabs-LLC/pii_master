TITLE 12 — BANKS AND BANKING
CHAPTER 47 — SAFEGUARDING OF NONPUBLIC PERSONAL INFORMATION HELD BY STATE-CHARTERED CREDIT UNIONS

SUBCHAPTER A — GENERAL PROVISIONS

    § 47.101 Purpose and Scope.
        (a) This chapter establishes the minimum standards a covered institution shall observe in the collection, retention, use, disclosure, and destruction of records containing personal information relating to natural persons.
        (b) This chapter applies to every credit union chartered under Title 8, and to any service provider acting for such an institution under written contract.
        (c) Nothing in this chapter relieves a covered institution of an obligation imposed by any other law of this State or of the United States. Where standards conflict, the more protective standard governs.

    § 47.102 Definitions.
        As used in this chapter, unless the context requires otherwise:
        (a) "Covered institution" means an entity described in § 47.101(b).
        (b) "Personal information" means an individual's first name or first initial and last name in combination with any one or more of the following data elements, when either the name or the data element is not encrypted or redacted:
            (1) Social Security number or other taxpayer identification number;
            (2) driver's license number or State identification card number;
            (3) financial account number, credit card number, or debit card number, with or without any required security code, access code, personal identification number, or password permitting access to the account;
            (4) date of birth in combination with mother's maiden name or other knowledge-based authentication response;
            (5) biometric identifier, including fingerprint, voice print, or retina or iris image.
        (c) "Direct identifier" means a data element listed in subsection (b)(1) through (b)(5).
        (d) "Indirect identifier" means a data element that does not itself identify an individual but that, in combination with other reasonably available information, permits identification. The term includes ZIP code, employer name, membership tenure, and branch of first account opening.
        (e) "Record" means any material on which information is recorded or preserved, regardless of physical form, including paper, microform, magnetic media, optical media, and electronic transmission.
        (f) "Holder of records" means a covered institution, or an officer or employee thereof, having custody or control of a record containing personal information.
        (g) "Records Custodian" means the officer designated under § 47.201(a).
        (h) "Breach of the security of the system" means unauthorized acquisition of computerized data that compromises the security, confidentiality, or integrity of personal information maintained by a covered institution. Good faith acquisition by an employee or agent for a purpose of the institution is not a breach, provided the information is not subject to further unauthorized disclosure.
        (i) "Disposal" means the discarding or abandonment of records, or the sale, donation, or transfer of any medium on which records are stored.

SUBCHAPTER B — OBLIGATIONS OF HOLDERS OF RECORDS

    § 47.201 Designation and Program.
        (a) Each covered institution shall designate in writing one or more officers, to be titled the Records Custodian, responsible for coordinating the information security program required by this section.
        (b) The program shall be documented, shall be appropriate to the size and complexity of the institution and the nature and scope of its activities, and shall be reviewed by the board of directors not less than annually.
        (c) The program shall be reasonably designed to:
            (1) ensure the security and confidentiality of member records;
            (2) protect against anticipated threats or hazards to the security or integrity of such records; and
            (3) protect against unauthorized access to or use of such records that could result in substantial harm or inconvenience to a member.

    § 47.202 Access Limitation.
        (a) A holder of records shall permit access to personal information only by persons whose duties reasonably require access.
        (b) The Records Custodian shall maintain an access register recording, for each grant of access, the following fields: position title; system or file to which access is granted; identifier categories accessible; date access granted; approving officer title; date access revoked; and reason for revocation.
        (c) Access shall be reviewed not less than semiannually and revoked upon transfer or separation not later than the close of the next business day.

    § 47.203 Retention and Disposal.
        (a) A holder of records shall retain records containing personal information only so long as required by law or reasonably necessary for a business purpose, and thereafter shall dispose of them under subsection (b).
        (b) Disposal shall render personal information unreadable and unreconstructable, by shredding, pulping, incineration, degaussing, or destruction of the storage medium.
        (c) A disposal log shall be kept for six years and shall record: record series; volume or item count; identifier categories present; method of destruction; date of destruction; and title of the witnessing officer.

    § 47.204 Third-Party Service Providers.
        (a) A covered institution shall take reasonable steps to select and retain service providers capable of maintaining appropriate safeguards.
        (b) Each contract shall require the service provider to implement and maintain such safeguards and to notify the Records Custodian of any breach of the security of the system without unreasonable delay.

SUBCHAPTER C — NOTIFICATION AND ENFORCEMENT

    § 47.301 Notification of Breach.
        (a) Upon discovery of a breach, the Records Custodian shall commence a good faith investigation without unreasonable delay.
        (b) Notice shall be given to each affected individual in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay, consistent with the legitimate needs of law enforcement and with measures necessary to determine the scope of the breach and restore the reasonable integrity of the data system.
        (c) Notice shall be given to the Commissioner not later than five business days after the institution concludes that notice under subsection (b) is required, and shall state: the date or estimated date range of the breach; the date of discovery; the identifier categories involved; the approximate number of individuals affected; and the remedial measures taken or planned.
        (d) Notice may be delayed if a law enforcement agency determines in writing that notice will impede a criminal investigation.

    § 47.302 Penalties.
        (a) A covered institution that violates this chapter is subject to a civil penalty of not more than $2,500 for each violation, and not more than $25,000 for a series of violations of a like nature occurring within a single examination cycle.
        (b) Each day a violation continues after written notice from the Commissioner constitutes a separate violation.
        (c) A holder of records who knowingly and willfully discloses personal information in violation of § 47.202 commits a misdemeanor punishable by a fine of not more than $5,000, imprisonment of not more than one year, or both.
        (d) In determining the amount of a penalty, the Commissioner shall consider the good faith of the institution, the gravity of the violation, any history of prior violations, and the extent to which the institution had implemented the program required by § 47.201.
        (e) Nothing in this section creates a private right of action.
