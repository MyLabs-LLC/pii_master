UNIFORM ADMINISTRATIVE RULE 12.7 — DISCLOSURE OF PERSONAL INFORMATION TO CONTRACTORS AND SERVICE PROVIDERS
(Excerpt, subsections .7-3 through .7-6)

.7-3 Applicability. This rule applies whenever an agency of record discloses personal information to a contractor for the purpose of test scoring, transportation routing, food service administration, health screening, data hosting, print or mail fulfillment, or student information system support.

.7-4 Required contract provisions. No disclosure shall be made unless the contract contains provisions requiring the contractor to —

 * limit collection to the data elements enumerated in an attached schedule, identified by element label and source system field name;
 * use the information solely for the purposes stated, and not for advertising, product development, or resale;
 * maintain administrative, technical, and physical safeguards not less protective than those imposed on the agency by Rule 12.4;
 * restrict subcontracting except upon prior written approval, and bind any approved subcontractor to identical terms;
 * notify the Records Custodian of any breach within seventy-two hours of discovery;
 * return or destroy all personal information within thirty days of contract expiration or termination, and furnish a certificate of destruction;
 * submit to audit by the agency or its designee upon ten days' notice.

.7-5 Schedule of elements. The schedule required by .7-4 shall be maintained in tabular form and shall identify, for each element disclosed:

 * Element label
 * Source system and field name
 * Classification (public / directory / confidential / restricted)
 * Purpose of disclosure
 * Transmission method
 * Retention period

.7-6 Sanctions. Where a contractor materially breaches a provision required by .7-4, the agency shall —

 * suspend transmission of personal information immediately;
 * declare default and pursue liquidated damages as provided in the contract;
 * report the contractor to the Department for placement on the register of ineligible vendors for a period of not less than three years;
 * assess against the contractor the documented cost of notification, credit monitoring, and call center services provided to affected individuals.
