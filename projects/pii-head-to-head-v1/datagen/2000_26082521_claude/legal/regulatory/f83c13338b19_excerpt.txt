ARTICLE III — NOTIFICATION OF UNAUTHORIZED ACQUISITION

301. Any holder of records that owns or licenses computerized data including personal information shall, upon discovering or being notified of a breach of the security of the system, conduct in good faith a prompt and reasonable investigation to determine the likelihood that personal information has been or will be misused. If the investigation determines that misuse of personal information has occurred or is reasonably likely to occur, the holder of records shall give notice as provided in this Article.

302. Notice shall be given in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay, but in no event later than forty-five days following the conclusion of the investigation, consistent with the legitimate needs of law enforcement as provided in section 305 and with any measures necessary to determine the scope of the breach and to restore the reasonable integrity of the data system. Where the holder of records maintains data on behalf of another, notice shall be given to the owner or licensee of the data within ten days of discovery, and the owner or licensee shall be responsible for notification to affected individuals.

303. The notice shall be written in plain language and shall include, to the extent known at the time of issuance: a general description of the incident; the categories of personal information reasonably believed to have been the subject of the unauthorized acquisition, identified by data element category and not by value; the approximate date or date range of the incident; the general steps taken by the holder of records to protect the information from further unauthorized acquisition; advice directing the recipient to remain vigilant by reviewing account statements and monitoring credit reports; and a telephone contact maintained by the office of the Records Custodian through which further information may be obtained.

304. Substitute notice may be given where the cost of providing written notice would exceed two hundred fifty thousand dollars, where the affected class exceeds five hundred thousand persons, or where the holder of records lacks sufficient contact information. Substitute notice shall consist of electronic mail notice where an electronic mail address is on file, conspicuous posting on the internet website of the holder of records for a period of not less than ninety days, and notification to major statewide media.

305. Notice required by this Article may be delayed if a law enforcement agency determines in writing that notification will impede a criminal investigation. Notice shall be made after the agency determines that it will not compromise the investigation.
