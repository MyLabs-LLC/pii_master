CHAPTER 44 — PROTECTION OF PERSONAL INFORMATION
SUBCHAPTER F — ENFORCEMENT AND PENALTIES
(Excerpt: §§ 44-118 to 44-120)

1. A record holder who fails to designate a Records Custodian, or who fails to maintain the register prescribed by § 44-103(a), commits a violation of this chapter. Each calendar quarter during which the failure continues shall constitute a separate violation, and the fact that no unauthorised acquisition of a record has occurred shall not be a defence, though it may be taken in mitigation.

2. A record holder who discloses a protected identifier category to a person other than a person whose role title appears upon the access roster maintained under § 44-103(b) commits a violation of this chapter, unless the disclosure was made pursuant to lawful process, to a governmental authority in the discharge of a customs, immigration or safety function, or to a consignee or its designated agent for the sole purpose of effecting delivery.

3. A record holder who disposes of a record otherwise than in the manner prescribed by § 44-112 commits a violation of this chapter, and the person by whom the disposal was physically effected shall not thereby be relieved of liability where that person acted contrary to written instruction.

4. Civil penalties shall be assessed by the Commissioner upon the following scale, per violation: for a first violation, not more than one thousand dollars; for a second violation occurring within twenty-four months of the first, not more than five thousand dollars; and for a third or subsequent violation within that period, not more than twenty-five thousand dollars. In assessing a penalty the Commissioner shall consider the number of persons whose identifier categories were affected, the sensitivity of the categories, the degree of culpability, the history of prior violations, and the remedial measures undertaken.

5. Where a violation is found to have been wilful, and where the record holder derived or intended to derive pecuniary benefit from the disclosure, the Commissioner may refer the matter for prosecution, and upon conviction the offence shall be punishable as a misdemeanour of the first degree.

6. Nothing in this subchapter shall be construed to limit any remedy otherwise available at law or in equity, nor to create a private right of action for a violation of § 44-103 standing alone.
