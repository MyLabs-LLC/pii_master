SECTION 7. DISPOSAL OF RECORDS; NOTICE OF UNAUTHORIZED ACQUISITION.

7.1 Standards for disposal. When a record containing personal information is no longer to be retained under the schedule maintained by the Records Custodian, the holder of records shall dispose of it so that the personal information cannot practicably be read or reconstructed. Paper records shall be redacted, burned, pulverized, or shredded. Electronic media and other non-paper media shall be destroyed or erased so that the information cannot practicably be read or reconstructed. Disposal performed by a contracted vendor shall be evidenced by a certificate of destruction retained in the compliance file and identifying, at minimum, the Record Series Title, the Volume or Container Count, the Method of Destruction, the Date of Destruction, and the Witnessing Role Title.

7.2 Notice of breach. A holder of records that maintains records containing personal information and that knows or has reason to know of an unauthorized acquisition of, or unauthorized access to, unencrypted personal information, or of encrypted personal information together with the confidential process or key capable of compromising the security of that information, shall provide notice as soon as practicable and without unreasonable delay. Notice shall be given to the Attorney General, to the Department, and to the affected residents. The notice to the resident shall not describe the nature of the breach or the number of residents affected, and shall be limited to the resident's right to obtain a police report, instructions for requesting a security freeze, and the fees, if any, that may be charged.

7.3 Cooperation with investigation. Delay of notice is permitted only where a law enforcement agency determines in writing that notice would impede a criminal investigation. The Records Custodian shall document each such determination in the compliance file and shall issue notice immediately upon release by that agency.
