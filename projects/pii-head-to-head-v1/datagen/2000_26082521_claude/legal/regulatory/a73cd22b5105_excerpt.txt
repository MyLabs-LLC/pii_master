§ 2. DEFINITIONS.
As used in this chapter, unless the context otherwise requires:

(a) "Holder of records" means any person or entity that acquires, maintains, stores, or uses records containing personal information in the ordinary course of business, including a title insurance producer, escrow agent, or settlement service provider, whether or not the holder is the originator of the record.

(b) "Personal information" means an individual's first name or first initial and last name in combination with one or more of the following data elements, where either the name or the data element is not encrypted or redacted:
    (1) Social Security number;
    (2) driver's license number or state identification card number;
    (3) financial account number, credit card number, or debit card number, alone or in combination with any required security code, access code, or password permitting access to an account;
    (4) taxpayer identification number;
    (5) medical or health insurance information;
    (6) biometric identifier, including fingerprint, retina image, or voiceprint;
    (7) username or electronic mail address in combination with a password or security question answer.

(c) "Record" means any material on which written, drawn, spoken, visual, or electromagnetic information is recorded or preserved, regardless of physical form or characteristics. "Record" does not include publicly available information lawfully made available to the general public from federal, state, or local government records.

(d) "Disposal" means the discarding, abandonment, sale, donation, transfer, or relinquishment of control over a record.

§ 3. DUTY TO SAFEGUARD.
A holder of records shall implement and maintain reasonable security procedures and practices appropriate to the nature of the information and the size and complexity of the holder's operations, to protect personal information from unauthorized access, acquisition, destruction, use, modification, or disclosure. The duty imposed by this section is continuing and is not discharged by delegation to a third party.

§ 4. DISPOSAL OF RECORDS.
(a) A holder of records shall, when the record is no longer to be retained, dispose of it by shredding, erasing, or otherwise modifying the personal information to render it unreadable or indecipherable.
(b) The Records Custodian shall maintain a disposal log recording, at minimum, the following fields: Record Series; Volume; Method of Destruction; Date of Destruction; Certifying Officer (title only); Vendor Certificate Reference.

§ 5. THIRD-PARTY SERVICE PROVIDERS.
A holder of records that discloses personal information to a nonaffiliated third party under a written contract shall require by that contract that the third party implement and maintain reasonable security procedures consistent with § 3.

§ 6. PENALTIES.
(a) A violation of this chapter constitutes an unfair or deceptive act or practice.
(b) The Commissioner may assess a civil penalty of not more than $500 for each violation, not to exceed $50,000 for a series of violations arising from a single act or omission.
(c) Each record containing personal information that is disposed of in violation of § 4 constitutes a separate violation.
(d) Nothing in this section limits the right of an aggrieved individual to bring an action for actual damages.
