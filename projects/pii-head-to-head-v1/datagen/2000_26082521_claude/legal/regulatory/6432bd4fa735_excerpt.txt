§ 9-419. PENALTIES; ENFORCEMENT; CIVIL REMEDIES

(a) Wilful unauthorized disclosure. Any officer or employee of a holder of records who, by virtue of employment or official position, has possession of or access to a record containing personal information and who wilfully discloses the record, or any direct identifier or indirect identifier contained therein, to any person or agency not entitled to receive it, shall be guilty of a misdemeanour and, upon conviction, shall be fined not more than five thousand dollars, or imprisoned for not more than one year, or both, and shall in addition be subject to removal from office or dismissal from employment.

(b) Obtaining records under false pretences. Any person who knowingly and wilfully requests or obtains a record containing personal information from a holder of records under false pretences shall be guilty of a misdemeanour and, upon conviction, shall be fined not more than five thousand dollars.

(c) Wilful maintenance of an unregistered system. Any officer of a holder of records who wilfully maintains a record system without having caused the notice required by § 9-406 to be published shall be liable to a civil penalty of not more than one thousand dollars for each thirty-day period during which the failure continues.

(d) Civil action by the individual concerned. Whenever a holder of records (i) refuses to amend a record in accordance with the individual's request, (ii) refuses to permit inspection of a record, (iii) fails to maintain a record with such accuracy, relevance, timeliness, and completeness as is necessary to assure fairness in any determination concerning the individual, and consequently a determination adverse to the individual is made, or (iv) fails to comply with any other provision of this chapter in such a way as to have an adverse effect upon the individual, the individual may bring a civil action in the circuit court of the county in which the individual resides or in which the holder of records maintains its principal office.

(e) Relief. In an action brought under subsection (d), the court may order the amendment or production of the record, and shall award actual damages sustained by the individual but in no case less than the sum of one thousand dollars, together with the costs of the action and reasonable attorney fees, where the court determines that the holder of records acted in a manner which was intentional or wilful.

(f) Limitation. An action under this section shall be commenced within two years from the date on which the cause of action arises, save that where the holder of records has materially and wilfully misrepresented information required to be disclosed, and the information so misrepresented is material to the establishment of liability, the action may be brought at any time within two years after discovery of the misrepresentation.
