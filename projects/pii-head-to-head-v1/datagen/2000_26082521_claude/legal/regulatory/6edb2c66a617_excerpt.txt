SUBCHAPTER C — NOTIFICATION OF BREACH

§ 44.301
Upon discovery of, or receipt of notification of, a breach of the security of the system, a licensee shall conduct in good faith a prompt and reasonable investigation to determine the likelihood that personal information has been or will be misused.

§ 44.302
If the investigation determines that misuse has occurred or is reasonably likely to occur, the licensee shall give notice to each affected individual in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay, but in no event later than forty-five (45) days following determination, subject to the legitimate needs of law enforcement under § 44.305.

§ 44.303
Notice shall be given by one or more of the following methods:
 (1) written notice to the last known mailing address of record;
 (2) electronic notice, where the individual has consented to receive communications electronically and such consent has not been withdrawn;
 (3) telephonic notice, provided contact is made directly with the individual and a log of the contact is retained;
 (4) substitute notice, where the cost of notice would exceed two hundred fifty thousand dollars, the affected class exceeds five hundred thousand individuals, or the licensee lacks sufficient contact information. Substitute notice shall consist of all of the following: electronic mail notice to those addresses on file; conspicuous posting on the licensee's internet website for not less than thirty (30) days; and notification to statewide media.

§ 44.304
Each notice shall contain, in plain language:
 (a) the date or estimated date range of the breach;
 (b) a description of the categories of personal information involved, stated by category and not by value;
 (c) a general description of the incident;
 (d) the toll-free contact established by the licensee for inquiries;
 (e) the toll-free numbers and mailing addresses of the consumer reporting agencies;
 (f) advice to the individual to remain vigilant and to review account statements and credit reports;
 (g) whether notice was delayed at the request of law enforcement.

§ 44.305
Notice may be delayed if a law enforcement agency determines in writing that notification will impede a criminal investigation. Notice shall be made promptly after the agency communicates that delay is no longer required.

§ 44.306
If a single breach requires notice to more than five hundred (500) residents of this state, the licensee shall, no later than the date individual notice is given, provide written notice to the Commissioner and to the Attorney General stating: the number of residents notified, the categories of personal information involved, the date of discovery, the steps taken to contain the breach, and the remedial services offered.

§ 44.307
A licensee shall maintain a record of each investigation conducted under § 44.301 for a period of five (5) years, including investigations concluding that notice was not required, together with the written basis for that conclusion.
