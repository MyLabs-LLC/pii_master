CHAPTER 9 — NOTIFICATION OF BREACH OF SECURITY OF DATA SYSTEMS

9.1 Trigger. Upon discovery, or upon receipt of notice from a vendor or contractor, of any breach as defined at Section 21-4-102(f), the Records Custodian shall commence an investigation without unreasonable delay and in no event later than the close of the second business day following discovery. The investigation shall determine the scope of the breach, the categories of data elements involved, the number of individuals affected, and whether the information has been or is reasonably likely to be misused.

9.2 Notice to Affected Individuals. Where the investigation concludes that personally identifiable information has been or is reasonably believed to have been acquired by an unauthorized person, the district shall give notice to each affected individual, or to the parent or guardian where the individual is a minor, in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay, consistent with the legitimate needs of law enforcement and with any measures necessary to determine the scope of the breach and restore the reasonable integrity of the data system. Notice shall be given in writing by first-class mail to the last address of record, or by electronic means where the individual has affirmatively consented to electronic notice. The notice shall be written in plain language and shall set forth: the date or estimated date of the breach; a description, by field label, of the categories of information involved; the steps the district has taken to contain the breach; the steps the individual may take to protect against identity theft, including the placement of a fraud alert or security freeze; the toll-free telephone numbers and addresses of the major consumer reporting agencies; and a contact point at the district, identified by position title, from which further information may be obtained. The notice shall not restate the individual's account number, identification number, or other affected identifier.

9.3 Substitute Notice. Where the cost of providing individual notice would exceed one hundred thousand dollars, where the class of affected individuals exceeds fifty thousand persons, or where the district lacks sufficient contact information, substitute notice may be given by conspicuous posting upon the district's internet website for a period of not less than ninety days together with notification to major statewide media.

9.4 Notice to the Department and the Attorney General. Where a breach affects more than five hundred individuals, the Records Custodian shall notify the Department and the Office of the Attorney General not later than ten business days following discovery, upon the form prescribed by the Department, which form shall require the following particulars and no others: District Code; System of Records Affected; Classification Level; Date of Breach; Date of Discovery; Method of Discovery; Cause Code; Categories of Elements Involved; Count of Individuals Affected by Category; Containment Measures Taken; Date Notice Issued; Method of Notice; and Certifying Official (Title).

9.5 Vendor Obligations. Every contract under which a vendor receives, stores, or processes personally identifiable information on behalf of a district shall contain a covenant obligating the vendor to notify the Records Custodian of any breach within twenty-four hours of discovery, to cooperate fully in the district's investigation, to bear the cost of notice and of credit monitoring where the breach is attributable to the vendor's act or omission, and to return or securely destroy all such information upon termination of the contract. A contract lacking such covenant shall be voidable at the election of the district.

9.6 Penalty. Failure to give notice as required by this Chapter shall subject the district to a civil penalty of not more than five hundred dollars per individual not timely notified, not to exceed fifty thousand dollars for any single breach, and shall be reported by the Department to the governing board at a public meeting.
