Sec. 9. Enforcement; Civil Penalties; Private Remedies; Recordkeeping.

9.1 General enforcement authority. The Department shall enforce this Article. The Department may, on its own initiative or upon the written complaint of any person, examine the books, papers, records, and premises of any holder of records for the purpose of determining compliance. Examination shall be conducted during ordinary business hours upon reasonable notice, except that notice is not required where the Department has reason to believe that records subject to examination are in immediate danger of alteration, concealment, or destruction. A holder of records shall make its written information security program, its records inventory, its disposal certificates, and its training attendance logs available to the examiner. Refusal to permit examination is itself a violation of this Article and is separately punishable under subsection 9.3.

9.2 Compliance record. Each holder of records shall maintain a compliance file containing, in a form permitting ready inspection, the following entries: Program Adoption Date; Program Revision Date; Records Custodian Role Title; Annual Risk Assessment Date; Incident Log Reference; Employee Training Session Dates; Service Provider Contract Review Date; and Disposal Certificate Index Number. Entries shall be retained for not less than three years from the date of the entry, or for such longer period as any other applicable law requires. Where the holder of records is an employment agency or temporary help service, the compliance file shall also record the date on which each assignment file containing applicant identifiers was transferred to a client employer, the role title of the person authorizing the transfer, and the categories of data elements transferred.

9.3 Civil penalties. A holder of records that violates any provision of this Article, or any rule adopted under it, is liable for a civil penalty of not more than five thousand dollars for each violation. Each record containing the personal information of a separate individual that is maintained, disclosed, or disposed of in violation of this Article constitutes a separate violation, provided that the aggregate penalty arising from a single act or omission affecting multiple records shall not exceed one hundred thousand dollars unless the Department finds that the act or omission was willful or was the second or subsequent occurrence within a period of two years. In determining the amount of the penalty, the Department shall consider the good faith of the holder of records, the gravity of the violation, the history of previous violations, and the demonstrated efforts of the holder to remedy the violation and to prevent its recurrence.

9.4 Injunctive relief. The Attorney General may bring an action in a court of competent jurisdiction to restrain a violation of this Article and to compel compliance. In such an action the court may order any relief the court considers necessary or proper, including the appointment of a receiver over the records at issue and an order directing the destruction, return, or sequestration of records unlawfully obtained or retained.

9.5 Effect on other remedies. Nothing in this Article limits the right of any individual to bring an action for damages arising from the unauthorized disclosure of personal information under any other law, nor does compliance with this Article constitute a defense to any such action. A violation of this Article does not by itself create a private right of action, but may be pleaded as evidence of the standard of care applicable to a holder of records.
