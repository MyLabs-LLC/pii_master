SUBCHAPTER D — BREACH NOTIFICATION AND ENFORCEMENT

  § 12.209  Discovery and Assessment of Unauthorized Acquisition.

      (a)  Presumption.  Any acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of a protected
           record in a manner not permitted by this chapter is presumed to be a
           breach unless the holder demonstrates, through a documented risk
           assessment, that there is a low probability that the identifier elements
           have been compromised.

      (b)  Risk Assessment Factors.  The assessment required by paragraph (a) shall
           address, at a minimum:

           (1)  the nature and extent of the identifier elements involved, including
                the categories enumerated at § 12.202(c) and the likelihood of
                re-identification;

           (2)  the identity of the unauthorized person who used the protected
                record or to whom the disclosure was made;

           (3)  whether the protected record was actually acquired or viewed; and

           (4)  the extent to which the risk has been mitigated.

      (c)  The assessment shall be entered in the compliance register maintained by
           the Records Custodian and shall be preserved for six (6) years.

  § 12.210  Notification.

      (a)  To Individuals.  Notification shall be made without unreasonable delay
           and in no case later than sixty (60) calendar days following discovery.

      (b)  To the Department.  Where a single breach involves five hundred (500) or
           more individuals, the holder shall notify the Department contemporaneously
           with notification under paragraph (a). All other breaches shall be
           reported in an annual log submitted not later than sixty (60) days after
           the close of the calendar year.

      (c)  Content.  Each notification shall describe the categories of identifier
           elements involved but shall not itself reproduce any identifier element.

  § 12.211  Penalties.

      (a)  Tier I — Unknowing Violation.  Not less than one hundred dollars ($100)
           nor more than fifty thousand dollars ($50,000) per violation.

      (b)  Tier II — Reasonable Cause.  Not less than one thousand dollars ($1,000)
           nor more than fifty thousand dollars ($50,000) per violation.

      (c)  Tier III — Willful Neglect, Timely Corrected.  Not less than ten thousand
           dollars ($10,000) nor more than fifty thousand dollars ($50,000) per
           violation, provided correction is effected within thirty (30) days of the
           date the holder knew or should have known of the violation.

      (d)  Tier IV — Willful Neglect, Uncorrected.  Not less than fifty thousand
           dollars ($50,000) per violation.

      (e)  Annual Cap.  The aggregate of penalties imposed upon any one holder for
           all violations of an identical provision within a single calendar year
           shall not exceed one million five hundred thousand dollars ($1,500,000).

      (f)  Criminal Referral.  Nothing in this section shall preclude referral to the
           appropriate prosecuting authority where a violation was committed
           knowingly and with intent to sell, transfer, or use an identifier element
           for commercial advantage, personal gain, or malicious harm.
