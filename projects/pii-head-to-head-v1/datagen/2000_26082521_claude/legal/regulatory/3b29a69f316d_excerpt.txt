CHAPTER 15, SUBCHAPTER D — ENFORCEMENT, PENALTIES, AND REMEDIES
(Excerpt)

§ 15-241. Prohibited acts. It is a violation of this chapter for a person to do any of the following:
  (a) Knowingly disclose personally identifiable information from a pupil record to a person not authorized to receive it.
  (b) Access a pupil record for a purpose unrelated to the person's assigned duties, including access undertaken out of curiosity or on behalf of a third party.
  (c) Sell, license, or exchange for consideration any pupil record or derivative dataset, or use such a record for targeted advertising.
  (d) Fail to designate a Records Custodian as required by § 15-203(a).
  (e) Fail to maintain the disclosure log required by § 15-208, or to enter in that log the date of disclosure, the recipient's name and organizational affiliation, the identifier categories disclosed, the stated purpose, and the legal authority relied upon.
  (f) Retaliate against an employee or a parent for reporting a suspected violation of this chapter.

§ 15-242. Duty upon discovery of unauthorized acquisition.
  (a) An agency that discovers, or has reason to believe, that protected information has been acquired by an unauthorized person shall commence an investigation without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 3 business days after discovery.
  (b) The agency shall notify the department on the prescribed form. The form shall state the date range of the incident, the number of records involved, the identifier categories involved, the systems affected, and the containment measures taken. The form shall not include the information itself.
  (c) The agency shall notify each affected parent or eligible pupil not later than 45 days after discovery, unless a law enforcement agency certifies in writing that notification would impede an active investigation, in which case the period is tolled for the duration certified.
  (d) Where the incident involves an element classified as Confidential-Sensitive, the agency shall offer identity protection services for a period of not less than 12 months at no cost to the affected individual.

§ 15-243. Civil penalties.
  (a) A person who violates § 15-241(a), (b), or (c) is subject to a civil penalty of not more than $2,500 for each violation. Each pupil record involved constitutes a separate violation.
  (b) An agency that violates § 15-241(d) or (e) is subject to a civil penalty of not more than $1,000 for each violation, and not more than $10,000 in the aggregate for violations arising from a single course of conduct.
  (c) Where a violation is willful, or where the person has been found in violation within the preceding 36 months, the maximum penalty under subsections (a) and (b) is doubled.
  (d) In determining the amount of a penalty, the department shall consider the number of individuals affected, the identifier categories involved, the duration of the violation, the adequacy of the safeguards in place at the time, the promptness of remediation, and the agency's resources.

§ 15-244. Administrative sanctions.
  (a) The department may suspend an agency's authorization to receive data from a state longitudinal data system until compliance is demonstrated.
  (b) The department may refer a licensed employee to the professional standards board for action against the employee's certificate.
  (c) A vendor found in violation may be declared ineligible to contract with any agency in the state for a period not exceeding 3 years.

§ 15-245. Cure period. Except in the case of a willful violation or a violation causing demonstrable harm, the department shall issue a written notice of deficiency and afford the agency 30 days to cure before assessing a penalty under § 15-243(b).

§ 15-246. Remedies not exclusive. The remedies provided in this subchapter are in addition to, and not in substitution for, any other remedy available at law or in equity. Nothing in this subchapter creates a private right of action.
