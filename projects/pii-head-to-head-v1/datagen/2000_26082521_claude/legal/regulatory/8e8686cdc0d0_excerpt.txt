§ 42.101  SHORT TITLE.
This chapter may be cited as the Personal Information Protection Act.

§ 42.102  DEFINITIONS.
As used in this chapter, unless the context otherwise requires:
 (a) "Agency" means a municipal utility, utility district, or joint utility
     authority organized under Title 6, and any contractor or billing agent
     acting for such an entity.
 (b) "Personal identifier" means any of the following, whether appearing alone
     or in combination with the name of a natural person:
     (1) social security number or taxpayer identification number;
     (2) driver's license number or state identification card number;
     (3) financial account number, credit card number, or debit card number,
         together with any required security code, access code, or password;
     (4) date of birth;
     (5) mother's maiden name or other authentication challenge response;
     (6) medical or disability status recorded for purposes of a life-support
         or medical-necessity service designation;
     (7) service address, where the customer of record is a participant in an
         address confidentiality program administered under Title 3, ch. 19.
 (c) "Record" means any writing, drawing, map, photograph, card, tape, or other
     documentary material, regardless of physical form or characteristics,
     including microfilm, microfiche, magnetic tape, optical disk, and data
     maintained in an electronic information system.
 (d) "Custodian" means the officer designated under § 42.104.
 (e) "Disclosure" means the release, transfer, provision of access to, or
     divulging in any manner of a record or any portion thereof outside the
     Agency.
 (f) "Redaction" means the permanent obliteration of a personal identifier from
     a copy of a record such that it cannot be reconstructed by ordinary means.

§ 42.103  APPLICATION.
This chapter applies to every record created, received, or maintained by an
Agency in connection with an application for service, a deposit, a billing or
collection account, a payment arrangement, a service order, a meter reading, or
an assistance or hardship program.

§ 42.104  DESIGNATION OF CUSTODIAN.
Each Agency shall designate in writing one officer as Records Custodian and one
officer as alternate. The designation shall be filed with the clerk of the
governing body within thirty (30) days of appointment and shall be a public
record. The Custodian shall maintain the register required by § 42.105(d).

§ 42.105  DUTIES OF HOLDERS OF RECORDS.
Every officer, employee, and agent in possession of a record subject to this
chapter shall:
 (a) limit access to those persons whose duties require it;
 (b) store records containing personal identifiers in a locked cabinet, locked
     room, or access-controlled information system when not in active use;
 (c) transmit personal identifiers outside the Agency only by sealed envelope,
     bonded courier, or encrypted electronic transmission, and not by facsimile
     except to a machine in a secured area attended at the time of transmission;
 (d) enter in the disclosure register the date, the requesting entity, the
     statutory basis, and the categories of identifiers released;
 (e) report any known or suspected unauthorized acquisition of a record to the
     Custodian not later than the close of the next business day.

§ 42.108  PENALTIES.
 (a) Knowing disclosure in violation of § 42.107 is a misdemeanor punishable by
     a fine of not more than five thousand dollars ($5,000), imprisonment for
     not more than one (1) year, or both.
 (b) Negligent disclosure is subject to a civil penalty of not more than one
     thousand dollars ($1,000) per record, assessed by the Commission.
 (c) An officer or employee found to have violated § 42.105 shall be subject to
     discipline up to and including dismissal, in addition to any penalty
     imposed under this section.
