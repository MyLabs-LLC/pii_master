TITLE 21 — EDUCATIONAL RECORDS AND INFORMATION PRIVACY
CHAPTER 4 — SAFEGUARDING OF PERSONALLY IDENTIFIABLE INFORMATION HELD BY LOCAL EDUCATION AGENCIES

Section 21-4-101. Short Title and Purpose.

This Chapter shall be known and may be cited as the Student and Employee Information Protection Act. The General Assembly finds and declares that local education agencies, in the ordinary course of enrolling pupils, employing personnel, administering transportation and nutrition programs, and disbursing funds, necessarily accumulate substantial quantities of information which, if improperly disclosed, may subject individuals to identity theft, financial injury, harassment, or unwarranted invasion of personal privacy. It is therefore the intent of the General Assembly to impose upon every local education agency, and upon every officer, employee, contractor, agent, and assignee thereof, an affirmative and continuing duty to safeguard such information, and to provide remedies and penalties for the breach of that duty.

Section 21-4-102. Definitions.

For purposes of this Chapter, the following terms shall have the meanings ascribed to them in this Section, unless the context clearly requires otherwise.

(a) "Local education agency" means any public school district, charter school authorizer, board of cooperative educational services, intermediate unit, or other public body politic charged by law with the operation of one or more public elementary or secondary schools, and includes any successor entity thereto.

(b) "Record" means any information, regardless of the physical form or characteristics of the medium upon which it is fixed, which is created, collected, received, maintained, or disseminated by a local education agency, whether recorded upon paper, microform, magnetic tape, optical disc, magnetic or solid-state disk, or any other medium now existing or hereafter devised, and whether stored upon premises controlled by the agency or upon premises controlled by a vendor, cloud service provider, or other third party acting at the agency's direction.

(c) "Personally identifiable information" means any element of a record, or any combination of elements of a record, from which the identity of a natural person may reasonably be ascertained, whether directly or by reference to other information reasonably available to the anticipated recipient. Without limiting the generality of the foregoing, the following categories of data elements shall be deemed personally identifiable information whenever they appear in a record in association with any individual: full legal name; former or maiden name; parent, guardian, or emergency contact name; residential or mailing address; telephone number of any type, including landline, mobile, and facsimile; electronic mail address; date and place of birth; mother's maiden name; social security number or other taxpayer identification number; state-issued identification or operator's license number; passport number; alien registration number; student identification number, state assigned student identifier, or local enrollment number; employee identification number, payroll number, or retirement system membership number; financial account number, debit or credit card number, routing number, or personal identification number or access code associated with any financial account; health insurance policy or subscriber number; medical record number or immunization registry identifier; biometric identifier, including fingerprint, retinal or iris image, voice print, facial geometry, and deoxyribonucleic acid profile; photographic image in which the face is discernible; internet protocol address, device serial number, or persistent online identifier; and any other number, characteristic, or code assigned to an individual which is unique to that individual.

(d) "Directory information" means those elements enumerated in Section 21-4-108 which a local education agency has, by policy duly adopted and annually published, designated as subject to disclosure without prior written consent, provided that no element listed in subsection (c) as a unique identifying number shall be so designated.

(e) "Records Custodian" means the officer designated in writing by the governing board of a local education agency pursuant to Section 21-4-104 as the person responsible for the administration of this Chapter, and includes any deputy or alternate so designated during the absence or incapacity of the principal designee.

(f) "Breach" means the unauthorized acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of personally identifiable information which compromises the security, confidentiality, or integrity thereof, excepting only (1) the good-faith acquisition or access by an employee or agent of the agency for a purpose authorized by this Chapter, where the information is not further used or disclosed in a manner prohibited hereunder, and (2) the acquisition or access to information rendered unusable, unreadable, or indecipherable through encryption or destruction in accordance with standards promulgated by the Department.

Section 21-4-103. General Obligations of Holders of Records.

Every local education agency holding a record containing personally identifiable information shall maintain reasonable administrative, technical, and physical safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the information, the size and complexity of the agency, and the nature and scope of its activities. Such safeguards shall include, at a minimum, the restriction of access to those employees, contractors, and agents having a legitimate educational or administrative interest in the information; the maintenance of a written access log identifying, for each inspection of a record by a person other than a parent, eligible student, or authorized school official, the identity of the requesting party, the date of the request, the specific interest asserted, and the disposition thereof; the periodic review, and not less frequently than annually, of user accounts and access privileges with prompt revocation upon separation or reassignment; the encryption of personally identifiable information stored upon or transmitted by portable devices, removable media, or public networks; and the secure destruction of records upon expiration of the applicable retention period by shredding, pulping, incineration, degaussing, or cryptographic erasure such that reconstruction is not reasonably practicable. No agency shall transmit personally identifiable information by unencrypted electronic mail, nor shall any agency permit the storage of such information upon a personally owned computing device absent a written agreement executed by the Records Custodian.

Section 21-4-107. Penalties.

Any officer or employee of a local education agency who knowingly discloses personally identifiable information in violation of this Chapter, or who knowingly permits such disclosure by another, shall be guilty of a misdemeanor and, upon conviction, shall be punished by a fine of not more than five thousand dollars for each violation, or by imprisonment for a term not exceeding one year, or by both such fine and imprisonment, and shall in addition be subject to such disciplinary action, including dismissal, as the governing board may impose. Where the disclosure is made for pecuniary gain, or with intent to cause harm to the individual who is the subject of the record, each such disclosure shall constitute a separate offense punishable by a fine of not more than twenty-five thousand dollars, or by imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years, or by both. A local education agency which negligently fails to maintain the safeguards required by Section 21-4-103 shall be liable to the Department for a civil penalty of not more than one thousand dollars per record affected, not to exceed one hundred thousand dollars in the aggregate for any single occurrence, and the Department may in addition condition the continued receipt of state financial assistance upon the submission and satisfactory execution of a corrective action plan. Nothing in this Section shall be construed to abrogate any right of action existing at common law or under any other statute.
