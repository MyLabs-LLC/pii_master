ARTICLE 7. CONFIDENTIALITY OF HEALTH AND PERSONAL INFORMATION
Adopted pursuant to the authority of the State Health and Safety Code. Effective July 1, 2011.

§ 7.01 PURPOSE
This Article establishes minimum standards for the collection, retention, disclosure, and destruction of personal information held by the Department and by any contractor, subrecipient, or agent acting on the Department's behalf.

§ 7.02 DEFINITIONS
As used in this Article:

(a) "Department" means the county health department and each of its divisions, clinics, and program units.

(b) "Personal information" means any information that identifies or reasonably can be used to identify a natural person, including but not limited to: full name; residential or mailing address; telephone or facsimile number; electronic mail address; social security number; driver's license or state identification card number; medical record number; health plan beneficiary number; account number; certificate or license number; date of birth; date of admission or discharge; biometric identifier; full-face photographic image; and any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code.

(c) "Direct identifier" means an element listed in subsection (b) that identifies a person without reference to other information.

(d) "Indirect identifier" means a data element that does not identify a person standing alone but that may identify a person when combined with other available information, including census tract, employer, occupation, and dates of service.

(e) "Holder of record" means any officer, employee, volunteer, student trainee, or contractor of the Department having custody or control of personal information, regardless of medium.

(f) "Records Custodian" means the Department employee designated in writing by the Health Officer to administer this Article.

(g) "Disclosure" means the release, transfer, provision of access to, or divulging in any manner of personal information outside the Department.

§ 7.03 OBLIGATIONS OF HOLDERS OF RECORD
(a) A holder of record shall collect only the minimum personal information necessary to accomplish the authorized purpose of the program.

(b) A holder of record shall not access personal information except as required to perform assigned duties. Curiosity, personal interest, and unofficial inquiry are not authorized purposes.

(c) Personal information in paper form shall be maintained in a locked cabinet or locked room when not under the direct observation of a holder of record.

(d) Personal information in electronic form shall be stored on Department-managed systems. Storage on removable media, personally owned equipment, or non-Department network services is prohibited absent written authorization of the Records Custodian.

(e) Personal information transmitted outside the Department network shall be encrypted.

(f) Each holder of record shall complete confidentiality training upon appointment and annually thereafter, and shall execute the Confidentiality Acknowledgment form maintained by the Records Custodian.

§ 7.04 PERMITTED DISCLOSURE
Disclosure is permitted only: (1) with the written authorization of the individual or the individual's legal representative; (2) as required by state or federal law; (3) pursuant to lawful process; (4) to another public health authority for a purpose authorized by law; or (5) in de-identified form from which all direct identifiers and all indirect identifiers enumerated in § 7.02(d) have been removed.

§ 7.05 BREACH REPORTING
A holder of record who knows or suspects that personal information has been accessed, acquired, or disclosed without authorization shall report the matter to the Records Custodian not later than the close of the next business day. The Records Custodian shall notify the Health Officer and County Counsel within five business days and shall maintain a Breach Log recording: date of discovery; date of occurrence; program unit; number of individuals affected; identifier categories involved; medium; and corrective action taken.

§ 7.06 RETENTION AND DESTRUCTION
Records shall be retained for the period prescribed in the Department Records Retention Schedule and destroyed thereafter by shredding, pulping, incineration, or, for electronic media, by overwriting or physical destruction. A Certificate of Destruction shall be completed and retained for seven years.

§ 7.07 PENALTIES
(a) Violation of this Article by an employee constitutes grounds for discipline up to and including dismissal.

(b) Violation by a contractor constitutes a material breach of contract and grounds for immediate termination.

(c) Nothing in this Article limits civil or criminal liability imposed by state or federal law. Unauthorized disclosure of medical information may subject the individual to administrative fine as provided by statute.
