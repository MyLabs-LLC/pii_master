CHAPTER 41 — SAFEGUARDING OF PERSONAL INFORMATION HELD BY FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS

Section 41-1. Application.
(a) This chapter applies to every state-chartered bank, savings institution, credit union, consumer loan company, and licensed money transmitter that owns, licenses, stores, or maintains records containing personal information about a resident of this State, whether the records are held in paper, electronic, magnetic, optical, or any other medium.
(b) Compliance with this chapter does not relieve a covered institution of any obligation arising under federal law, the rules of its chartering authority, or its share insurance agreement.

Section 41-2. Definitions.
As used in this chapter, the following terms have the meanings given:
(1) "Personal information" means a natural person's first name or first initial and last name in combination with any one or more of the following data elements, when either the name or the data element is not encrypted or redacted:
(i) Social Security number;
(ii) driver's license number or state identification card number;
(iii) financial account number, credit card number, or debit card number, with or without any required security code, access code, personal identification number, or password that would permit access to the account;
(iv) date of birth;
(v) mother's maiden name or other shared secret used to authenticate the person.
(2) "Personal information" does not include information lawfully obtained from federal, state, or local government records made available to the general public.
(3) "Record" means any material on which written, drawn, spoken, visual, or electromagnetic information is recorded or preserved, regardless of physical form.
(4) "Holder of records" means a covered institution that owns or licenses personal information, and includes a service provider acting on the institution's behalf under contract.
(5) "Records Custodian" means the officer or employee designated under Section 41-3(b) to administer the institution's information security program.
(6) "Breach of security" means the unauthorized acquisition or unauthorized use of unencrypted personal information, or of encrypted personal information together with the confidential process or key, that creates a substantial risk of identity theft or fraud.
(7) "Disposal" means the discarding, abandonment, sale, donation, transfer, or relinquishment of control over a record.

Section 41-3. Duties of holders of records.
(a) A holder of records shall develop, implement, and maintain a comprehensive written information security program containing administrative, technical, and physical safeguards appropriate to the size and complexity of the institution, the nature and scope of its activities, and the sensitivity of the personal information held.
(b) The board of directors shall designate a Records Custodian and shall record the designation in the minutes. The Records Custodian shall report to the board, or to a committee of the board, not less than annually on the status of the program.
(c) The program shall provide for:
(1) identification of internal and external risks to the security, confidentiality, and integrity of records containing personal information;
(2) restriction of physical and logical access to such records to those employees whose duties require access;
(3) verification that service providers are capable of maintaining appropriate safeguards, and contractual requirements that they do so;
(4) secure disposal of records under Section 41-5; and
(5) documented review of the program not less than annually, and whenever a material change in business practices could reasonably affect the security of personal information.
(d) The Records Custodian shall maintain an inventory identifying, for each system of records, the record series name, the categories of data elements contained, the retention period, and the office of primary responsibility.

Section 41-4. Effective date.
This chapter takes effect January 1, 2005, as to records created on or after that date, and July 1, 2005, as to all other records.
