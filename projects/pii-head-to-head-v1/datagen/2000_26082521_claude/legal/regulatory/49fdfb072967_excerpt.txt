CHAPTER 1387, ARTICLE VI — ENFORCEMENT, PENALTIES, AND REMEDIES
Excerpt retained in the Billing Office compliance binder, Tab 6

§ 6.01. Enforcement Authority.
  [ ] The Attorney General is authorized to investigate any alleged violation of this Chapter upon complaint or upon their own initiative, and to issue civil investigative demands compelling the production of records, provided that such demand shall specify the record series sought by reference to the inventory fields prescribed in § 4.01.
  [ ] The Department is authorized to conduct compliance examinations of any holder of records not more frequently than once in any twelve (12) month period, absent evidence of a continuing violation.

§ 6.02. Civil Penalties.
  [ ] A holder of records that violates any provision of Article III or Article IV shall be liable for a civil penalty not to exceed five thousand dollars ($5,000) for each such violation.
  [ ] A holder of records that fails to give notice as required by Rule 12-4.007 shall be liable for a civil penalty not to exceed one thousand dollars ($1,000) per affected individual, provided that the aggregate penalty arising from a single breach shall not exceed five hundred thousand dollars ($500,000).
  [ ] Each day on which a violation continues after written notice from the Department shall constitute a separate and distinct violation for purposes of this Section.
  [ ] In assessing any penalty hereunder, the Department shall consider: the nature and extent of the violation; the identifier categories implicated; the degree of culpability; the history of prior violations; the good-faith efforts of the holder of records to comply; and the effect of the penalty upon the continued operation of the holder of records.

§ 6.03. Willful and Knowing Violations.
  [ ] Any person who knowingly and willfully obtains, discloses, or uses personal information maintained by a holder of records for a purpose not authorized by this Chapter, or who knowingly conceals a breach of the security of a system, commits a misdemeanor of the first degree, punishable as provided by the general penal statutes of this State.
  [ ] Where the conduct described in the preceding paragraph is undertaken for commercial advantage, personal gain, or malicious harm, the offense shall be reclassified as a felony of the third degree.

§ 6.04. Affirmative Defense.
  [ ] It shall be an affirmative defense to any action brought under § 6.02 that the personal information at issue was encrypted, redacted, or otherwise rendered unusable, unreadable, or indecipherable at the time of the unauthorized acquisition, and that no encryption key, access credential, or decryption method was acquired in the same incident.

§ 6.05. Private Right of Action; Limitation.
  [ ] Nothing in this Chapter shall be construed to create a private right of action, save that an individual aggrieved by a willful violation may bring an action for actual damages sustained, together with reasonable attorney's fees and costs, in any court of competent jurisdiction.
  [ ] No action under this Section shall be commenced more than three (3) years after the date on which the aggrieved individual received notice of the breach, or three (3) years after the date on which such notice was required to have been given, whichever occurs first.
