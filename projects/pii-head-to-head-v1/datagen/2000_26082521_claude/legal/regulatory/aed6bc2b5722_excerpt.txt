RULE 27-9. RETENTION, DISPOSAL, AND ENFORCEMENT

(a) Retention. A holder of records shall retain each record series containing personal information for the period prescribed by the retention schedule filed with the Department, and no longer, except where a longer period is required by statute, by order of a court or administrative tribunal, or by a litigation hold issued by counsel. The retention schedule shall identify for each series: series title, record medium, business owner, categories of personal information contained, retention trigger, retention period, and approved disposition method. The schedule shall be reviewed by the Records Custodian not less than every three years.

(b) Disposal. When records containing personal information are no longer required to be retained, the holder of records shall dispose of them by shredding, pulping, or incineration of paper records, and by degaussing, cryptographic erasure, or physical destruction of electronic media, in each case so that the information cannot practicably be read or reconstructed. Disposal performed by a vendor shall be evidenced by a certificate of destruction retained in the disposal log, which log shall record: series title, volume disposed, method, date of disposal, vendor name, and the position title of the person authorizing disposal.

(c) Recordkeeping and Examination. The information security program, risk assessments, access control register, disposal log, incident register, and training attendance records shall be maintained for a period of not less than five years and shall be produced to the Commissioner upon request in connection with any examination conducted under this title. Failure to produce such records within the time specified in the examination call shall constitute a separate violation of this Rule.

(d) Penalties. Any holder of records that violates any provision of this Rule shall be subject to an administrative penalty of not more than five thousand dollars for each violation, and not more than fifty thousand dollars for a course of conduct constituting a knowing and willful violation. Each day during which a violation continues after written notice from the Commissioner shall constitute a separate violation for purposes of this subsection. In determining the amount of any penalty, the Commissioner shall consider the nature, circumstances, extent, and gravity of the violation; the degree of culpability; any history of prior violations; the effect of the penalty on the ability of the holder of records to continue to do business; the extent to which the holder of records self-reported the violation; and such other matters as justice may require.

(e) Additional Remedies. The imposition of a penalty under subsection (d) shall not preclude suspension or revocation of a certificate of authority or license, the issuance of a cease and desist order, or referral to the Attorney General. Nothing in this Rule shall be construed to create a private right of action.
