ARTICLE 14 — SAFEGUARDING OF PERSONAL IDENTIFYING INFORMATION IN THE CUSTODY OF LICENSED PHARMACY ESTABLISHMENTS

Compliance Checklist — Retained Pursuant to Section 14.09(c)

- § 14.01 SHORT TITLE. This Article shall be cited as the Personal Identifying Information Custody and Disposal Rule, and shall be construed liberally to effectuate its remedial purpose, namely the prevention of unauthorized acquisition of Protected Records held by any Covered Establishment.

- § 14.02 DEFINITIONS. For all purposes of this Article, and unless the context clearly requires a contrary construction, the following terms shall have the meanings hereinafter ascribed:

  - § 14.02(a) "Personal Identifying Information" means an individual's first name or first initial and last name in combination with any one or more of the following data elements, when either the name or the data element is not encrypted or redacted: (i) Social Security Number; (ii) driver's license number or state-issued identification card number; (iii) financial account number, credit card number, or debit card number, with or without any required security code, access code, personal identification number, or password permitting access to such account; (iv) medical history, mental or physical condition, or treatment or diagnosis rendered by a health care professional; (v) health insurance policy number, subscriber identification number, or any unique identifier used by a health insurer.

  - § 14.02(b) "Protected Record" means any writing, drawing, chart, photograph, magnetic tape, optical disk, electronic data file, or other documentary material, regardless of physical form or characteristic, containing Personal Identifying Information, whether originated by the Covered Establishment or received from a third party.

  - § 14.02(c) "Covered Establishment" means any pharmacy, pharmacy chain, mail-order pharmacy, or central fill facility licensed by the Board, together with each of its agents, employees, contractors, and subcontractors having access to Protected Records.

  - § 14.02(d) "Records Custodian" means the individual designated in writing by the Covered Establishment pursuant to § 14.04(a) and charged with the duties therein enumerated.

  - § 14.02(e) "Breach of the Security of the System" means the unauthorized acquisition of computerized or physical data that compromises the security, confidentiality, or integrity of Personal Identifying Information, provided that good faith acquisition by an employee or agent for a legitimate business purpose shall not constitute a Breach so long as the information is not subject to further unauthorized disclosure.

- § 14.03 SCOPE OF APPLICATION. The obligations imposed herein shall attach to every Covered Establishment irrespective of the number of Protected Records maintained, the medium in which such records are maintained, or whether such records are maintained within or without the jurisdiction, provided that the individual to whom the Personal Identifying Information pertains was a resident at the time of collection.

- § 14.04 OBLIGATIONS OF HOLDERS OF PROTECTED RECORDS. Each Covered Establishment shall:

  - § 14.04(a) Designate in writing a Records Custodian, and maintain on file the designation instrument bearing the position title, department, date of designation, and countersignature of the responsible officer;

  - § 14.04(b) Develop, implement, maintain, and periodically revise a comprehensive written information security program containing administrative, technical, and physical safeguards reasonably appropriate to the size and scope of the Covered Establishment, the resources available to it, the amount of stored data, and the need for security and confidentiality of both consumer and employee information;

  - § 14.04(c) Restrict access to Protected Records to those persons whose duties reasonably require such access, and maintain an access authorization log recording the following fields: Employee Position Title, Department, Authorization Level, Date Granted, Date Revoked, Approving Officer Title;

  - § 14.04(d) Cause all Protected Records to be stored in locked facilities, storage areas, or containers when not in active use, and to be transported only in sealed and traceable conveyances;

  - § 14.04(e) Effect the destruction of Protected Records no longer required to be retained by shredding, pulping, incineration, or, in the case of electronic media, by degaussing, overwriting, or physical destruction rendering the data unreadable and unreconstructable by any means; and

  - § 14.04(f) Retain for a period of not less than three (3) years a Certificate of Destruction bearing the following fields: Record Series, Volume Destroyed, Method of Destruction, Date of Destruction, Witnessing Employee Title, Vendor Name, Vendor License Number.

- § 14.05 VENDOR AND THIRD-PARTY OBLIGATIONS. No Covered Establishment shall disclose Protected Records to any third-party service provider absent a written contract whereby such provider covenants to implement and maintain safeguards no less protective than those required by § 14.04, and whereby such provider consents to audit by the Covered Establishment upon reasonable notice.

- § 14.06 NOTIFICATION UPON BREACH. Upon discovery or notification of a Breach of the Security of the System, the Records Custodian shall, in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay, but in no event later than forty-five (45) days following discovery, cause notice to be given to each affected individual and to the Board, such notice to state the date of the Breach, the categories of Personal Identifying Information involved, the remedial steps undertaken, and a toll-free contact through which further information may be obtained.

- § 14.07 PENALTIES. Any Covered Establishment that violates any provision of this Article shall be subject to a civil penalty of not more than five thousand dollars ($5,000) for each such violation, each day of continuing violation constituting a separate and distinct offense, together with such suspension, probation, or revocation of licensure as the Board in its discretion may impose; provided that willful or knowing violation shall subject the offender to a civil penalty of not more than twenty-five thousand dollars ($25,000) per violation and to referral for such criminal prosecution as may be available at law.
