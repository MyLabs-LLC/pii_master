INFORMATION CLASSIFICATION AND RECORDS HANDLING STANDARD
Issued by the Compliance and Risk Committee
Effective: First quarter, 2016
Applies to: all branch offices, the central closing unit, and the records warehouse

PURPOSE AND SCOPE

- This Standard governs the classification, access, retention, and destruction of personal information collected in the course of title search, examination, closing, escrow disbursement, and post-closing recording activities.
- It applies to information held in any medium: the escrow production system, the imaging repository, working paper files, correspondence, and physical closing binders.
- It does not govern corporate business records, vendor contracts, or employment records, which are addressed under separate policies.

CATEGORIES OF PERSONAL INFORMATION HELD

- Category A — Identity attributes. Full legal name, prior or maiden names, date of birth, and marital status as reported on the settlement statement.
- Category B — Government identifiers. Social security number or taxpayer identification number, driver's licence number, passport number, and alien registration number where a non-resident withholding determination is required.
- Category C — Contact attributes. Residential street address, mailing address where different, telephone numbers, and electronic mail address.
- Category D — Financial account attributes. Depository institution name, routing number, account number, and wire instruction reference used for disbursement or receipt of settlement funds.
- Category E — Transaction attributes. Property address, parcel identifier, sale price, loan amount, lender name, loan number, and payoff figures.
- Category F — Source-of-funds documentation. Bank statements, gift letters, and evidence of earnest money deposit.
- Category G — Instrument images. Executed deeds, deeds of trust, affidavits, powers of attorney, and notarial certificates bearing signatures.
- Category H — Adverse-matter attributes. Judgment and lien search results, bankruptcy filings, divorce decrees, probate filings, and child support enforcement records.
- Category I — Communication records. Correspondence, call notes, and recorded lines associated with a file number.

ACCESS AUTHORISATION BY ROLE

- Escrow Officer of record: Categories A through I for assigned files only.
- Escrow Assistant: Categories A, C, E, F, G, I for assigned files only.
- Title Examiner: Categories A, C, E, H. No access to Category B or D.
- Funding and Disbursement Clerk: Categories A, B, D, E. Access to Category D limited to the funding window.
- Post-Closing and Recording Clerk: Categories A, C, E, G.
- Claims and Underwriting Counsel: all categories, on presentation of a claim or underwriting reference number.
- Information Technology Administrator: system-level access only. Content access prohibited except under a logged support request countersigned by the Records Custodian.
- External auditor or underwriter examiner: read-only, supervised, time-limited access under an executed confidentiality undertaking.
- Access is granted on the least-privilege principle and reviewed by the Records Custodian semi-annually. Terminated personnel are de-provisioned the same business day.

RETENTION PERIODS

- Category B: purged from the production system 90 days after final disbursement; retained thereafter only within the encrypted archive where required for information return reporting.
- Category D: purged 45 days after the wire has been confirmed and reconciled.
- Categories A, C, E, G: retained seven years from the date of policy issuance or file cancellation, whichever is later.
- Category F: retained five years from disbursement.
- Category H: retained three years from file closing unless a claim is pending.
- Category I: retained three years from the last entry.
- Files subject to a litigation hold, an open claim, or a regulatory examination are exempt from all destruction schedules until the hold is released in writing by Counsel.

DISPOSAL PROCEDURE

- The Records Custodian generates a monthly destruction eligibility list from the retention register.
- Counsel reviews the list against the active hold register and endorses it before any action is taken.
- Physical media are cross-cut shredded on site or destroyed by a bonded vendor that issues a certificate of destruction.
- Electronic records are removed from primary storage, backups, and the imaging repository by cryptographic erasure or overwriting; deletion is confirmed by the Information Technology Administrator in writing.
- Certificates and confirmations are filed in the destruction log and retained for ten years.
- The destruction log records the file number range, categories destroyed, method, date, vendor, and the endorsing officers.
