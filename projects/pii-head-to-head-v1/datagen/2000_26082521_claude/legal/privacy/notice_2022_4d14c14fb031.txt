SECTION B — CLAUSES

1. Purpose
1.1 This Standard defines the categories of personal information held by the Company, identifies the role groups authorised to access each category, prescribes retention periods, and sets out the procedure for authorised disposal.
1.2 It is binding on all business units, contracted administrators and service providers acting on the Company's behalf.

2. Scope
2.1 This Standard applies to personal information in all media, including production databases, the document management repository, imaged correspondence, voice recordings, electronic mail stores, backup media, and physical files held in the Records Centre or the off-site vault.
2.2 Aggregated or de-identified information from which no living individual can reasonably be identified falls outside this Standard.

3. Categories of Personal Information Held
3.1 Category A — Identification Data. Data elements: full legal name; former or alternate names; date of birth; gender marker; marital status; government-issued identification number; taxpayer identification number.
3.2 Category B — Contact Data. Data elements: residential address; mailing address; primary and secondary telephone numbers; electronic mail address; preferred contact method and language.
3.3 Category C — Policy Administration Data. Data elements: policy number; product code; coverage limits and deductibles; effective and expiry dates; beneficiary designations; producer of record.
3.4 Category D — Financial Data. Data elements: bank account and routing details; payment card details; premium billing history; loss payee instructions.
3.5 Category E — Underwriting Data. Data elements: risk questionnaire responses; prior loss history; credit-based insurance score; motor vehicle record extracts; property inspection findings.
3.6 Category F — Claims Data. Data elements: claim reference; loss description and loss location; adjuster case notes; medical reports and treatment records; independent examination findings; subrogation and litigation files.
3.7 Category G — Workforce Data. Data elements: application materials; compensation and benefits records; performance documentation; licensing and continuing-education records; occupational injury reports.
3.8 Category H — Digital Data. Data elements: portal credentials and authentication logs; device and network identifiers; telematics and usage-based rating data; call recordings and interaction transcripts.
3.9 The elements at 3.5 (credit-based insurance score) and 3.6 (medical reports and treatment records) are designated Sensitive and attract the additional controls at clause 4.4.

4. Authorised Access
4.1 Access is provisioned to role groups, not to individuals, through the enterprise identity management system on the principle of least privilege.
4.2 Approved role groups and their permitted categories:
 (a) Underwriting Associates — A, B, C, E
 (b) Claims Examiners and Field Adjusters — A, B, C, F
 (c) Premium Accounting Analysts — A, C, D
 (d) Human Resources Business Partners — A, B, G
 (e) Information Technology Administrators — H, and technical access to all categories solely under change control
 (f) Internal Audit and Compliance Officers — all categories, read-only
4.3 Appointed producers and third-party administrators receive access only to records of the policies they service, and only to Categories A, B and C.
4.4 Sensitive elements require standing approval of the Privacy Steward for the receiving unit. Access events are logged and reviewed monthly by the Records Custodian.
4.5 Role membership is recertified quarterly by the responsible business owner.

5. Retention Periods
5.1 Categories A, B and C — seven years from termination of the policy.
5.2 Category D — seven years from the date of the transaction.
5.3 Category E — five years from declination or termination.
5.4 Category F — ten years from final closure of the claim; where the claimant was a minor at the date of loss, three years beyond the age of majority.
5.5 Category G — seven years from separation.
5.6 Category H — twenty-four months for authentication and device logs; thirty-six months for call recordings and transcripts.
5.7 Where a record contains elements of more than one category, the longest applicable period governs.

6. Disposal Procedure
6.1 The Records Custodian generates a disposal eligibility report each quarter.
6.2 The responsible business owner reviews the report within fifteen business days and confirms or objects in writing.
6.3 The Office of the General Counsel confirms that no litigation hold, regulatory examination or market conduct inquiry attaches to the listed series.
6.4 Approved physical records are destroyed by cross-cut shredding to a particle size not exceeding 4 mm by 40 mm by the bonded vendor, against a certificate of destruction.
6.5 Electronic records are purged or the media destroyed in accordance with NIST SP 800-88; where records reside on shared storage, cryptographic erasure of the record key is the approved method.
6.6 Backup media are not purged individually and expire under the thirteen-month rotation schedule.

7. Disposal Register
7.1 The Records Custodian maintains a permanent register with the following columns: Register Entry Number; Category Code; Record Series; Media Type; Volume; Retention Trigger Date; Disposal Authorisation Reference; Method of Destruction; Vendor Certificate Number; Approving Role; Date of Destruction.

8. Legal Hold
8.1 A record under legal hold is suspended from disposal until the hold is released in writing by the Office of the General Counsel, notwithstanding the expiry of its retention period.
