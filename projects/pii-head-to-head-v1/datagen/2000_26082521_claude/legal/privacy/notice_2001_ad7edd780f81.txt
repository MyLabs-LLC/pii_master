PERSONAL INFORMATION HANDLING — DESK STANDARD FOR BRANCH PERSONNEL

Issued 2016. Supersedes the branch data handling memorandum of the prior year.

1. What we hold

We hold, for every file we open, the following classes of personal information about buyers, sellers, borrowers, and their authorised representatives:

  a. Names, former names, and marital status.
  b. Dates of birth.
  c. Social security or taxpayer identification numbers.
  d. Government-issued photographic identification details, including the issuing authority, the identification number, and the expiry date.
  e. Home and mailing addresses, telephone numbers, and electronic mail addresses.
  f. Bank names, routing numbers, and account numbers used to send or receive settlement funds.
  g. Property addresses, parcel identifiers, loan numbers, sale prices, and payoff amounts.
  h. Search results disclosing judgments, liens, bankruptcies, dissolutions of marriage, and probate matters.
  i. Signed and notarised instruments.
  j. Correspondence, call notes, and portal messages.

2. Who may see it

Access follows the file. An officer or assistant may open only those files to which they are assigned. Nobody opens a file out of curiosity, on behalf of a friend, or to assist a party who is not our customer.

Item (c) and item (f) are seen only by the Funding and Disbursement Clerk and the Escrow Officer of record. Title Examiners do not need them and do not have them. Post-Closing does not need them and does not have them.

Counsel and the Records Custodian may open any file, and every such opening is logged.

3. How long we keep it

Items (c) and (f) leave the production system shortly after disbursement — 90 days for (c), 45 days for (f) — and survive only in the encrypted archive where the law requires us to keep them for reporting.

Items (a), (b), (e), (g), and (i) are kept seven years from policy issuance or cancellation.

Item (h) is kept three years. Item (j) is kept three years from the last entry.

A litigation hold or an open claim stops the clock entirely. Do not destroy anything on a held file, in any medium, for any reason, without written release from Counsel.

4. How we destroy it

Paper goes into the locked destruction consoles, never into the general waste or recycling stream. The vendor collects, shreds, and returns a certificate. The certificate goes to the Records Custodian.

Electronic records are destroyed centrally. Branch personnel do not delete records from the production system. If you believe a record should be destroyed, refer it to the Records Custodian.

Workstations, scanners, and multifunction devices are wiped by the Information Technology Administrator before disposal or return to the leasing company. Do not surrender a device without a wipe certificate.

5. If something goes wrong

Report a lost device, a misdirected wire instruction, a misdelivered closing package, or an electronic mail sent to the wrong recipient to the Records Custodian and to Counsel immediately and in writing. Do not attempt to recall, correct, or investigate on your own.
