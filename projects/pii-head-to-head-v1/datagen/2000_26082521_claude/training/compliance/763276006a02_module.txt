PATIENT FINANCIAL SERVICES
Supplemental Instruction Sheet PFS-114-S
Scenario Examination With Instructor Key
For use in the classroom session only; the key is not distributed to participants

I. Instructions to the Instructor. Read each scenario aloud in full before inviting response. Do not permit participants to substitute the name of an actual account or an actual caller; the exercises are constructed so that no identifying element is required to reach the correct answer, and the introduction of such elements is itself a lapse to be corrected on the spot. Allow four minutes per scenario. Record the number of participants reaching the correct clause citation on the tally sheet returned to the Education Coordinator.

II. Scenario One. A representative receives a telephone inquiry from a person who states a relationship to the patient, supplies the correct spelling of the surname, and cannot supply any second element on file. The person becomes insistent and states that a collection notice has already been received at the household. Expected answer: verification has failed under clause 4.1, and the surname alone is a single element and is frequently obtainable from the notice itself. No balance, no service date, and no confirmation that an account exists may be given. The representative offers to mail a statement to the guarantor address of record and escalates to the Patient Accounts Manager.

III. Scenario Two. During claim follow-up, a representative learns that a coworker in another department is a patient with an unpaid balance, and mentions this at lunch to a third employee who also knows the coworker. Expected answer: two separate violations. The access itself is permissible only if the account appeared on the assigned work list; the disclosure at lunch is impermissible in all circumstances. The matter proceeds under clause 6.2 rather than under progressive discipline, because the disclosure served no business purpose.

IV. Scenario Three. A staff member preparing a payer appeal photocopies the itemized statement, retains the copy in a desk drawer after the appeal is resolved, and later discards it in the general waste basket beneath the desk. Expected answer: the retention is unnecessary duplication and the disposal is a reportable disclosure, since the general waste stream is not secure. The columns exposed include Patient Name, Account Number, Date of Service, Revenue Code, and Total Charges. Report the same business day; the facility assesses whether notification obligations arise.

V. Scenario Four. A supervisor asks a representative to pull a list of self-pay accounts above a stated balance threshold and to forward the file electronically to an outside collection vendor with whom the facility has no executed agreement. Expected answer: the request is refused and escalated. Transmission to a vendor absent an executed business associate agreement is prohibited irrespective of the seniority of the person directing it, and the representative who complies bears individual exposure under clause 6.4.

VI. Closing Point. Emphasize in summary that in every scenario the safe action was available, took less than a minute, and involved either declining to speak or asking a supervisor.
