DIVISION OF PUBLIC HEALTH
CONFIDENTIALITY IN-SERVICE — MODULE FOUR
CLOSING NARRATIVE AND ACKNOWLEDGMENT

The Department wishes to close this in-service with a statement of the principle underlying everything that has preceded it, because staff who understand the principle will decide correctly in situations this training has not anticipated, and staff who have memorized only the rules will not.

The principle is that the persons who come to this Department have, in very many instances, no alternative. They come because the clinic is free, or because it is the only clinic, or because a statute requires them to come, or because a communicable disease investigation has brought us to their door. They do not choose us and they frequently cannot decline to answer. Every field we collect — Client Name, Date of Birth, Social Security Number, Residence Address, Household Income, Household Composition, Employer, Presenting Complaint, Test Result, Diagnosis Code, Contact Investigation Number — is obtained under that condition. The confidentiality obligation is the entire consideration given in exchange. It is what makes the disclosure lawful at the point of collection, and a breach is therefore not a mishap occurring after the fact but a retroactive falsification of the basis upon which the information was obtained.

There is a practical dimension as well. Public health depends upon voluntary reporting and voluntary participation in contact investigation. Persons who believe that a Test Result or a Contact Investigation Number will circulate through a small community do not present for testing and do not name contacts. A single improper disclosure, once known in a neighborhood, suppresses participation across that neighborhood for years. This is not speculative. It is the documented experience of this and other jurisdictions.

Staff are asked to hold in mind the following, which the Department regards as the operative test in any situation of doubt: would the person whose information this is, knowing what I am about to do, regard it as consistent with what was promised at intake? Where the answer is no, or is uncertain, the disclosure does not occur and the matter is referred to the Records Custodian. There is no circumstance in which referring a question to this Office constitutes an error. There are many circumstances in which failing to refer one does.
