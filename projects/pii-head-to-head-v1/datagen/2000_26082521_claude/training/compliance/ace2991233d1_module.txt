DIVISION OF PUBLIC HEALTH — OFFICE OF THE RECORDS CUSTODIAN

CONFIDENTIALITY IN-SERVICE, MODULE TWO
HANDOUT FOR PARTICIPANTS — RETAIN FOR YOUR UNIT'S POLICY BINDER, TAB 7

1. SCOPE AND APPLICATION

1.1 This handout supplements and does not supersede Administrative Directive 94-11, as amended, and is to be read in conjunction therewith. Where any statement herein is found to conflict with the Directive, with state statute, or with the conditions of any grant award, the latter shall govern in all respects.

1.2 The provisions hereof apply to all persons having access to Department records, without regard to classification, bargaining unit, or duty station, and specifically including but not limited to registered nurses, public health nurses, sanitarians, environmental health specialists, clerical staff, eligibility technicians, health educators, outreach workers, interpreters, custodial staff assigned to clinic space, and any person performing services under contract.

2. THE RESTRICTED FIELDS

2.1 The following field labels, wherever they appear — whether upon a paper form, a screen of the client information system, a microfiche, a card file, a telephone message slip, an appointment book, a sign-in sheet, or a handwritten note — denote information which is restricted and which shall not be disclosed except as provided in Section 4:

    Client Name                    Date of Birth
    Social Security Number         Medicaid Identification Number
    Medical Record Number          Case Number
    Residence Address              Mailing Address
    Home Telephone                 Work Telephone
    Emergency Contact              Head of Household
    Diagnosis Code                 Presenting Complaint
    Test Ordered                   Test Result
    Treatment Plan                 Medication and Dosage
    Referral Source                Referral Destination
    Contact Investigation Number   Immunization History
    Household Income               Household Composition
    Employer                       Insurance Carrier
    Policy or Group Number         Disposition

2.2 The presence of a field label upon a document is not itself restricted. The blank form is a public document. The completed form is not. This distinction is important and is frequently misunderstood: staff may freely discuss the existence and structure of our forms, our reporting requirements, and our program eligibility criteria; staff may not discuss what any particular form says.

2.3 Fields appearing upon the communicable disease report, the tuberculosis worksheet, the sexually transmitted disease morbidity report, the HIV counseling and testing record, and the substance abuse or mental health assessment are subject to additional statutory restriction and shall not be disclosed even to other Department staff absent a demonstrated need to know arising from a current work assignment.

3. PROHIBITED PRACTICES — NON-EXHAUSTIVE

3.1 Accessing a record for any purpose other than the performance of assigned duties, including accessing one's own record, the record of a family member, the record of a coworker, or the record of any person known to the employee socially.

3.2 Removing any record, or any copy or extract thereof, from Department premises absent written authorization.

3.3 Discussing restricted fields in a corridor, elevator, waiting room, break room, parking area, restaurant, or Department vehicle where the discussion may be overheard.

3.4 Transmitting restricted fields by facsimile to a receiving number which has not been verified by telephone in advance, or leaving restricted fields upon an answering machine or with a third party.

3.5 Recording restricted fields in a personal calendar, address book, or notebook.

3.6 Disposing of any document bearing a completed restricted field otherwise than by deposit in a locked shredding receptacle.

4. AUTHORIZED DISCLOSURE

4.1 Disclosure is authorized only upon (a) written authorization executed by the client or the client's legal representative upon Form DPH-40, current revision, bearing an expiration date not more than one year from execution; (b) lawful subpoena or court order, which shall be transmitted immediately and without response to County Counsel through the Records Custodian; (c) mandatory reporting obligation imposed by statute; or (d) internal need to know arising from a current assignment.

5. CONSEQUENCES

5.1 Employment consequences range from documented counseling through dismissal. Willful disclosure, disclosure for gain, disclosure of Section 2.3 information, and disclosure by a supervisor are each grounds for dismissal upon a first offense.

5.2 Criminal referral, licensure referral, and civil liability in the personal capacity of the employee are each possible and are not mutually exclusive.

5.3 Failure to report a known improper disclosure within one business day is an independent offense.

6. SELF-ASSESSMENT

Answer each in writing. Retain your answers; they will not be collected.

(a) A caller states an urgent medical need and requests a Test Result for a person the caller says is unconscious. No release is on file. What do you do, and whom do you notify?

(b) You observe a coworker viewing a screen displaying Diagnosis Code and Residence Address for a case not assigned to that coworker's unit. What do you do, and within what period?

(c) A local newspaper reporter asks the general nature of a cluster under investigation, requesting no names. Which fields, if any, may be discussed, and by whom?

(d) You discover a printout bearing Client Name and Social Security Number in a public copier tray. Describe the sequence of actions required.

(e) Your own Medical Record Number is in the system because you were seen in a Department clinic. May you view your own record? State the rule and the reason for it.
