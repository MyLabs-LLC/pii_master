DESK REFERENCE: RESTRICTED INFORMATION
Regional Operations — Claims, Underwriting, and Member Services
Issued by the Privacy Officer. Post at workstation.

1. CATEGORIES YOU MAY NOT DISCLOSE WITHOUT AUTHORIZATION
   a. Identity elements — Social Security Number, Taxpayer Identification Number, Date of Birth, Driver License Number, Residence Address, Home Telephone.
   b. Coverage elements — Policy Number, Certificate Number, Group Number, Member Identification Number, Effective Date, Termination Reason.
   c. Health elements — Diagnosis Code, Procedure Code, Attending Provider, Date of Service, Attending Physician Statement, Lab Panel Result, Behavioral Health Indicator, HIV/AIDS Status, Substance Abuse Treatment Indicator.
   d. Financial elements — Bank Routing Number, Account Number, Credit Card Number, Premium Draft Authorization, Reserve Amount, Settlement Offer.
   e. Investigative elements — SIU Referral Reason, Surveillance Log, Recorded Statement Transcript, Fraud Bureau Referral Status.

2. HEIGHTENED-SENSITIVITY SUBSET
   a. Items 1.c marked Behavioral Health Indicator, HIV/AIDS Status, Substance Abuse Treatment Indicator, and Genetic Test Result carry additional state restrictions.
   b. These require specific written authorization referencing the category by name. A general authorization on file is not sufficient.
   c. Do not restate these elements in correspondence, voicemail, or claim notes visible to servicing units without a business need.

3. DAILY CONTROLS
   a. Lock terminal when leaving the desk. Do not share sign-on credentials, including with a supervisor.
   b. Clear the printer and fax immediately. Unclaimed output goes to the Records Custodian, not the recycle bin.
   c. Verify two authentication elements before any telephone disclosure.
   d. Route all media disposal through the locked shred bin or the Records Custodian's log.
   e. Report suspected disclosure to the Privacy Officer the same business day. Do not investigate independently.

4. CONSEQUENCES
   a. Disciplinary — written warning through termination, per the Associate Handbook.
   b. Regulatory — examination findings, corrective action plans, fines, and license action against individual licensees.
   c. Legal — civil suit by affected persons; criminal referral for disclosure for gain.
   d. Contractual — loss of group accounts where breach notification is triggered.

5. SCENARIO EXERCISE
   a. A Customer Service Representative is asked by a caller to confirm whether a specific address is on file for a policy. The caller supplies the address. Is confirming the address a disclosure?
   b. An Underwriter forwards an attending physician statement to a reinsurer using ordinary electronic mail. Which control was omitted?
   c. A Claims Examiner leaves a voicemail at a workplace number describing a pending surgery authorization. Which two categories were exposed?
   d. A Temporary Clerk is instructed to photocopy a batch of applications and take them home to complete indexing. Which controls prohibit this?
   e. A Team Lead discovers a report containing member identification numbers was faxed to a wrong destination. What is the required first step, and what is the deadline?
