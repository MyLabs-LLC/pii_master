PART ONE — WHAT IS RESTRICTED

The Department holds four tiers of restricted information. Know which tier you touch.

Tier 1 — Protected Health Information. Any clinic or program record that pairs a health condition, service, or payment with an identifier. The identifier fields include: Patient Name, Medical Record Number, Date of Birth, Street Address, Telephone Number, Social Security Number, Health Plan Beneficiary Number, and Admission Date. A record does not stop being Tier 1 because the name field is blank; the remaining fields can re-identify a person.

Tier 2 — Vital Records. Birth and death registrations. Fields include Registrant Name, Date of Event, Place of Event, Parent Name(s), Informant Name, and Certificate Number. Access is limited to the Vital Records Custodian and designated deputy registrars.

Tier 3 — Communicable Disease Reports. Case reports, contact lists, and outbreak line lists. Fields include Case Name, Reporting Provider, Diagnosis, Onset Date, and Contact Name. Disclosure outside the Epidemiology Unit requires the Health Officer's written authorization.

Tier 4 — Personnel and Business. Employee files, applicant materials, payroll registers, sealed bid packages, and legal correspondence. Fields include Employee Name, Employee Number, Home Address, Compensation Rate, and Bank Routing and Account Number.

PART TWO — CONSEQUENCES

Mishandling restricted information carries consequences at three levels.

Departmental. Progressive discipline under the County Personnel Rules: written counseling, suspension without pay, demotion, or dismissal. Access privileges are suspended during any investigation.

Civil and administrative. Federal privacy rules provide monetary penalties assessed against the County per violation, with higher tiers for willful neglect. The County may seek indemnification from the responsible employee. State vital records law provides separate penalties for unauthorized issuance or alteration of a certificate.

Criminal. Knowingly obtaining or disclosing protected information under false pretenses, or for personal gain or malicious harm, is a federal offense carrying fines and imprisonment.

Reporting is not optional. Any suspected loss, unauthorized access, or misdirected disclosure must be reported to the Privacy Officer within one business day. Self-reporting is considered in mitigation; concealment is not.

PART THREE — SCENARIO QUIZ

Answer on the reverse. Supervisors collect completed sheets.

1. A staff member takes a paper outbreak line list home to finish data entry over the weekend. The list is left in a personal vehicle overnight. What tier of information is involved, and what two actions are required Monday morning?

2. A caller states they are the spouse of a person who received services at the immunization clinic and asks for the person's vaccination dates. No written authorization is on file. What is the correct response?

3. A clerk emails a spreadsheet of case follow-up assignments to a state program contact. The spreadsheet's hidden columns still contain full names and dates of birth. Has a disclosure occurred? What is the reporting deadline?

4. A supervisor asks a subordinate to look up whether a former coworker's relative is enrolled in a county program. There is no program-related reason for the inquiry. Is the lookup permitted? What should the subordinate do?

5. A death certificate application is submitted by a funeral home representative who is listed on the establishment's registry but who is requesting a certificate for an unrelated decedent. Which tier applies, and who decides whether to issue?

6. A workstation in a shared area is left logged in while staff attends a meeting down the hall. The screen displays a case management queue. Identify the failure and the control that prevents it.

Answer key is reviewed aloud at the close of the session. Questions may be directed to the Privacy Officer or the Vital Records Custodian.
