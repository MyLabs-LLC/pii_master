Good morning. Every employee, volunteer, student intern, contractor, and temporary clerical assistant assigned to any program area of this Department is required, as a condition of continued assignment, to complete this in-service and to execute the acknowledgment appended hereto. The obligations described herein arise under state statute, under the administrative regulations promulgated by the State Department of Health, under the ordinances of this County, and under the terms of the several federal grant agreements pursuant to which a substantial portion of our program funding is received. These obligations are not discretionary, they are not waived by custom or by the informal practices of any particular clinic or field office, and they do not terminate upon separation from employment. An employee who resigns, retires, or is separated for any reason whatsoever remains bound by the duty of confidentiality with respect to all information acquired in the course of the assignment, in perpetuity.

Let us begin with the categories. The first category, and the one most frequently mishandled, comprises the identifying particulars appearing on any client-facing form. These are the fields denominated Client Name, Date of Birth, Social Security Number, Residence Address, Mailing Address, Home Telephone, Work Telephone, Medicaid Identification Number, Medical Record Number, and Head of Household. It is the position of this Department, and of the State, that these fields are restricted in their entirety and that no portion of them may be disclosed, transcribed, repeated aloud in a public area, transmitted by facsimile to an unverified receiving number, or entered into any log, ledger, message pad, or personal notebook not itself maintained as a Department record. The second category comprises the clinical and programmatic fields, being Diagnosis Code, Presenting Complaint, Test Ordered, Test Result, Treatment Plan, Referral Source, Referral Destination, Contact Investigation Number, Immunization History, and Disposition. The third category comprises those fields subject to heightened statutory protection above and beyond the general rule, being any field appearing on the communicable disease reporting form, the tuberculosis contact investigation worksheet, the sexually transmitted disease morbidity report, the human immunodeficiency virus counseling and testing record, the substance abuse assessment, and the mental health intake. Disclosure of information within this third category is, in certain enumerated circumstances, a misdemeanor punishable by fine, by imprisonment, or by both, wholly apart from any employment consequence.

The fourth category, frequently overlooked, comprises administrative and financial fields which are restricted although not clinical in character: Employee Payroll Number, Bank Routing Number, Bank Account Number, Vendor Taxpayer Identification Number, and the contents of any personnel action form. That a record is not a medical record does not render it a public record.

As to consequences. The Department's progressive discipline policy is applicable, but the Department expressly reserves the right to depart from progressive discipline and to impose termination for a first offense where the disclosure was willful, where it was made for personal gain or curiosity, or where it involved a third-category field. Documented counseling, written reprimand, suspension without pay, demotion, and dismissal are all available dispositions. In addition, the Department is obligated to report certain disclosures to the State Department of Health, to the licensing board of any licensed employee, and, where the facts warrant, to the District Attorney. An employee who becomes aware of an improper disclosure, whether the employee's own or another's, shall report the same to the immediate supervisor and to the Records Custodian before the close of the next business day. Failure to report a known disclosure is itself a disciplinary offense.

We turn now to the scenarios. Consider each and state the correct action aloud.

Scenario One. A clerk in the immunization clinic receives a telephone call from a person who states that she is the mother of a child seen at the clinic last week and who requests the child's Immunization History. The caller is unable to provide the Medical Record Number and becomes agitated. What is the correct action?

Scenario Two. A field nurse conducting a contact investigation is stopped in the parking lot by a neighbor who says he has heard there is "something going around" on his street and asks whether he was one of the people listed on the investigation worksheet. What is the correct action?

Scenario Three. A supervisor asks a records clerk to pull the file of a Department employee who has been out on extended leave, stating that she wishes to know the Diagnosis Code so that she may plan the unit's coverage schedule. What is the correct action?

Scenario Four. A facsimile is received in the administrative office bearing the letterhead of another county's health department and requesting transmission of a Test Result. The cover sheet lists a Work Telephone for verification. What is the correct action?

Scenario Five. Two employees riding together in a Department vehicle discuss a case by Presenting Complaint and Residence Address only, using no name. What is the correct action?

The answers are, in each instance, that no restricted field is to be released absent verified identity, documented authorization on the approved release form, and a lawful purpose recognized by statute or regulation; and that in every case of doubt the matter is to be referred to the Records Custodian before, and not after, any disclosure occurs.
