INFORMATION SECURITY AND CUSTOMER PRIVACY — STAFF TRAINING HANDOUT
Escrow, Title, and Closing Operations
Revised for the 2004 training cycle

WHY THIS TRAINING EXISTS

Our business runs on documents that other people would very much like to have. In the course of a single residential closing file, this office collects a buyer's loan application data, a seller's payoff authorization, wire instructions from two or three institutions, a credit report summary, and identification photocopies from every party at the table. Under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and the privacy rules adopted by our state department of insurance, all of that is nonpublic personal information, and we are the custodian of it. The obligation does not end when the file closes. It follows the paper into storage, follows the images onto the file server, and follows any copy an employee makes for convenience.

WHAT COUNTS AS RESTRICTED INFORMATION

Restricted information is any field on a closing document that could be used, alone or in combination with other fields, to impersonate a party or reach their money. In practice this means the Social Security Number or Taxpayer Identification Number captured on the settlement statement and the 1099-S reporting worksheet; the loan number, lender account number, and payoff account number; the borrower's date of birth and driver's license number as recorded on the identity verification form; the buyer's and seller's home telephone number, mailing address, and forwarding address; the routing number and account number on every wire instruction sheet and disbursement authorization; the earnest money check images retained in the file; and the credit and income figures forwarded to us by the lender. Escrow trust account numbers belong on this list as well, because a disclosed trust account number is an invitation to attempt an unauthorized transfer.

Certain items are public record and are not restricted: the recorded deed, the recorded mortgage or deed of trust, the legal description, the parcel identification number, and the chain of title as it appears in the county index. Do not confuse a public record with a public file. A recorded instrument may be quoted freely; the closing file that contains it may not.

HOW RESTRICTED INFORMATION IS MISHANDLED

Almost every incident this company has investigated began as an ordinary act of helpfulness. A closer reads a wire instruction aloud over the telephone to a caller who claims to be the buyer's agent. A processor faxes a full settlement statement to a real estate office because someone asked for the numbers. A file is left face up on a desk overnight in a room that the cleaning service enters. A courier packet is handed to a party in the lobby without confirming who is standing there. Copies are taken home to be worked on over the weekend. A departing employee forwards a spreadsheet of pending files to a personal address. None of these people set out to cause harm, and all of them created a reportable event.

The technical rules follow from that. Restricted information leaves this office only through a channel the file itself authorizes: a signed disclosure authorization in the file, a written lender instruction, a statutory or court demand, or delivery to the party who supplied the information in the first place. Verbal identification over the telephone is not sufficient to release wire instructions or account numbers to anyone, including a caller who correctly recites the property address and the closing date. Wire instructions received by facsimile or electronic mail are verified by outbound telephone call to a number obtained from our own records, never a number printed on the incoming document. Paper containing restricted fields goes to the locked shred consoles, not to the recycling bin or the wastebasket. Files in active use are returned to the fireproof cabinets at the end of each business day. Passwords to the title production system and the trust accounting system are not shared with a coworker under any circumstances, including a coworker covering your desk during vacation; the branch manager arranges temporary access instead.

CONSEQUENCES

For the customer, the consequence of a disclosure is identity theft, a diverted closing wire, or a fraudulent loan opened in their name — losses that are difficult to unwind and that often surface months later. For the company, the consequences are a privacy complaint to the state insurance regulator, a federal examination under the safeguards rule, indemnity claims from our underwriter, loss of the lender approvals on which our order volume depends, and the cost of notifying every affected party. For the employee, mishandling restricted information is a disciplinary matter up to and including immediate termination, separate from any civil or criminal liability the individual may incur. Failing to report an incident you know about is treated as seriously as causing one. Report to your branch manager and to the Privacy Officer the same day; there is no penalty in this company for a good-faith report that turns out to be nothing.

SCENARIO QUIZ

Read each scenario and write your answer on the reverse. Discuss your answers with your supervisor before filing this handout.

1. A caller states that they are the listing agent on a file closing tomorrow, correctly names the property address and the scheduled closing time, and asks you to read back the seller's payoff account number so the lender can be contacted directly. What do you do, and what would you do differently if the caller instead asked for the closing time?

2. A processor discovers that a settlement statement for a file that closed last week was faxed to a real estate brokerage office at a number that does not match anything in the file. The transmission report shows it went through. Who is notified, and when?

3. A closer is asked by a coworker who is covering the desk during a vacation to share the title production system password so pending orders can be updated. The coworker is a long-tenured employee in the same branch. Is this permissible, and what is the correct alternative?

4. During a walkthrough of the office after hours, a manager finds three closing files open on a desk in a room the cleaning contractor services nightly. No one can say whether anything was removed. What is the exposure, and what should have happened at the end of the business day?

5. An escrow assistant receives an electronic mail message that appears to come from the lender on a pending file, containing revised wire instructions and asking that disbursement be sent to a different receiving bank. The message thread quotes earlier correspondence correctly. What verification step is required before any change is made, and which telephone number is used to perform it?

6. A staff member is leaving the company in two weeks and asks to keep a copy of a spreadsheet listing open orders, so their contacts can be transferred to the new employer. The spreadsheet contains borrower names, addresses, telephone numbers, and loan numbers. What is the answer, and what should the branch manager do about access before the last day?
