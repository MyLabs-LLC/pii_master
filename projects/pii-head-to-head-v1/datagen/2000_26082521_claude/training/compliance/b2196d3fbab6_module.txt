Supervisor Facilitation Notes — Restricted Information Module
For use by unit supervisors delivering the module at staff meeting. Not for distribution to attendees.

    I.  Before the meeting
        A.  Print the Quick Reference Card, one per attendee.
        B.  Print the sign-in sheet. Attendance is tracked by employee ID, not by name, on the roster submitted to Compliance.
        C.  Review your unit's most recent access-audit summary. If your unit had findings, address them generally without identifying the employee involved.

    II. Setting the tone
        A.  Open by acknowledging that the volume of restricted information staff handle has grown substantially during the current response, and that many staff are working from home or from field sites for the first time.
        B.  Avoid framing the session as punitive. The message is: the rules exist because people who fear disclosure will not come forward for testing, and an investigation nobody trusts does not work.
        C.  Do not use real incidents from the department as examples, even without names. Staff frequently recognize the underlying event.

    III. Points that generate the most questions
        A.  "Minimum necessary" is judged by what you accessed, not by what you used. Opening a full chart to find one immunization date is a finding.
        B.  Household members overhearing telework calls: the standard is a reasonable safeguard, not perfection. A closed door and a headset satisfy it. A shared kitchen table does not.
        C.  Personal cell phones used for contact tracing calls: the call itself is permitted under the current field protocol; storing numbers in the phone's contacts is not. Direct staff to the department-issued handset request form.
        D.  Requests from other government agencies feel authoritative and are frequently improper. The answer is always the same — route to the Records Custodian, do not release, do not confirm the record exists.
        E.  Staff often ask whether they may access their own record. They may not, through the clinical system. They submit a records request like any other member of the public.

    IV. Running the scenario quiz
        A.  Read the scenario, then wait. Silence for ten seconds usually produces a volunteer.
        B.  If the group answers correctly and quickly, ask the follow-up: "What would have made this easier to do right?" The answers are useful and should be forwarded to Compliance.
        C.  Expect pushback on the birthday card scenario. Staff perceive it as harmless. Redirect to the principle: the clerk cannot know the requester's purpose, and every improper lookup begins with a plausible reason.
        D.  Close the quiz by noting that in every scenario, the correct action was available and took less time than the incorrect one.

    V.  After the meeting
        A.  Submit the roster to the Training Coordinator within five business days.
        B.  Forward unanswered questions to the Privacy Officer rather than improvising an answer. Written guidance will be issued to all units.
        C.  Confirm that any staff absent from the session complete the module before the end of the quarter.
