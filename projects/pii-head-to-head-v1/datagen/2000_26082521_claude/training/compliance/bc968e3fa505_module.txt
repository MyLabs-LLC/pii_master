MEMBER INFORMATION SECURITY — ANNUAL STAFF TRAINING HANDOUT
Compliance Department · Revision 3 · November 1998
Retain at your workstation until superseded.

PURPOSE
- Every employee of this credit union handles member information as a condition of employment.
- This handout states which categories are restricted, how they are to be handled, and what happens when they are mishandled.
- Completion of the scenario quiz on the reverse is required before the close of the training quarter.

RESTRICTED CATEGORIES — TREAT ALL OF THE FOLLOWING AS NON-PUBLIC
- Identifying fields: Member Name, Member Number, Social Security Number, Date of Birth, Mother's Maiden Name, Driver's License Number.
- Contact fields: Street Address, Mailing Address, Home Telephone, Work Telephone, Employer Name.
- Account fields: Share Account Number, Share Draft Number, Certificate Number, Loan Account Number, ATM/Debit Card Number, PIN Mailer Contents, Available Balance, Current Balance, Ledger Balance.
- Credit fields: Credit Bureau Score, Credit Bureau Report, Debt-to-Income Ratio, Loan Application Status, Denial Reason Code, Charge-Off Amount, Delinquency Bucket.
- Transaction fields: Deposit Amount, Withdrawal Amount, Payee Name, Check Serial Number, Wire Beneficiary, Direct Deposit Source.
- Regulatory fields: Currency Transaction Report contents, Suspicious Activity Report contents, Subpoena or Levy contents, IRS Backup Withholding Status.
- Employment fields concerning coworkers: Payroll Amount, Employee Account Activity, Disciplinary Record.

HANDLING CHECKLIST — DO THESE
- Verify identity before disclosing anything on an account, including whether the account exists.
- Clear your CRT screen or lock your terminal whenever you step away from the workstation.
- Place all member documents face down when a non-employee is at the counter.
- Route all outbound member paper to the shred bin, not the wastebasket, including cash-in tickets, teller proof tape, and adding machine tape.
- Use a cover sheet on every outbound fax and confirm the receiving number before sending.
- Store microfiche, printouts, and diskettes in the locked file room; sign the Records Access Log on entry and exit.
- Refer all requests from attorneys, law enforcement, collection agencies, and news media to the Compliance Officer without comment.
- Refer all member disputes over information accuracy to the Records Custodian.

HANDLING CHECKLIST — DO NOT DO THESE
- Do not look up an account you have no business reason to view, including your own household, coworkers, or public figures.
- Do not confirm or deny that a person is a member, even to a spouse, parent, adult child, or employer.
- Do not discuss account matters in the lobby, break room, elevator, parking lot, or on a cellular telephone.
- Do not remove member records from the building for any reason, including work performed at home.
- Do not share a teller number, network sign-on, or password, and do not write either where it can be seen.
- Do not tell any person that a Suspicious Activity Report has been considered, prepared, or filed.

CONSEQUENCES OF MISHANDLING
- First substantiated instance of unauthorized access: written warning, loss of inquiry rights, mandatory re-training.
- Repeated or willful unauthorized access: termination of employment.
- Disclosure to an outside party for payment, or disclosure that results in loss to a member: termination, referral to the bonding company, and referral to law enforcement.
- Disclosure of Suspicious Activity Report information: federal civil penalty and criminal referral, independent of credit union action.
- All instances are reported to the Supervisory Committee and are recorded in the Employee Compliance File.

SCENARIO QUIZ — CIRCLE THE CORRECT ACTION
1. A caller states they are the employer of a member and asks whether a payroll deposit posted this morning. You:
   a. Confirm the deposit amount. b. Confirm only that the account exists. c. Decline and refer the caller to the member.
2. A person at the counter asks you to look up the balance of an account held by their adult child, and produces the account number. You:
   a. Provide the balance since the account number was given. b. Provide the balance only if the amount is small. c. Decline; the account number is not proof of authority.
3. You notice a coworker's account on your terminal because their surname matches your search. You:
   a. Read the transaction history. b. Exit the record immediately and note nothing. c. Mention it to another teller.
4. A member asks why their loan application was denied and requests a copy of the credit bureau report. You:
   a. Read the score aloud. b. Photocopy the report at the counter. c. Refer the member to the Loan Officer for the adverse action notice.
5. A branch printer produces a member statement that belongs to another branch. You:
   a. Leave it in the tray. b. Place it in the shred bin. c. Log it and give it to the Branch Manager for secure return.
6. A local reporter asks whether the credit union holds an account for a person named in a news story. You:
   a. Say no if the person is not a member. b. Say nothing and refer the call to the Compliance Officer. c. Take a message and check.
