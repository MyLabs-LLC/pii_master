Presenter: Records Custodian or designee

SLIDE 1 — TITLE
------------------------------------------------------------
On screen: Course title, department seal, revision number.

Say: "This is Module 2. Module 1 covered why the Department collects

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 2 — THE THREE QUESTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
On screen:
    1. Do I need this to do my job right now?
    2. Am I sending it somewhere approved?
    3. Would I be comfortable explaining this lookup to a supervisor?

Say: "If any answer is no, stop. Ask. The Department has never
disciplined anyone for asking."

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 4 — HOW EXPOSURES ACTUALLY HAPPEN
------------------------------------------------------------
On screen (bulleted):
    - Attachments sent to the wrong address book entry
    - Route sheets left in vehicles or at job sites
    - Screens visible to lobby traffic
    - Verbal disclosure over an unverified telephone call
    - Curiosity lookups
    - Spreadsheets exported "just to work from home"

Say: "Note that five of six have nothing to do with hackers. Read the
last one again. A file taken home on a thumb drive is a reportable
exposure the moment it leaves the building."

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 5 — CONSEQUENCES
------------------------------------------------------------
On screen:
    To the customer:  identity theft, financial loss, physical danger
    To the Department: statutory penalties, notification cost,
                       Council inquiry, audit finding
    To you:            reprimand, suspension, termination, referral

Say: "Curiosity lookups are the most common cause of termination in this
Department. The audit log records every account you open, the workstation,
and the timestamp. Reviews are quarterly and automated."

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 6 — SCENARIO WALKTHROUGH (discuss aloud, do not collect)
------------------------------------------------------------
Read each scenario. Solicit responses. Correct answers in brackets —
do not display.

  S1. A collections clerk is asked by a coworker in another division to
      look up whether a specific address is behind on payments. The
      coworker says it is for a code enforcement matter but has no work
      order. [Decline. Require a documented request. Business need must
      be documented, not asserted.]

  S2. A meter technician photographs a gate access code on a work order
      so it can be read on a phone in the field. [Prohibited. Device
      photography of restricted fields is a Tier 2 violation regardless
      of intent.]

  S3. A customer service representative notices an account carrying an
      Address Confidentiality Flag and mentions it to the next caller,
      who is asking about a neighboring property line. [Tier 4. Report
      immediately. Do not discuss further with anyone but the Records
      Custodian.]

  S4. During a system outage, a supervisor asks staff to export account
      lists to a personal laptop so calls can continue. [Decline. Escalate
      to the Records Custodian. An instruction from a supervisor does not
      authorize an unapproved storage location.]

  S5. A billing analyst receives a spreadsheet from a vendor that contains
      more restricted fields than the contract requires. [Report. Do not
      forward, open in shared drives, or "clean it up" independently.]

------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 7 — CLOSE
------------------------------------------------------------
On screen: Reporting extension; one business day.

Say: "One business day. That is the number to remember. Self-reporting
moves an incident from Tier 2 to Tier 1. Concealment moves it to Tier 3.
Sign the roster on your way out."
