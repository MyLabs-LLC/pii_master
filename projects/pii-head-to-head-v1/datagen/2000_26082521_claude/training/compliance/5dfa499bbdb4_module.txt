Restricted Information Handling — Escalation and Decision Reference
Compliance Unit | Companion to the Annual Privacy & Security Refresher

    1.  How to use this reference
        1.1  This document answers the question staff ask most often after training: "I have a specific request in front of me. What do I do right now?"
        1.2  Work the decision points in order. Stop at the first one that applies.
        1.3  When no decision point clearly applies, that itself is the answer: route to the Records Custodian and take no other action.

    2.  Decision point one — who is asking
        2.1  The individual who is the subject of the record, in person, with government-issued photo identification: process as a records request through the designated intake channel. Front-line staff do not release directly from the clinical system.
        2.2  A parent, guardian, or personal representative: verify the legal basis for representation before proceeding. Minor consent rules for sensitive services override parental access in specific categories; the Records Custodian determines this, not the requesting staff member.
        2.3  Another department program: permitted only where a documented internal use exists. Convenience is not a documented use.
        2.4  An external clinical provider: permitted for treatment, payment, or operations, through the established secure channel only.
        2.5  Law enforcement, an employer, a school, a landlord, an attorney, or a member of the media: route to the Records Custodian. Do not confirm or deny that a record exists. This holds even when the requester states they already know the information.
        2.6  A state or federal agency: route to the Records Custodian. Reporting obligations to state registries flow through established interfaces, not through individual staff.

    3.  Decision point two — what is being requested
        3.1  A record covered by a heightened-sensitivity category requires condition-specific written authorization. A general release form does not authorize release of HIV, STI, TB, or substance use treatment information.
        3.2  Aggregate or statistical output is not automatically releasable. Small cell sizes re-identify individuals. All aggregate release passes through the Epidemiology Unit's suppression review.
        3.3  A request that names a facility, workplace, school, or congregate setting is treated as a request for investigation records regardless of how it is phrased.
        3.4  Employee information, including verification of employment or dates of service, goes to Human Resources without exception.

    4.  Decision point three — how it would be transmitted
        4.1  Department email to a department address: permitted.
        4.2  Department email to an external address: permitted only with encryption applied and only to an address verified against the data-sharing agreement.
        4.3  Personal email, personal cloud storage, personal messaging applications, removable media: prohibited. There is no exception for urgency, and there is no exception for a failing work channel. Contact the Help Desk.
        4.4  Fax: permitted to a pre-verified number only. Confirm the number before each transmission and confirm receipt after.
        4.5  Paper carried off site: requires written approval from the unit supervisor and a signed transport log entry.

    5.  Decision point four — something has already gone wrong
        5.1  Report to the Privacy Officer the same day, and no later than the next business day.
        5.2  Preserve everything. Do not delete the message, recall the email, shred the document, or edit the record. Alteration after an incident is treated as a separate and more serious offense.
        5.3  Write down what you know while it is fresh: what was disclosed, how many individuals were affected, who received it, and what time you became aware.
        5.4  Do not contact the affected individual yourself. Notification is a formal process with legal deadlines and required content, handled by the Privacy Officer.
        5.5  Do not discuss the incident with colleagues outside the reporting chain.

    6.  Timeframes staff are expected to know
        6.1  Suspected incident reported to the Privacy Officer: same day, next business day at the latest.
        6.2  Lost or stolen device reported to the Help Desk: immediately, including outside business hours.
        6.3  Access revocation on separation or transfer: effective the last day of the assignment.
        6.4  Annual Confidentiality Acknowledgment: signed within thirty days of hire and each year thereafter.
        6.5  Retention of the signed acknowledgment: employment plus six years.

    7.  Standing reminders
        7.1  Authorization is granted by role, and it expires when the role changes. Staff reassigned out of a program lose access to that program's records on the day of reassignment.
        7.2  Systems log every view, not only every edit.
        7.3  A request that arrives with urgency attached deserves more scrutiny, not less.
        7.4  When in doubt, the department would rather absorb a delay than a breach.
