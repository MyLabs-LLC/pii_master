FORM HR-PRV-104
ANNUAL PRIVACY AND INFORMATION SECURITY TRAINING — PHARMACY STAFF HANDOUT

Effective Date: January 2011
Supersedes: FORM HR-PRV-103
Applies To: Pharmacists, Pharmacy Technicians, Pharmacy Interns, Front-End Cashiers with pharmacy register access, Store Managers
Training Duration: 45 minutes
Document Owner: Corporate Compliance — Privacy Office
Delivery Method: In-store, led by Store Manager or designee; sign-in sheet returned to District Office

SECTION 1 — PURPOSE
This handout instructs staff on which categories of information are restricted, how each category must be handled, and what happens when handling rules are not followed. Completion is mandatory within 30 days of hire and annually thereafter.

SECTION 2 — RESTRICTED INFORMATION CATEGORIES
Category A — Protected Health Information (PHI)
Fields treated as restricted: Patient Name; Date of Birth; Home Address; Telephone Number; Prescription Number; Fill Date; Drug Name and NDC; Directions for Use; Prescriber Name; Prescriber DEA Number; Diagnosis or ICD Code; Insurance Member ID; Group Number; BIN; PCN; Counseling Notes.

Category B — Payment Card Data
Fields treated as restricted: Cardholder Name; Primary Account Number; Expiration Date; Card Verification Value; Magnetic Stripe Track Data.

Category C — Employee Personal Data
Fields treated as restricted: Social Security Number; Employee ID; Bank Routing Number; Bank Account Number; Wage Rate; Garnishment Status; Leave Records.

Category D — Controlled Substance and Pseudoephedrine Records
Fields treated as restricted: Purchaser Name; Identification Type; Identification Number; Quantity Sold; Date and Time of Sale; Purchaser Signature.

Category E — Company Proprietary Information
Contract reimbursement rates, payer fee schedules, store profit and loss statements, planograms, wholesaler cost files, unreleased promotional pricing.

SECTION 3 — MINIMUM NECESSARY RULE
Access only the record required to complete the task in front of you. Looking up a record out of curiosity, for a family member, or on behalf of a person who is not the patient is a violation even if nothing is disclosed and nothing is printed.

SECTION 4 — PROHIBITED HANDLING
4.1 Discussing a prescription within hearing distance of the waiting area or drive-thru lane.
4.2 Leaving hard copies, will-call bags, or fax output unattended on the counter.
4.3 Placing restricted records in general trash rather than the locked shred bin.
4.4 Sharing register or pharmacy system credentials, including with a supervisor.
4.5 Removing restricted records from the store on paper, thumb drive, or personal camera phone.
4.6 Writing full payment card numbers on paper for any reason.

SECTION 5 — CONSEQUENCES OF MISHANDLING
5.1 Company: coaching, written warning, suspension pending investigation, or termination, depending on severity and intent.
5.2 Licensure: referral to the State Board of Pharmacy, which may impose probation, fine, suspension, or revocation.
5.3 Federal: civil monetary penalties under the HITECH-amended HIPAA penalty tiers, and criminal penalties where information was obtained or disclosed under false pretenses or for personal gain.
5.4 Notification: incidents may require written notice to affected individuals, to the Secretary of HHS, and to media where the incident exceeds 500 individuals in a state.
5.5 Payment card: card brand fines, forensic review costs, and loss of card acceptance privileges at the affected location.

SECTION 6 — REPORTING
Report any suspected loss, misdirected fax, lost hard copy, or improper access to the Store Manager and to the Compliance Hotline the same day. Reporting in good faith is protected from retaliation. Failure to report a known incident is itself a violation.
