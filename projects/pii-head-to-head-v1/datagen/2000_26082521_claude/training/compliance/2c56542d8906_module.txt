CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION — SLIDE SCRIPT
Pharmacy Operations Training Series, Module 4
Presenter: Store Manager or Pharmacist in Charge
Running time: 20 minutes
Materials: Overhead transparencies 4-1 through 4-9, acknowledgment forms

SLIDE 4-1 — TITLE
[Read aloud] "Module Four. Protecting Customer and Company Information."
Say: This module is required for every employee, pharmacy and front end, full time and part time. It takes twenty minutes. Nobody leaves early.

SLIDE 4-2 — WHAT IS RESTRICTED
[Show list. Read the field names, not examples.]
Restricted at all times:
  - Patient Name, Date of Birth, Address
  - Drug Name, Strength, Directions, Quantity
  - Prescriber Name and DEA Registration Number
  - Insurance Cardholder ID and Group Number
  - Social Security Number
  - Credit Card Number and Expiration Date
  - Refill History
Say: If it appears on a label, a profile, a claim form, or a register receipt, treat it as restricted.

SLIDE 4-3 — WHO MAY SEE IT
Say: Access follows job function, not curiosity and not seniority. A cashier does not need the profile screen. A technician does not need the wage file. A pharmacist does not need a record for a person they are not serving today.
Ask the room: What is your business reason? If you cannot state one in a sentence, you do not have one.

SLIDE 4-4 — THE THREE PLACES IT LEAKS
Say: In our experience, disclosure happens in three places.
  1. The counter. Voices carry. The line hears everything.
  2. The trash. Paper leaves the building intact.
  3. The telephone. Callers are not who they say they are.
Say: Every rule in this module addresses one of those three.

SLIDE 4-5 — TELEPHONE PROCEDURE
Say: Verify the caller against the profile before releasing anything. Do not accept a callback number the caller supplies. Do not confirm that a person is a customer here. If the caller becomes insistent, transfer to the Pharmacist in Charge and log the call.

SLIDE 4-6 — PAPER PROCEDURE
Say: Locked shred bin for every printed label, misprint, reject slip, profile, and register tape carrying a restricted field. Not the regular trash. Not the recycling. Not scratch paper. If the bin is full, the Pharmacist in Charge secures the material in the safe until pickup.

SLIDE 4-7 — SYSTEM PROCEDURE
Say: Your sign-on is yours. Every screen you open is logged with your ID and the time. Audit reports are pulled monthly by Loss Prevention and reviewed by the Compliance Officer. Sign off when you step away.

SLIDE 4-8 — CONSEQUENCES
Say plainly:
  - Deliberate disclosure: termination, same day.
  - Accessing a record without a business reason: termination.
  - Disclosure for payment: termination and referral for prosecution.
  - Careless handling: written warning, retraining, and a second occurrence is termination.
  - Licensed and registered staff: every case is reported to the State Board.
Say: There is no version of this where the employee is protected and the company is not. We go down together.

SLIDE 4-9 — SCENARIOS
[Present each. Take answers from the floor. Do not move on until someone states the correct action.]

One. A caller identifies himself as a physician's office and asks you to read back the last three fills on a profile, but the number on the caller ID does not match the prescriber file.

Two. An employee is asked by a friend outside work whether a mutual acquaintance is taking a certain medication. The employee says only "I can't answer that," but later mentions to a coworker that the question was asked.

Three. A stack of insurance reject slips is found on top of the shred bin rather than inside it, in an area customers can reach over the counter.

Four. During a system outage, staff write patient names and drug names on a notepad to enter later. The outage lasts six hours across two shifts.

Five. A technician's own family member is a customer of the store and asks the technician to check whether a refill is ready rather than waiting at the counter.

CLOSING
Say: Sign the acknowledgment before you leave the room. Give it to me, not to the next shift. If you are unsure about a situation, stop and ask the Pharmacist in Charge. There is no penalty for asking. There is a penalty for guessing.
