SECTION 1 — WHY THIS MATTERS

Every day this chain fills prescriptions for people who live and work in our neighborhoods. They give us information they would not give a stranger because they have to. That trust is the business. When it breaks, it does not break quietly — it breaks in the form of a complaint to the State Board, a lawsuit, or a news story with our sign in the background.

SECTION 2 — THE FOUR CATEGORIES

Category A — Patient Health Information. Includes: Patient Name; Date of Birth; Prescriber Name; Drug Name; Strength; Directions for Use; Quantity Dispensed; Refills Remaining; Diagnosis Code; Allergy List; Prior Fill History.

Category B — Payment and Identity Information. Includes: Insurance Carrier; Group Number; Cardholder ID; Bank Identification Number; Credit Card Number; Expiration Date; Social Security Number; Driver's License Number.

Category C — Regulated Substance Records. Includes: DEA Registration Number; Controlled Substance Schedule; Perpetual Inventory Count; Loss/Theft Report Contents; Pharmacist Initials on Dispensing Log.

Category D — Company Confidential. Includes: Contract Reimbursement Rates; Store Sales by Category; Employee Wage Rate; Disciplinary Action Records; Vendor Cost Sheets; Loss Prevention Case Files.

Categories A, B, and C are restricted at all times. Category D is restricted outside the management chain.

SECTION 3 — THE RULES

3.1 Do not discuss any Category A item in the waiting area, at the register, or in the aisles. Move the conversation to the consultation window.

3.2 Do not confirm or deny that a person is a customer of this pharmacy. Not to a relative. Not to an employer. Not to a caller who says it is an emergency. Refer all such inquiries to the Pharmacist in Charge.

3.3 Do not release records to law enforcement without the Pharmacist in Charge reviewing the subpoena or warrant. Write down the requesting agency and badge number on the Records Request Log.

3.4 Do not leave the terminal signed on when you step away. Do not share your login. Do not write your password anywhere in the pharmacy.

3.5 Place all printed labels, patient profiles, insurance rejects, and register tape containing Category B fields into the locked shred bin. Do not use the regular trash. Do not use them as scratch paper.

3.6 Do not fax a patient profile to a number you have not verified against the prescriber file.

3.7 Return will-call bags that have not been picked up in fourteen days to stock and remove the label.

SECTION 4 — CONSEQUENCES

For the employee: Written warning for a first minor violation. Termination for a deliberate disclosure, for accessing a record you have no business reason to view, or for any disclosure made for money. Termination is reported to the State Board of Pharmacy where the employee holds a license or technician registration.

For the chain: Civil damages, State Board action against the store permit, and loss of third-party contracts.

For the customer: There is no remedy. Once the information is out, it is out.
