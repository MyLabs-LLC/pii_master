INFORMATION CLASSIFICATION & HANDLING
Compliance Training Module CT-114 — Annual Refresher
Regional Operations / All Staff, All Lines
Revision 3 — Q2

I. PURPOSE
    A. Establish uniform handling standards for information held by the carrier.
    B. Satisfy annual training requirement under GLBA Safeguards Rule, HIPAA
       Security Rule, and state insurance data security statutes.
    C. Completion is mandatory. Attendance is recorded by the Compliance
       Coordinator.

II. RESTRICTED CATEGORIES
    A. Nonpublic Personal Information (NPI)
        1. Field labels in scope: Full Name; Social Security Number; Date of
           Birth; Driver's License Number; Residential Address; Telephone
           Number; Email Address.
        2. Source systems: Policy Admin, Underwriting Workbench, Claims Desktop.
    B. Protected Health Information (PHI)
        1. Field labels in scope: Member ID; Group Number; Diagnosis Code;
           Procedure Code; Treating Provider; Date of Service; Claim Narrative.
        2. Applies to all group health, disability, and workers' compensation
           files.
    C. Financial Account Information
        1. Field labels in scope: Bank Routing Number; Account Number; Payment
           Card Number; Card Expiration; Premium Draft Authorization.
    D. Underwriting and Actuarial Work Product
        1. Field labels in scope: Rate Factor; Loss Ratio; Reserve Estimate;
           Reinsurance Treaty Terms.
        2. Restricted as trade secret irrespective of personal content.
    E. Litigation and Investigation Material
        1. Field labels in scope: Matter Number; SIU Referral Code; Counsel
           Opinion; Reserve Adjustment Memo.

III. HANDLING RULES
    A. Transmission
        1. External email requires carrier-approved encryption gateway.
        2. Facsimile permitted only to numbers verified against the Approved
           Recipient Register.
        3. Personal email accounts and consumer file-sharing services are
           prohibited without exception.
    B. Storage
        1. Restricted data resides on network shares or the document repository.
        2. Local drives, removable media, and personal devices are prohibited.
        3. Paper output is secured under clean-desk standard at end of shift.
    C. Access
        1. Least privilege. Access is role-derived, not requested.
        2. Browsing files without a business reason constitutes misuse, whether
           or not information is disclosed.
    D. Disposal
        1. Paper: cross-cut shred bins only.
        2. Electronic: submit media to the Records Custodian for certified
           destruction.

IV. CONSEQUENCES OF MISHANDLING
    A. Individual
        1. Corrective action up to and including termination.
        2. Referral to licensing authority where an agent license is held.
        3. Personal civil liability under certain state statutes.
        4. Criminal exposure for knowing disclosure of PHI.
    B. Organizational
        1. Regulatory fines assessed per affected record.
        2. Mandatory consumer notification and credit monitoring expense.
        3. Market conduct examination.
        4. Reputational loss and agent attrition.
    C. Reporting Obligation
        1. Suspected incidents reported to the Privacy Office within one
           business hour of discovery.
        2. Delay in reporting is itself a violation.
        3. No retaliation for good-faith reporting.

V. SCENARIO QUIZ
    A. Instructions: select the single best response. Discuss in session.
    B. Scenario 1
        1. A claims examiner receives a call from a person stating they are the
           spouse of a claimant and requesting the status of an open bodily
           injury claim. No authorization is on file.
        2. Options: (a) provide status, relationship is sufficient; (b) decline
           and direct the caller to submit written authorization; (c) provide
           status but omit dollar amounts; (d) transfer to the adjuster.
    C. Scenario 2
        1. An underwriter working from home copies a rating spreadsheet
           containing applicant field data to a personal cloud drive to finish
           after hours.
        2. Options: (a) acceptable, work is unfinished; (b) acceptable if the
           file is deleted after; (c) violation, report to the Privacy Office;
           (d) acceptable with manager approval.
    D. Scenario 3
        1. A staff member notices a coworker viewing the claim file of a
           neighbor with no assignment to that file.
        2. Options: (a) ignore, not your file; (b) ask the coworker to stop;
           (c) report to the Privacy Office; (d) inform the neighbor.
    E. Scenario 4
        1. A misdirected fax containing member enrollment data is returned by an
           unrelated business four days after transmission.
        2. Options: (a) file the return receipt; (b) report immediately, the
           four-day lapse does not excuse notification; (c) shred and move on;
           (d) notify only if the recipient read it.
    F. Answer key held by the Compliance Coordinator. Scores are not recorded;
       attendance is.
