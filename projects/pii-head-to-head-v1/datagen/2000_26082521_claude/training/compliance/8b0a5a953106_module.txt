SCENARIO ANSWERS
1. Employer calling about a payroll deposit — Correct: decline and refer the caller to the member. An employer has no authority over the member's share account. Do not confirm existence of the account. Common wrong answer is confirming existence "because they already knew"; correct that in the room.
2. Parent asking about an adult child's account, holding the account number — Correct: decline. Possession of an account number is not authority and is not verification. Age of majority ends the parent's access unless the parent is a joint owner or holds a power of attorney on file with the Records Custodian.
3. Coworker's record appearing during a surname search — Correct: exit immediately, view nothing further, mention it to no one. Note that inquiry logs are reviewed monthly and that a look-up with no transaction behind it stands out.
4. Denied applicant asking for the credit report — Correct: refer to the Loan Officer for the adverse action notice. Staff do not read scores aloud and do not copy bureau reports at the counter. The notice states the reason and the bureau's contact procedure.
5. Misprinted statement from another branch — Correct: log it and give it to the Branch Manager for secure return. Shredding is acceptable only when the item is a duplicate; when in doubt, log and return.
6. Reporter asking whether a person holds an account — Correct: say nothing at all and refer the call to the Compliance Officer. Emphasize that "no" is itself a disclosure.

DISCUSSION POINTS IF TIME REMAINS
- Ask the room where the last near-miss occurred in their own branch. Do not let attendees identify the member or the employee involved.
- Walk the group past the fax machine and the shared printer and ask what is sitting in each tray.
- Remind attendees that access rights follow the role, and that a transfer or promotion changes them.
