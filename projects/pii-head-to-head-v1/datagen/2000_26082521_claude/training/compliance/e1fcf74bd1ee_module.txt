PRIVACY RULE REFRESHER — WORKFORCE TRAINING SCRIPT
County Health Department, Office of the Privacy Officer
Session 04-C. Duration 40 minutes. Sign-in sheet required at door.

I. SLIDE 1 — AUTHORITY
   A. 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164, Subparts A and E.
   B. State communicable disease confidentiality statute.
   C. Department Policy PR-101, as revised.
   D. Training mandated at hire and annually. Attendance recorded on the Workforce Training Roster.

II. SLIDE 2 — WHAT IS RESTRICTED
   A. Protected health information: information in any medium that identifies an individual, or could reasonably be used to identify an individual, and that relates to health status, care, or payment.
   B. Restricted identifier fields include, without limitation:
      1. Patient Name; Guarantor Name; Next of Kin
      2. Street Address; ZIP Code; County of Residence
      3. Date of Birth; Date of Admission; Date of Death
      4. Social Security Number; Medical Record Number; Client Index Number; Case Number
      5. Telephone Number; Facsimile Number; Electronic Mail Address
      6. Health Plan Beneficiary Number; Account Number; License Number
      7. Full-Face Photographic Image; Biometric Identifier
   C. Elevated-sensitivity categories. Restrictions apply above the baseline:
      1. HIV/AIDS test results and counseling records
      2. Sexually transmitted disease investigation files
      3. Tuberculosis directly observed therapy records
      4. Substance abuse treatment records, 42 CFR Part 2
      5. Mental health and developmental disability records
      6. Genetic test results

III. SLIDE 3 — MINIMUM NECESSARY
   A. Access limited to the minimum necessary for assigned duties.
   B. Role-based access assigned by the Program Manager, provisioned by Information Systems.
   C. Curiosity is not a job duty. Acquaintance with the subject is not a job duty.
   D. Exceptions: disclosure to the individual; disclosure under written authorization; disclosure required by law.

IV. SLIDE 4 — CONSEQUENCES
   A. Departmental: verbal counseling, written reprimand, suspension without pay, termination. Termination is available at first offense for willful disclosure or for access without business purpose.
   B. Civil: federal civil monetary penalties assessed per violation, subject to an annual cap per identical requirement.
   C. Criminal: knowing disclosure carries fine and imprisonment; enhanced penalties where the offense is committed under false pretenses or for commercial advantage or malicious harm.
   D. Licensure: referral to the applicable licensing board.
   E. Collateral: notification costs, mandatory retraining of the affected unit.

V. SLIDE 5 — SCENARIO QUIZ (read aloud; solicit response before advancing)
   A. Scenario 1. A clerk in the immunization program is asked by a neighbor whether a child on the same street is up to date, purportedly for a school form. Permitted?
      1. No. Third-party disclosure requires written authorization from the parent or guardian. Refer the requester to the Records Custodian.
   B. Scenario 2. Field staff print a line list containing Client Index Number and Diagnosis Code, then leave it in a county vehicle overnight. Reportable?
      1. Yes, same business day. Unsecured storage outside a controlled area is an incident whether or not the document was read.
   C. Scenario 3. A staff member recognizes a relative on the investigation queue and opens the record to confirm. Nothing is repeated to anyone. Violation?
      1. Yes. Access without business purpose is the violation. Repetition is an aggravating factor, not an element.
   D. Scenario 4. A caseworker forwards a working spreadsheet to a personal electronic mail account to finish over a weekend. Permitted?
      1. No. Transmission to non-county systems is prohibited. Request remote access through Information Systems.

VI. SLIDE 6 — REPORTING
   A. Report suspected incidents to the Privacy Officer within one business day, Incident Intake Form PR-7.
   B. Retaliation against a reporting workforce member is prohibited.
