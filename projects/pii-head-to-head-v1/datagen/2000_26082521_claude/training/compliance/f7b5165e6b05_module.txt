MODULE PC-2: RESTRICTED INFORMATION AT THE PHARMACY COUNTER

SLIDE 1 — WHY THIS MODULE EXISTS
1. Every associate who works behind the counter, at the drive-thru window, or at the register handles information that the store is legally obligated to protect.
2. State pharmacy practice acts, the state Board of Pharmacy regulations, and Company Policy 400 series all restrict disclosure. Company policy is stricter than the minimum required by statute. Follow company policy.
3. Certification is annual. Sign the roster before leaving the room.

SLIDE 2 — WHAT COUNTS AS RESTRICTED
4. Restricted information is any item that identifies a patient, or that could identify a patient when combined with another item. This includes, at minimum, the following fields as they appear on the patient profile screen and on printed hard copy: Patient Name; Date of Birth; Street Address; City/State/ZIP; Home Telephone; Work Telephone; Social Security Number; Patient ID; Rx Number; Fill Date; Drug Name and Strength; NDC; Quantity; Days Supply; Directions for Use; Refills Remaining; Prescriber Name; Prescriber DEA Number; Allergy Flags; Chronic Condition Flags; Third-Party Plan Name; Cardholder ID; Group Number; Person Code; BIN; PCN; Copay Amount; Payment Method.
5. The fact that a person is a patient of this pharmacy is itself restricted. Do not confirm or deny that a prescription exists for anyone.
6. Controlled substance records — the perpetual inventory log, DEA Form 222 files, and the Schedule II dispensing register — are restricted for a second reason: they are subject to inspection and must not be altered, copied, or removed.
7. Employee information is also restricted. This includes the Employee Number, wage rate, Social Security Number, disciplinary file, and any medical or leave documentation.

SLIDE 3 — HOW BREACHES HAPPEN
8. Speaking at volume in the waiting area.
9. Leaving hard copy, will-call bags, or third-party rejection slips face up on the counter.
10. Discussing a fill with a caller who has not been verified.
11. Discarding labels, patient leaflets, or claim printouts in the store dumpster instead of the locked shred container.
12. Looking up a profile out of curiosity. Audit trails record every profile accessed by Employee Number.

SLIDE 4 — CONSEQUENCES
13. Unauthorized access or disclosure is grounds for immediate termination under Policy 402.
14. The Board of Pharmacy may suspend or revoke an individual license or registration.
15. Civil liability may attach to the associate and to the company.
16. Report any suspected breach to the Pharmacy Manager within the same shift and to the Regional Compliance Coordinator within twenty-four hours.

SLIDE 5 — SCENARIO QUIZ (CIRCLE ONE: PERMITTED / NOT PERMITTED / VERIFY FIRST)
17. A caller states that the patient is a family member and asks whether a prescription in a particular therapeutic class was picked up.
18. A prescriber's office calls to request the last fill date for a patient under their care.
19. A shift supervisor from another store asks for a printout of a profile to settle a transfer question.
20. A cashier at the front register is asked by a customer what a waiting patient is picking up.
21. A law enforcement officer appears at the counter and requests dispensing records without a written order.
