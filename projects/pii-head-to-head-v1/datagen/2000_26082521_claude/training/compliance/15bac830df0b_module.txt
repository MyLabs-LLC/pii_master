RESTRICTED INFORMATION — QUICK REFERENCE CARD
County Health Department | Office of Compliance
Post at every workstation. Do not remove from the facility.

    1. BEFORE YOU OPEN A RECORD, ASK:
        1.1  Do I have a job duty that requires this record today?
        1.2  Am I opening the minimum necessary portion?
        1.3  Would I be comfortable explaining this access to an auditor?
        If the answer to any of these is no — stop.

    2. RESTRICTED CATEGORIES AT A GLANCE
        2.1  Protected health information — any clinical, diagnostic, or treatment record tied to an identifiable person.
        2.2  Communicable disease investigation records — case forms, contact logs, outbreak line lists.
        2.3  Heightened-sensitivity conditions — HIV, STI, TB, substance use treatment. Condition-specific written authorization required.
        2.4  Employee and applicant PII — Social Security number, home address, direct deposit routing and account number, medical accommodation file.
        2.5  Payment card data — cardholder name, account number, expiration date, security code.
        2.6  Vital records applications and supporting documents.
        2.7  Security and infrastructure information — badge logs, alarm codes, network credentials, facility security plans.
        2.8  Pre-award procurement records and open personnel investigations.

    3. NEVER, WITHOUT WRITTEN APPROVAL
        3.1  Remove paper records from the facility.
        3.2  Save restricted data to a personal device, personal cloud account, or removable drive.
        3.3  Send restricted data to a non-department email address.
        3.4  Photograph a screen or a document.
        3.5  Discuss a case in a hallway, elevator, break room, or parking lot.
        3.6  Look up a friend, relative, neighbor, coworker, or yourself.

    4. WHAT HAPPENS WHEN IT GOES WRONG
        4.1  Every access is logged. Audits are routine, not triggered by suspicion.
        4.2  Discipline ranges from retraining to termination, based on intent and harm.
        4.3  Licensing board referral where a professional license is held.
        4.4  Criminal exposure for knowing disclosure or disclosure for gain.
        4.5  Breach notification costs and grant audit findings fall on the whole department.

    5. REPORT WITHIN ONE BUSINESS DAY
        5.1  Privacy Officer — for disclosures, snooping, lost paper.
        5.2  Help Desk — for lost devices, suspected phishing, malware.
        5.3  Compliance Hotline — anonymous reporting available.
        5.4  Good-faith reporting is protected. Concealment is not.

    6. THREE-QUESTION SELF-CHECK BEFORE SENDING ANYTHING
        6.1  Is the recipient authorized?
        6.2  Is the channel encrypted?
        6.3  Is this the minimum necessary?
