SLIDE SCRIPT — DECK 04-C
"WHAT WE PROTECT, AND WHAT HAPPENS IF WE DON'T"
Presented by the Records Custodian to Billing Office staff
Six slides, approximately twelve minutes, no handout distributed during presentation

SLIDE 1 — TITLE
[On screen: department name, course code, program year]
NARRATION: Read the room and begin. Clause one: everything on your desk this morning is restricted. Clause two: that includes the pile you have not opened yet.

SLIDE 2 — THE FIVE BUCKETS
[On screen: five boxes, unlabeled until the click sequence]
NARRATION: Advance one click per bucket. Bucket one, identity: Patient Name, Medical Record Number, Account Number, Date of Birth, Social Security Number, Street Address, Home Telephone. Bucket two, clinical: Admitting Diagnosis, Diagnosis Code, Procedure Code, Attending Physician, Admission Date, Discharge Date. Bucket three, money: Total Charges, Patient Balance, Payment Method, Credit Card Type. Bucket four, coverage: Subscriber Name, Subscriber Identification Number, Group Number, Authorization Number. Bucket five, business: contract rate schedules, the charge description master, and your coworkers' personnel files. Clause three: if you can see the label, you are looking at restricted information, whether or not the field is filled in.

SLIDE 3 — MINIMUM NECESSARY
[On screen: a single work-list screen capture with all data columns emptied]
NARRATION: Clause four: you open the account the work list gives you, and no other. Clause five: your own account, your mother's account, and the account of the person whose surgery was in the newspaper are all prohibited. Clause six: the audit trail records operator, terminal, date, and time, and the Privacy Officer reads it monthly.

SLIDE 4 — WHAT GOES WRONG
[On screen: fax machine, unattended terminal, open shredder bin, car trunk]
NARRATION: Clause seven: four failures account for nearly every incident in this department — the misdialed fax, the unlocked workstation, the report left on the printer overnight, and the file taken home. Clause eight: three of those four are prevented by a habit, not a policy.

SLIDE 5 — CONSEQUENCES
[On screen: three columns, headed Employment, Civil, Criminal]
NARRATION: Clause nine: employment consequences run from documented counseling to termination, and curiosity access starts at termination. Clause ten: civil penalties run one hundred dollars per violation to twenty-five thousand dollars annually per identical requirement. Clause eleven: criminal exposure begins at fifty thousand dollars and one year and rises steeply where the disclosure was for gain, and it attaches to you personally.

SLIDE 6 — QUICK QUIZ
[On screen: three scenarios, revealed one at a time]
NARRATION: Take these verbally. Scenario one: a caller will not give a second identifier but insists the balance is urgently needed to prevent a collection referral. Scenario two: a representative on break tells a coworker in the cafeteria why an account is on hold. Scenario three: a remittance advice is found on the floor of the elevator lobby. For each, ask the room: what was disclosed, which clause governs, and who is notified today.
