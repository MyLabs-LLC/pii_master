TRAINING MODULE 7 — INFORMATION CLASSIFICATION AND CUSTODY
Bus Operations, Rail Operations, Maintenance, and Dispatch
Issued by the Office of Records and Information Governance, June 2011
Instructor: complete all four sections. Do not shorten Section 3.

SECTION 1 — WHAT COUNTS AS RESTRICTED
1.1 Personnel data held by the Authority
    - Employee Name, Employee Number, Social Security Number, Date of Birth, Home Address, Emergency Contact Name, Emergency Contact Telephone
    - Medical certification status, fitness-for-duty findings, and any test result governed by 49 CFR Part 40
1.2 Customer data held by the Authority
    - Cardholder Name, Account Number, Payment Card Number, Billing Address, Telephone Number, E-mail Address
    - Any record linking a fare card to a time, station, or vehicle
1.3 Rider eligibility data
    - Client Identification Number, Diagnosis, Assessment Notes, Physician Name, Pickup Address, Drop-off Address
1.4 Operational security data
    - Camera placement, alarm codes, key inventories, revenue pickup schedules, radio encryption material
1.5 Third-party data received under agreement
    - Motor vehicle record extracts, criminal history results, and school district rider rosters

SECTION 2 — CUSTODY RULES
2.1 Restricted material stays in the assigned work area. It does not go home, into a personal vehicle, or into a personal bag.
2.2 Screens facing a public counter are angled away or fitted with a privacy filter.
2.3 Paper containing any field listed in Section 1 goes to the locked shred console, never to the recycling bin.
2.4 Removable media must be Authority-issued and encrypted. Personal drives are prohibited on Authority equipment.
2.5 Shared login credentials are prohibited without exception. Each query must be attributable to one person.
2.6 Photographs of screens, incident reports, or dispatch displays are prohibited on personal devices.

SECTION 3 — CONSEQUENCES
3.1 Administrative: written warning, suspension without pay, or termination, per the severity matrix in Personnel Rule 12.
3.2 Contractual: removal from Authority property and written notice to the contracting firm.
3.3 Legal: referral to the General Counsel; potential personal liability under the Driver's Privacy Protection Act and health information statutes.
3.4 Financial: breach notification, call center staffing, and credit monitoring charged to the originating department.
3.5 Regulatory: a Sensitive Security Information violation is reportable to the federal administration and may suspend the Authority's access to threat products.

SECTION 4 — SCENARIO EXERCISE
4.1 A dispatcher receives a telephone request from a caller identifying as a police officer, asking for the home address of an operator. No written request is offered. What is the correct action?
4.2 A maintenance technician photographs a defect on a fare gate. The photograph also captures a screen showing a cardholder name and account number. What must happen to the photograph?
4.3 A paratransit trip manifest is left on the seat of an out-of-service vehicle overnight and is found the next morning. Who is notified, and by when?
4.4 An employee asks a Payroll clerk to confirm whether a coworker's wages are being garnished. What is the correct response?
4.5 A vendor requests the revenue collection schedule to plan a maintenance visit. What is released?

ANSWER KEY
4.1 Take the caller's agency, name, and callback number; route to Transit Police. Release nothing by telephone.
4.2 Delete it, take a replacement image with no screen in frame, and report the capture to the Supervisor.
4.3 Secure the manifest, notify the Supervisor immediately and the Records Custodian by close of the next business day.
4.4 Decline. Garnishment status is released only to the employee of record and to authorized Payroll staff.
4.5 Nothing. Provide a coordination window through Facilities. Collection schedules are security sensitive.
