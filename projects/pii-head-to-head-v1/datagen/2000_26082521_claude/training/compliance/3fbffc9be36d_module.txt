INFORMATION CLASSIFICATION AND MISHANDLING — DESK TRAINING NOTES
Prepared for: Payroll Operations and Onboarding Support
Cycle: FY2021 Q2 refresher
Format: Self-paced. Estimated completion 20 minutes.

    I. Basis of classification
        A. Data is classified by field label, not by source, format, or medium.
        B. A field retains its classification after export, print, screenshot, or transcription into a message.
        C. Aggregation rule: two or more R5 field labels held together with any R1 field label are handled at the R1 control level.

    II. Restricted inventory
        A. Tier 1 — Immediate identity or financial exposure
            1. Social Security Number
            2. Individual Taxpayer Identification Number
            3. Deposit Account Number and Bank Routing Number
            4. Pay Card Number
            5. Driver's License Number
        B. Tier 2 — Statutorily protected attributes
            1. Drug Screen Result
            2. Accommodation Request Type and Physical Capacity Restriction
            3. Leave Reason Code
            4. Genetic or family medical history entries appearing in any intake field
        C. Tier 3 — Adjudication work product
            1. Consumer Report Reference
            2. Criminal Record Disposition
            3. Adverse Action Stage and Adjudication Outcome
        D. Tier 4 — Contact and demographic
            1. Full Legal Name, Date of Birth, Home Address
            2. Personal Telephone Number, Personal Email Address
            3. Emergency Contact Name and Relationship
            4. Voluntary self-identification entries: Ethnicity, Gender, Veteran Status, Disability Status

    III. Prohibited handling
        A. Storage of Tier 1 or Tier 2 fields in local spreadsheets, personal note applications, or unmanaged cloud storage.
        B. Transmission of Tier 1 through Tier 3 fields by electronic mail, text message, or facsimile to an unverified endpoint.
        C. Retention of screening documentation in the candidate record rather than the segregated medical file.
        D. Disclosure to a client contact of anything beyond the fitness determination.
        E. Access to a record without an assigned requisition or payroll task.

    IV. Consequences
        A. Detection: gateway blocking, quarterly access audit, client audit, or self-report.
        B. Individual: entitlement suspension on confirmation; documented corrective action; termination for intentional disclosure or for concealment.
        C. Organizational: contract remedies, indemnity claims, state breach notification, and regulatory findings under FCRA and the ADA confidentiality provisions.
        D. Aggravating factor: failure to report within one hour of discovery.

    V. Scenario check
        A. A requisition note records the reason a worker declined a shift, and the reason is a medical condition. Classify and correct.
        B. An audit shows a payroll account was opened by a specialist with no assigned task on that record. Identify the violated control.
        C. A screening result is attached to an outbound message and the gateway blocks delivery. State the required next step.
        D. Responses: A — Tier 2; remove the note, record only availability, move detail to the segregated file. B — access without business need, Section III.E. C — treat as an attempted disclosure, report to the Records Custodian, resend through the portal.
