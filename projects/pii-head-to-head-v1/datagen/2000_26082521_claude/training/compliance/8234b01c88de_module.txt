PATIENT FINANCIAL SERVICES DIVISION
BILLING OFFICE COMPLIANCE EDUCATION
Course PFS-114: Handling of Restricted Information
Participant Handout — Program Year 2004, Revision 3

1.0 Purpose and Scope. This handout accompanies the ninety-minute classroom session required annually of every person assigned to the Billing Office, including full-time and part-time employees, agency temporaries, students on clinical rotation, and contracted early-out collectors, and including those whose duties are limited to cashiering, mail opening, coupon posting, or operation of the microfilm and document imaging equipment. The obligations described below arise under the federal Standards for Privacy of Individually Identifiable Health Information, in force at this facility since April 14, 2003, under state law governing the confidentiality of hospital records, and under the Employee Confidentiality Agreement executed at the time of hire and reaffirmed each year. Attendance is recorded by the Education Coordinator and reported to the Privacy Officer; failure to attend a scheduled session or an approved make-up session is itself a compliance deficiency and is handled under clause 6.0.

2.0 Categories of Restricted Information. Staff frequently assume that only the clinical chart is protected. That assumption is incorrect and is the most common source of inadvertent disclosure in this department. Restricted information includes, first, the registration and demographic elements captured on the face sheet and in the admitting screens, among them Patient Name, Medical Record Number, Account Number, Date of Birth, Sex, Marital Status, Street Address, Home Telephone, Work Telephone, Employer Name, Social Security Number, Guarantor Name, and Guarantor Relationship. Second, it includes the clinical elements that pass through this office in the ordinary course of billing, among them Admitting Diagnosis, Principal Diagnosis Code, Secondary Diagnosis Codes, Procedure Code, Attending Physician, Admission Date, Discharge Date, Discharge Disposition, and Revenue Code descriptions appearing on the itemized statement. Third, it includes financial elements such as Total Charges, Contractual Adjustment, Patient Balance, Payment Method, Credit Card Type, Bank Routing, and the contents of any payment plan agreement. Fourth, it includes payer elements such as Subscriber Name, Subscriber Identification Number, Group Number, Plan Code, Certification Number, and Authorization Number. Fifth, it includes non-patient restricted material, such as payer contract rate schedules, the charge description master, and personnel records of coworkers.

3.0 The Minimum Necessary Standard. Access to the patient accounting system is granted by job class, not by curiosity. Clause 3.1: an employee may retrieve only those accounts required by an assigned work list, correspondence item, telephone inquiry, or supervisor's directive. Clause 3.2: the inquiry of one's own account, or the account of a relative, neighbor, coworker, or public figure, is prohibited without exception, and remains prohibited even where the employee holds a legitimate interest in the balance; such inquiries must be routed to the Patient Accounts Manager. Clause 3.3: system audit trails record the operator identifier, terminal, date, and time of every account accessed, and these trails are reviewed monthly.

4.0 Verification Before Disclosure. Clause 4.1: before releasing any balance or account detail by telephone, the representative shall verify the caller against at least two elements on file. Clause 4.2: a request from a person other than the patient or the guarantor of record requires a signed authorization on file, except where the requester is the payer, a business associate under contract, or a party otherwise permitted under the treatment, payment, and health care operations provisions. Clause 4.3: no restricted information shall be left on an answering machine or voice mail beyond the office name and a return number.

5.0 Safeguards. Clause 5.1: facsimile transmissions require the approved cover sheet and confirmation of the destination number before sending. Clause 5.2: workstations shall be locked when unattended, and the password-protected screen saver shall not be disabled. Clause 5.3: diskettes, printouts, aged trial balance reports, and remittance advices shall be secured in the locked file room at the close of business and destroyed only in the designated shredding receptacles. Clause 5.4: no restricted information shall be removed from the premises without the written authorization of the Director of Patient Financial Services.

6.0 Consequences of Mishandling. Clause 6.1: the facility applies progressive discipline, beginning with documented counseling for a first inadvertent lapse and proceeding to written warning, suspension without pay, and termination. Clause 6.2: an intentional access, an access for personal curiosity, or any disclosure for private gain is treated as a terminable offense at first occurrence, without progressive steps. Clause 6.3: federal civil monetary penalties of one hundred dollars per violation, to an annual maximum of twenty-five thousand dollars for violations of an identical requirement, may be assessed against the facility, and the facility reserves its rights against the responsible individual. Clause 6.4: criminal penalties reaching fifty thousand dollars and one year of imprisonment, and substantially greater sums and terms where the offense is committed under false pretenses or for commercial advantage, attach to the individual and are not indemnified. Clause 6.5: licensure and certification bodies are notified where applicable.

7.0 Duty to Report. Any employee who becomes aware of a suspected disclosure, a misdirected facsimile, a lost report, or an account accessed without business purpose shall notify the immediate supervisor and the Privacy Officer the same business day. Good-faith reporting is protected; concealment is not.

8.0 Scenario Examination. Answer in writing and return to the instructor before leaving the room.

8.1 A caller states that he or she is the adult child of a recently discharged patient, cannot supply the account number, and asks for the outstanding balance in order to pay it. What verification is required, what may be disclosed, and to whom is the matter escalated if verification fails?

8.2 A representative recognizes the name on a work-list account as a former neighbor, and opens the account solely to see the diagnosis. No information is repeated to anyone. Identify every clause violated and state the disciplinary exposure.

8.3 An itemized statement is faxed to a payer, and the confirmation sheet shows a destination number one digit different from the number on file. State the actions required, the deadline for reporting, and the person to whom the report is made.

8.4 A collector wishes to work from home over the weekend and prints an aged trial balance containing Patient Name, Account Number, and Patient Balance columns. State whether this is permitted and on what condition.
