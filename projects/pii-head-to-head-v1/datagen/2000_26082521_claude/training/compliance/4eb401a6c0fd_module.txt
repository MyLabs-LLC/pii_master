SLIDE SCRIPT — INFORMATION HANDLING REFRESHER
Presenter: Records Custodian, Office of Information Governance
Duration: 25 minutes
Audience: Customer Accounts and Meter Services

SLIDE 1 — WHY WE ARE HERE
Say: "Every one of you touches customer data daily. The Authority holds information that customers are compelled by law to give us — they cannot shop for another water provider. That compulsion creates a heightened duty."

SLIDE 2 — THE RESTRICTED LIST
Say: "Memorize the labels, not the values. If you see any of these column headings on a screen, a report, or a printout, you are in restricted territory."

Read aloud:
  Account Holder Name · Service Address · Mailing Address · Telephone Number
  Email Address · Date of Birth · Social Security Number · Bank Routing Number
  Bank Account Number · Payment Card Number · Delinquency Balance
  Medical Necessity Certification Flag · Life-Support Equipment Designation
  Address Confidentiality Program Participant Flag · Employee Identification Number
  SCADA Endpoint Identifier · Substation Keying Schedule

SLIDE 3 — THE THREE FAILURE MODES
Say: "Nearly every incident we have investigated falls into one of three buckets."
  1. Wrong recipient — correct data, unauthorized party.
  2. Wrong container — restricted data placed in an unprotected medium
     (personal device, unencrypted attachment, shared drive, printed and left).
  3. Wrong volume — a legitimate request answered with a bulk extract
     rather than the single record actually needed.

SLIDE 4 — WHAT IT COSTS
Say: "This is not abstract."
  · Discipline up to termination under Personnel Rule 12.4.
  · Individual civil exposure; indemnification is not automatic.
  · Criminal referral for confidential-address and infrastructure categories.
  · Mandatory customer and Attorney General notification.
  · Notification costs charged to your Division's budget.
  · Access credentials suspended during investigation.

SLIDE 5 — SCENARIO QUIZ (poll the room; no writing required)

  Scenario One. A walk-in customer asks a representative to read back the
  payment card number on file "to confirm it's the right one." Permissible?
  [Answer: No. Never read back a stored payment credential. Confirm by last
  four only if the party is already authenticated as the account holder.]

  Scenario Two. A supervisor asks a clerk to email a spreadsheet of past-due
  accounts — Account Holder Name, Service Address, Delinquency Balance — to a
  collections vendor. Permissible?
  [Answer: Only if a current data protection addendum is on file and the
  transmission uses the approved secure transfer method. Verify both.]

  Scenario Three. A meter technician's tablet displays a Life-Support
  Equipment Designation. A neighbor at the curb asks why the technician is
  spending extra time at that property. Permissible response?
  [Answer: Decline. State only that the visit is routine. The designation is
  a protected program element.]

  Scenario Four. A staff member finds a printed work order in the break room
  showing Account Holder Name and Service Address. Required action?
  [Answer: Secure it immediately, place it in the locked shred bin, and file
  an incident report within 24 hours. Finding it does not excuse reporting.]

SLIDE 6 — CLOSING
Say: "When in doubt, stop and call the Records Custodian. There is no penalty for asking. There is a substantial penalty for guessing."
