INTEROFFICE MEMORANDUM

TO:      All Program Managers, Supervisors, and Unit Leads
FROM:    Office of the Records Custodian
SUBJECT: Confidentiality In-Service Module Three — Supervisory Reinforcement Points and Quiz Key
CC:      Personnel Officer; County Counsel; Training Coordinator

Supervisors are directed to deliver the following reinforcement points at the next regularly scheduled unit meeting following completion of Modules One and Two by their staff, and to record the delivery in the unit meeting minutes. The narrative form below is provided so that the material may be read aloud without paraphrase; supervisors are cautioned that paraphrase has in past years produced material variance in what staff understand the rule to be, and variance is precisely the condition under which improper disclosure occurs.

The first reinforcement point is that the restriction attaches to the information and not to the medium. Staff frequently form the impression that the client information system is the locus of confidentiality, and that a note upon a message pad, a name written upon a whiteboard in a clinic corridor, or an appointment book left open upon a counter is somehow outside the rule. It is not. Client Name, Date of Birth, Medical Record Number, Case Number, Residence Address, Home Telephone, Diagnosis Code, Test Result, and every other restricted field carries its restriction wherever it is written, spoken, displayed, or stored. A whiteboard is a record. An appointment book is a record. A sticky note is a record.

The second reinforcement point concerns need to know. Need to know is not established by employment with the Department, by curiosity however well-intentioned, by concern for the welfare of the person, by having been the person who originally took the intake, or by supervisory rank. It is established by a current work assignment which cannot be performed without the specific field sought. Supervisors are reminded that they hold no general right of access to clinical records of their own subordinates and that a request for a subordinate's Diagnosis Code, Treatment Plan, or Medication and Dosage for scheduling, accommodation, or return-to-work purposes must be routed through the Personnel Officer, who will obtain only such certification as the law permits and will not obtain the underlying clinical fields.

The third reinforcement point concerns the duty upon receipt of an external demand. Staff are not to evaluate the validity of a subpoena, a court order, a law enforcement request, an attorney's letter, or a request from another jurisdiction. Staff are to accept the document, note the time of receipt, disclose nothing, and transmit the document to this Office the same business day for referral to County Counsel. A well-meaning employee who releases records in response to a facially valid but legally insufficient demand has committed the disclosure notwithstanding good faith.

The fourth reinforcement point concerns consequences, which supervisors are asked to state plainly rather than to soften. The available dispositions are documented counseling, written reprimand, suspension without pay, demotion, and dismissal. Dismissal upon a first offense is available and has been imposed. Referral to the District Attorney and to the applicable licensing board is mandatory in defined circumstances and is not within the discretion of the unit. Personal civil liability is not indemnified where the disclosure was willful or outside the course and scope of duty.

QUIZ KEY — for supervisory use in discussing Module Two, Section 6.

(a) Disclose nothing. Refer the caller to emergency medical services. Notify this Office. Urgency does not create authority; the emergency treatment exception, where it applies at all, is exercised by clinical staff under protocol and not by the person answering the telephone.

(b) Report to your immediate supervisor and to this Office before the close of the next business day. You are not to confront the coworker, and you are not to make an independent determination whether that coworker had an assignment justifying access.

(c) The general nature of an investigation, the number of cases where the number is large enough not to identify, and the public health guidance being issued may be discussed — by the designated public information contact only, and by no one else. No field listed in Module Two, Section 2.1 may be discussed with any member of the press under any circumstance.

(d) Secure the document immediately. Do not read beyond what is necessary to identify it. Deliver it to the Records Custodian, who will determine the originating unit, complete an incident log entry, and determine whether notification obligations have been triggered. Do not shred it; it is now evidence of a potential incident.

(e) No. Access to one's own record is obtained by the same request procedure available to any member of the public, upon Form DPH-40. The reason is that the audit trail cannot distinguish a legitimate self-access from a pretextual one, and permitting the practice would render the audit trail useless as a control.
