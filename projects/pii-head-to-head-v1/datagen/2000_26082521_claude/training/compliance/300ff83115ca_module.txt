RESTRICTED INFORMATION: WHAT STAFF MUST KNOW
Slide Script — Annual Privacy In-Service, 2004
Presenter: Privacy Officer or designee | Duration: 25 minutes

SLIDE 1 — TITLE
ON SCREEN: Course title; policy citation ADM-114; agency seal.
SCRIPT: "This module covers three points: which categories of information
are restricted, what happens when they are mishandled, and how the rules
apply to situations you will encounter this week."

SLIDE 2 — THE LEGAL BASIS
ON SCREEN: Bulleted citations to the federal privacy rule, the state
communicable disease statute, the state vital statistics act, and the
public records act exemptions.
SCRIPT: "Restriction is not departmental preference. Each category on the
next slide is closed by a specific authority. When you refuse a request,
you are applying a statute, not exercising discretion."

SLIDE 3 — CATEGORY MATRIX

+----------------------------+--------------------------+----------------+
| RESTRICTED CATEGORY        | MINIMUM SAFEGUARD        | RETENTION      |
+----------------------------+--------------------------+----------------+
| Clinical chart, paper      | Locked file room; sign-  | 10 years       |

| STD/HIV case report        | Sealed envelope; hand    | Per statute    |

|                            | storage overnight        |                |
+----------------------------+--------------------------+----------------+

|                            | courier transfer         | close          |
+----------------------------+--------------------------+----------------+

SCRIPT: "Read the middle column as a floor, not a ceiling. The safeguard
attaches to the category, not to the room you happen to be standing in."

SLIDE 4 — FIELD LABELS THAT TRIGGER RESTRICTION
ON SCREEN: Patient Name. Date of Birth. Social Security Number. Medical
Record Number. Diagnosis Code. Contact Name. Home Address. Account Number.
SCRIPT: "If a form, screen, or spreadsheet column carries any of these
labels, the whole document inherits the restriction. Removing one column
does not declassify the remainder."

SLIDE 5 — CONSEQUENCES
ON SCREEN: Four-step escalation graphic.
SCRIPT: "First, an internal finding: counseling and retraining. Second,
loss of system access, which in practice ends most clinical assignments.
Third, termination and referral to the licensing board where a professional
license is held. Fourth, referral for prosecution. Disclosure for personal
gain moves the matter to step four immediately. Civil penalties are
assessed against the department, and the department recovers through the
disciplinary process."

SLIDE 6 — SCENARIO ONE
ON SCREEN: A records clerk is asked by a local reporter to confirm whether
a person treated at the clinic was diagnosed with a reportable disease.
SCRIPT: "The correct response is neither confirmation nor denial. Refer the
inquiry to the Public Information Officer and log the contact."

SLIDE 7 — SCENARIO TWO
ON SCREEN: A billing technician finds an eligibility packet left in the
photocopier overnight.
SCRIPT: "Secure the packet, then report it the same shift. The event is
reportable whether or not anyone read it."

SLIDE 8 — SCENARIO THREE
ON SCREEN: A nurse is asked by a relative, employed in another division,
about a case the nurse is managing.
SCRIPT: "Employment in the department is not authorization. Access follows
assignment. Decline and document."

SLIDE 9 — CLOSE
ON SCREEN: Reporting channel; Records Custodian; Form HD-114A.
SCRIPT: "Report within one business day. Prompt reporting is the single
factor that most often keeps an event at step one."
