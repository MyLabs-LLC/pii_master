RESTRICTED INFORMATION AWARENESS — PRESENTER SCRIPT
Quarterly Compliance Session, Third Quarter 2021
Prepared by the Compliance Department for delivery by the Branch Compliance Liaison
Estimated runtime: eighteen minutes

SLIDE 1 — TITLE
ON SCREEN: "What We Protect, Why We Protect It, and What Happens If We Don't"
NARRATION: Good morning. Every file we touch contains information that a consumer surrendered involuntarily as a condition of closing. They had no meaningful choice. That fact, and not merely the regulation, is the basis of the obligation described in this session.

SLIDE 2 — THE FOUR RESTRICTED CATEGORIES
ON SCREEN: Four quadrants labeled Identity, Financial, Transactional, Derived
NARRATION: Read the quadrants aloud with the room. Identity covers the fields labeled Social Security Number, Date of Birth, Driver's License Number, and Legal Name. Financial covers Account Number, Routing Number, Card Number, and Payoff Amount. Transactional covers Wire Instructions, Earnest Money Deposit, and Loan Number. Derived covers anything we compute or infer — Credit Score, Income, and any Consumer Report. Emphasize: the column heading on the form is not the secret. The value typed under it is.

SLIDE 3 — WHERE IT LEAKS
ON SCREEN: Printer tray, personal e-mail, unencrypted attachment, spoken hallway conversation, unlocked workstation, uncrosscut disposal
NARRATION: Six exposures, none of them exotic. Five of the six require no technical sophistication whatsoever on the part of the party who benefits.

SLIDE 4 — CONSEQUENCE LADDER
ON SCREEN: Counseling → Suspension → Termination → Regulatory referral → Personal civil liability → Loss of commission
NARRATION: State plainly that intent is not an element. Negligent disclosure and deliberate disclosure occupy the same rung on this ladder for purposes of the employment consequence. Only the regulatory outcome distinguishes them.

SLIDE 5 — SCENARIO EXERCISE (facilitated discussion; no written answers collected)
ON SCREEN: Three scenarios, revealed one at a time

  Scenario A. An escrow officer takes a call from a party claiming to represent the lender, who asks that the payoff figure and the borrower's account number be read back for verification. The caller supplies the file number correctly. Should the request be honored? Under what verification procedure, if any?

  Scenario B. A transaction coordinator working remotely photographs a monitor displaying a commitment schedule so the image can be reviewed later on a personal device. The photograph is deleted the same evening. Has an incident occurred, and is the deletion relevant to that determination?

  Scenario C. A branch receives a courier delivery of paper files intended for a different branch. The receiving clerk opens the envelope, recognizes the error, and reseals it. What must be logged, and to whom is the log delivered?

SLIDE 6 — CLOSE
NARRATION: Direct all participants to the incident reporting form on the intranet compliance page. Remind the room that reporting within one hour of discovery is a mitigating factor in every disposition the Company has ever issued; concealment has never been one.
