MEMORANDUM

TO:      Members of the Credit Union
FROM:    The Member Services Supervisor
RE:      Protecting Your Personal Information — A Short Guide

Identity theft is now the most common complaint received by consumer protection authorities. Most cases begin not with a computer intrusion but with paper, a telephone call, or an email. This handout explains what we do, what we will never do, and what you can do.

WHAT WE WILL NEVER DO

We will never telephone, email, or write to you asking you to supply, confirm, or "re-verify":

  - your Social Security Number
  - your account, card, or member number
  - your PIN or online banking password
  - your date of birth or mother's maiden name
  - the answers to your security questions

We already hold this information. Any message asking for it is fraudulent, regardless of how closely it resembles our letterhead, our logo, or our website. We will never send you a link and ask you to log in through it.

WHAT TO SHRED AT HOME

A cross-cut shredder costs less than a single fraudulent charge. Shred before discarding:

  - account statements and cancelled checks
  - deposit and withdrawal receipts, and ATM slips
  - pre-approved credit offers, whether opened or not
  - convenience checks mailed with a card statement
  - expired cards, cut through the account number and the magnetic stripe
  - insurance and investment statements
  - anything showing your Social Security Number
  - old tax records past your retention need
  - address labels from prescription containers and magazine wrappers

Pre-approved offers deserve particular attention. An offer removed from a kerbside recycling bin can be redirected to another address and accepted in your name.

WHAT NEVER TO SEND BY EMAIL

Ordinary email is not private. Treat it as a postcard. Never place in an email, to us or to anyone:

  - a Social Security Number
  - a full account or card number
  - a PIN, password, or security answer
  - an image of a check or driver's licence

To send documents securely, ask any branch representative for the secure upload facility, or bring the documents to a branch. To discuss your account, telephone us using the number printed on your statement or on the back of your card — not a number provided in an email or by a caller.

RECOGNISING A PRETEXTING CALL

A pretexting caller invents a story to obtain your information. They may claim to be from the credit union, a card issuer, a government agency, a charity, or a prize promotion. The signs:

  - they call you, then ask you to prove who you are
  - they know one true fact and use it to seem legitimate
  - they create urgency: an account will be closed, a warrant issued, a prize forfeited
  - they discourage you from hanging up or checking

Hang up. Call the institution back on a number you already had. A genuine caller will not object.

OTHER SENSIBLE STEPS

Review your statement every month and report anything unfamiliar within sixty days. Collect mail promptly and place a hold when travelling. Request your free credit report once a year from each of the three nationwide bureaus. Do not carry your Social Security card in your wallet. Memorise your PIN; do not write it on the card or keep it in the same wallet.

IF YOU BELIEVE YOU HAVE BEEN VICTIMISED

Telephone or visit any branch and ask for the Member Services Supervisor. We will restrict the affected accounts, issue new cards and numbers, and refer the matter to our Fraud Investigator. We will also help you place a fraud alert with the credit bureaus and complete a police report. Do this promptly; your protections under federal law are strongest when the report is early.
