MEMORANDUM

TO:      All Employees
FROM:    The Information Security Officer
RE:      Pretexting — What the Call Sounds Like
COPY:    Branch Managers, Training Coordinator

We have received three pretexting attempts against our call centre this quarter. Two were refused. One resulted in the disclosure of a member's balance and last transaction. That employee followed our written procedure only partly. This memo describes what the calls actually sounded like so that you will recognise the fourth one.

THE SETUP

The caller opens with something true. They may know the member's name, the branch, or the fact that a loan was recently closed. This information is inexpensive to obtain — from a mailbox, a discarded statement, an obituary, or a previous call to us in which a small fact was confirmed. Do not treat opening knowledge as verification. Each fact the caller already holds is a fact someone gave away.

THE ASK

It is always small. Not "read me the account number" but:

- "Can you confirm the balance is under a thousand? I just need to know if the check will clear."
- "I'm the son. She's in the hospital. Is the direct deposit posted?"
- "This is the title company. I need the payoff figure faxed today."
- "I'm calling from the item processing vendor. What core release are you on?"

The last one is not about a member at all. Information about our systems, our verification questions, our staffing, and our hold procedures is also protected. Vendors and examiners do not obtain it by cold call.

THE PRESSURE

Time, sympathy, or authority. A closing at four o'clock. A grieving relative. A supervisor at another branch who is annoyed that you are being difficult. A real colleague will not be annoyed. A real examiner arrives with credentials through the Chief Executive Officer's office, not by telephone.

WHAT YOU DO

Verify fully or decline. There is no partial verification. Do not confirm that a person banks here. Do not read back any digit of any identifier. Do not accept a callback number offered by the caller — use the number in the core system only. End the call politely if pressed.

WHAT YOU DO NEXT

Report it to me the same day, even if you refused and nothing was disclosed. Refused attempts tell us who is being targeted. Note the time, the claimed identity, the exact request, and any number displayed. Your Branch Manager should hear of it as well. If you believe anything was disclosed, tell me immediately — not tomorrow, not after you have decided how bad it was. Early notice lets the Fraud Investigator place a watch on the account before loss occurs.

No one has ever been disciplined here for refusing a caller.
