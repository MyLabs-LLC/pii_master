OFFICE OF INFORMATION SERVICES
GUIDANCE HANDOUT — PROTECTING IDENTIFIERS IN DAILY WORK
For distribution at site in-service; retain in the staff handbook

(1) Every clerk, teacher, aide, coach, and contractor handles identifiers. This handout states the minimum practice.

(2) SHRED. Place in the locked console: withdrawn student folders, duplicate transcript printouts, testing accommodation lists, bus manifests, field trip permission slips, athletic physical forms, fee waiver applications, and any page bearing a Student Identification Number.

(3) DO NOT RECYCLE. Paper placed in a recycling bin leaves District control unshredded.

(4) NEVER EMAIL. The following are prohibited in any message, attachment, or calendar invitation:
  — Social Security Number
  — Date of Birth paired with Home Address
  — Bank Routing Number or Direct Deposit Account Number
  — Immunization Record or medication order
  — Screenshots of the student information system

(5) NEVER TEXT. Personal messaging applications are outside the records system and cannot be produced on request.

(6) PRETEXTING. Expect the caller to sound routine. Watch for:
  — a claim of vendor support during an outage no one announced;
  — a request that you read a field aloud rather than send it through a ticket;
  — a stated deadline that would expire before you could verify;
  — flattery, then a small request, then a larger one;
  — a caller who asks who else is in the office today.

(7) You are never obligated to answer. Terminate the call. Write down the time, the number of the extension that received it, the substance of the request, and any name the caller offered.

(8) CONTACTS.
  Records and release of records — the Records Custodian
  Privacy incidents and pretexting reports — the Data Privacy Officer
  Passwords, devices, phishing — the Help Desk Supervisor
  Staff records — the Director of Human Resources
  Pay and banking — the Payroll Supervisor
  Health records — the Health Services Coordinator
  Meal eligibility — the Food Services Director

(9) When in doubt, do not send it. Ask the Data Privacy Officer first.
