OFFICE OF THE BUSINESS OFFICE DIRECTOR
                 GUIDANCE ON THE PROTECTION OF PERSONAL IDENTIFIERS
                              Handout Series, Number Seven
                         Issued for the plan year commencing 1999

     I.   STATEMENT OF THE OBLIGATION

          A.   Every employee of Patient Accounts, Admitting, Cashiering,
               and Collections holds a duty of confidence over the
               identifiers gathered in the course of billing.
          B.   The duty survives the closure of the account, the transfer
               of the balance to an outside agency, and the separation of
               the employee from the institution.
          C.   Curiosity is not a business purpose. Access the record only
               where the account before you requires it.

     II.  THE PROTECTED FIELDS

          The following labels appear on the face sheet, the UB-92, the
          HCFA-1500, and the collection worksheet. Each is protected:

               Patient Name .................. Guarantor Name
               Medical Record Number ......... Account Number
               Social Security Number ........ Date of Birth
               Guarantor Address ............. Employer Name
               Subscriber Identification ..... Group Number
               Policy Number ................. Admission Date

     III. MATTER REQUIRING DESTRUCTION

          A.   Shred, by cross-cut machine or by deposit in a locked
               destruction console:

               1.   Face sheets, encounter forms, and superbills once the
                    charge has posted.
               2.   Insurance verification worksheets and benefit
                    call sheets.
               3.   Explanation of benefits documents, remittance advices,
                    and denial correspondence after posting.
               4.   Aged trial balance printouts, unbilled reports, and
                    census listings, without exception, at day's end.
               5.   Thermal facsimile pages, which fade and are therefore
                    frequently mistaken for blank stock.
               6.   Draft correspondence, envelope proofs, misprinted
                    statements, and the discard tray of the laser printer.

          B.   Do not shred, but surrender to the Records Custodian:
               diskettes, backup cartridges, microfiche jackets, and any
               original document bearing an original signature.

     IV.  ELECTRONIC MAIL AND FACSIMILE

          A.   No message carrying a Social Security Number, Date of
               Birth, Account Number, or Policy Number may be sent over
               the Internet. Mail leaving this network travels in the
               clear and is copied at every hop between here and its
               destination.
          B.   Internal mail is not a safe harbour. Reference the account
               by internal batch sequence and hold the identifiers for
               the telephone or the interoffice envelope.
          C.   Facsimile transmissions are preceded by a cover sheet, are
               directed only to a machine confirmed to sit in a controlled
               area, and are confirmed by return call.
          D.   Home computers, personal dial-up services, and personal
               mail accounts are outside the scope of authorised
               processing and may not be used for any account matter.

     V.   RECOGNISING THE PRETEXT CALL

          A pretext caller is a person who telephones under a false
          pretence to draw out an identifier. The following indicators,
          taken together, warrant refusal:

          A.   The caller supplies partial information and invites you to
               supply the remainder.
          B.   The caller asserts authority — an auditor, a supervisor, a
               physician's office, a sister facility — but cannot be
               reached at a number in the house directory.
          C.   The caller manufactures urgency, invokes a senior title, or
               expresses displeasure at being asked to hold.
          D.   The call arrives at shift change, at the meal relief, or
               after the close of the administrative day, when experienced
               staff are fewest.
          E.   The caller resists the offer of a return call. A legitimate
               enquirer accepts a call back without complaint.

     VI.  INTERNAL CONTACTS BY ROLE

          A.   First instance: the Patient Accounts Manager.
          B.   Records and destruction questions: the Records Custodian.
          C.   Access, passwords, terminals: the Information Systems
               Security Coordinator.
          D.   Suspected improper disclosure: the Corporate Compliance
               Officer, who may be reached without notice to the
               employee's supervisor.
          E.   After hours and weekends: the House Supervisor.
