INFORMATION FOR PATIENTS AND GUARANTORS
HOW THIS OFFICE PROTECTS YOUR PERSONAL INFORMATION
Patient Financial Services — Handout PFS-C2 — 2004 Edition

Clause 1. Purpose of this handout.
This office bills for services you have received. This handout states what we will ask you for, what we will never ask you for, and what to do if you are contacted by someone claiming to act on our behalf.

Clause 2. What we may ask you to confirm.
When you call us, we will ask you to confirm a limited number of items already held in your record. These are drawn from the following fields:

     Patient Name
     Date of Birth
     Account Number as shown on your statement
     Last four positions of the Social Security Number
     Guarantor Mailing Address of record
     Subscriber Identification Number

Clause 3. What we will never do.
3.1 We will never ask you to send a Social Security Number, Payment Card Number, Card Verification Value, Bank Routing Number, or Checking Account Number by electronic mail.
3.2 We will never send you an electronic mail message containing a Diagnosis Code, Procedure Code, or Medical Record Number.
3.3 We will never ask for your online banking password, your sign-on to any web site, or a Personal Identification Number.
3.4 We will never require immediate payment by wire transfer to avoid a stated consequence.

Clause 4. Recognizing a pretexting call.
A pretexting call is one in which a caller invents a reason to obtain your personal information. Indicators include:
4.1 The caller states that your account will be sent to collection, or your coverage cancelled, unless you answer immediately.
4.2 The caller declines to give a name, a department, or a number at which they may be reached.
4.3 The caller already appears to hold part of your information and asks you to complete or correct it.
4.4 The caller asks for information a hospital would already have, such as your Date of Birth or Medical Record Number.
4.5 The caller asks you to remain on the line while you retrieve a card or a statement.

Clause 5. What to do.
5.1 End the call. You are not obliged to remain on the line.
5.2 Call this office back using the number printed on your statement, not a number given to you by the caller.
5.3 Ask for the Patient Financial Services Manager and state that you believe you received a pretexting call.

Clause 6. Documents in your possession.
6.1 Statements, remittance advices, and explanation of benefits notices carry your Account Number and Medical Record Number. Destroy them by cross-cut shredding when you no longer need them.
6.2 Do not discard them intact in household or workplace refuse.

Clause 7. Further contact.
7.1 Questions concerning the privacy of your information: the Privacy Officer.
7.2 Requests for copies of records: the Records Custodian.
7.3 Both may be reached in writing at the address printed on your statement, marked to the attention of the role named above.
