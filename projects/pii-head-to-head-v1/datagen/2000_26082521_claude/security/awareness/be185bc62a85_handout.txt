MERIDIAN COAST TITLE & ESCROW COMPANY
EMPLOYEE GUIDANCE HANDOUT — PROTECTING CUSTOMER IDENTIFIERS
Issued by the Office of the Privacy Coordinator
Effective: March 2011 — Review annually

I. PURPOSE
    A. This handout explains the handling rules that apply to every escrow,
       title, and closing file held in this branch.
    B. It applies to all staff, including temporary and contract personnel,
       from the first day of assignment.
    C. It supplements, and does not replace, the company Information
       Security Program adopted under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley safeguards
       requirements.

II. WHAT COUNTS AS A PROTECTED IDENTIFIER
    A. Any of the following field labels, when filled in on a form, in a
       spreadsheet column, or in an escrow accounting screen:
        1. Borrower Name / Seller Name / Buyer Name
        2. Social Security Number or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number
        3. Date of Birth
        4. Driver's License Number and Issuing State
        5. Loan Number, Escrow Number when paired with a name
        6. Bank Routing Number, Deposit Account Number, Wire Beneficiary Account
        7. Credit Report Score and Tradeline Detail
        8. Home Address, Mailing Address, Personal Telephone, Personal Email
    B. A blank form is not protected. A completed form is protected from the
       moment the first identifier is entered.

III. WHAT TO SHRED
    A. Shred, do not recycle and do not discard whole:
        1. Working copies of the settlement statement, preliminary report, and
           closing instructions.
        2. Draft wire instructions and any handwritten note bearing an account
           or routing field.
        3. Copies of identification documents made at signing.
        4. Fax cover sheets and confirmation pages that display a customer name.
        5. Mis-printed or jammed pages pulled from the copier or printer.
    B. Use the locked shred consoles only. Do not stack material on top of a
       console or beside it overnight.
    C. Cross-cut desk shredders may be used for single pages. Bulk destruction
       goes to the console for the certified vendor pickup.
    D. Media — CDs, diskettes, thumb drives, backup tapes — is never placed in
       a shred console. Surrender it to the Records Custodian for degaussing
       or physical destruction.

IV. WHAT NEVER GOES BY EMAIL
    A. Do not send, forward, or reply with any of the following in the body of
       a message or in an unprotected attachment:
        1. Social Security Number or Taxpayer Identification Number
        2. Full Deposit Account Number or Routing Number
        3. Wire Instructions of any kind
        4. Images or scans of identification documents
        5. Credit report content
    B. Approved alternatives:
        1. The company secure document portal, with the recipient enrolled
           beforehand.
        2. Courier or certified mail in a sealed inner envelope.
        3. Verbal confirmation by callback to a number obtained from the file,
           never from the requesting message.
    C. Personal web mail accounts are not to be used for company business in
       any direction, including sending a file to yourself to work at home.

V. RECOGNISING A PRETEXTING CALL
    A. Common patterns:
        1. The caller claims to be the lender's closer, an auditor, or a
           company officer, and is calling from an unfamiliar line.
        2. The caller supplies part of an identifier and asks you to confirm
           or complete the rest.
        3. The caller applies time pressure — a rate lock expiring, a signing
           in progress, a wire cut-off.
        4. The caller asks you to bypass the callback step "just this once."
    B. Required response:
        1. Take the caller's name, company, and file reference.
        2. State that you will return the call through the number of record.
        3. End the call. Do not confirm or deny any field on the file.
        4. Report the contact the same business day.

VI. WHO TO CONTACT
    A. Suspected pretexting or a request that fails verification —
       the Branch Escrow Manager, then the Privacy Coordinator.
    B. Lost file, missing media, or an open shred console found unsecured —
       the Records Custodian.
    C. Mis-sent email or fax containing an identifier — the Information
       Security Officer, immediately, before attempting any recall.
    D. Questions about retention periods or destruction schedules —
       the Records Custodian.
    E. Subpoenas, regulator requests, and law-enforcement inquiries — refer to
       the Compliance Officer without comment.
