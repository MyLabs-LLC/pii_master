MEMORANDUM

TO:      All Branch Staff, Member Services, Lending, and Operations
FROM:    The Vice President of Compliance / Privacy Officer
RE:      Safeguarding Member Identifying Information — Required Practices
CLASS:   Internal — Post at Every Workstation

This memorandum supersedes the identity-protection guidance circulated with last year's Gramm-Leach-Bliley refresher. Read it fully. Branch Managers will document staff acknowledgement in the training log.

1. WHAT MUST BE SHREDDED

Cross-cut shredding is required — strip-cut bins are being removed from service. Shred, do not discard, anything bearing:

- Member number, share or draft account number, loan or line-of-credit number
- Social Security Number or Taxpayer Identification Number
- Date of birth, mother's maiden name, or any other challenge answer
- Debit, ATM, or credit card number, expiration date, or CVV
- Driver's license or state ID number, passport number
- PIN mailers, PIN change forms, and returned PIN envelopes
- Signature cards, membership applications, and account change forms
- Credit bureau reports and any printout containing a credit score
- Wire instructions, ACH authorization forms, voided checks
- Adding-machine tapes and teller balancing sheets showing account numbers
- Fax cover sheets and confirmation pages listing member data
- Any misprinted, jammed, or test-run page from a printer or statement folder

Locked shred consoles are located at each branch. Do not place these items in a recycling bin, a trash can, or the box beside the copier. Do not carry them home. Do not leave a full console bag unattended in a hallway overnight; it goes to the vault or the secured supply room until pickup.

2. WHAT NEVER GOES BY ELECTRONIC MAIL

Our email system is not encrypted end to end. Assume any message could be read in transit or forwarded outside the credit union. The following must never appear in the body of an email, in a subject line, or in an unencrypted attachment — internally or externally:

- Social Security Number or Taxpayer Identification Number
- Full account, loan, card, or member numbers
- PINs, passwords, online banking credentials, or security question answers
- Images or scans of checks, licenses, or signature cards
- Credit reports, scores, or underwriting decisions with identifiers attached
- Lists or spreadsheets of members for any purpose

If a member emails this information to us unsolicited, do not reply with it quoted. Delete the message from the Inbox and the Deleted Items folder, and telephone the member using the number on file to continue. Report the incident to the Information Security Officer the same business day.

Where transmission is genuinely necessary, use the secure file transfer facility, interoffice courier pouch, or the encrypted fax line. The Operations Manager will arrange access.

3. RECOGNISING A PRETEXTING CALL

Pretexting is the use of a false story to talk an employee out of information. The caller is usually pleasant, hurried, and plausible. Watch for these markers:

- Claims to be a member but cannot pass full verification, and asks you to "just this once" waive a step
- Claims to be from another branch, the data centre, a vendor, an examiner, or a law firm, and requests member data by voice
- Applies time pressure: a closing, a funeral, a repossession, a system outage
- Flatters, or grows indignant, when you ask a verification question
- Asks you to confirm rather than provide — "the number ends in 4, correct?"
- Requests a callback to a number they supply instead of the number on file
- Asks what verification questions we use, what our hold times are, or who your supervisor is by name

Required response: verify by our standard procedure or decline. Never confirm or deny that a person is a member. Never read back a full identifier. If the caller persists, say that you will return the call, hang up, and dial only the number recorded in the core system. Then notify your Branch Manager and the Information Security Officer. Write down the time, what was asked, and what was said while it is fresh; the Fraud Investigator will need it.

4. DESK AND WORKSTATION DISCIPLINE

Lock your screen whenever you step away. Clear your desk of member paperwork before leaving. Retrieve print jobs immediately. Face monitors away from the lobby. Store working files in a locked drawer overnight; permanent records go to the Records Custodian for filing.

5. WHO TO CONTACT

- Suspected pretexting call, lost document, or misdirected email — Information Security Officer, then your Branch Manager
- Shred console pickup, supply of locked bags — Facilities Coordinator
- Retention questions, file retrieval, destruction certificates — Records Custodian
- Suspected member account fraud or forgery — Fraud Investigator
- Regulatory or Suspicious Activity Report questions — BSA Officer
- Member requesting our privacy notice or an opt-out — Member Services Supervisor
- Access rights, secure transfer, password resets — Information Systems Manager

When in doubt, stop and ask. No member has ever been harmed by a five-minute delay for verification. Several have been harmed by an answer given too quickly.
