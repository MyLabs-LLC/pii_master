METROPOLITAN TRANSIT AUTHORITY
CUSTOMER SERVICES DIRECTORATE
HANDOUT CS-11: KEEPING YOUR DETAILS SAFE WHEN YOU DEAL WITH US
First published March 2021

A. WHY WE ISSUE THIS HANDOUT

    A.1 The Authority holds a limited set of details about each cardholder. These are recorded under the following labels only: Cardholder Name, Correspondence Address, Date of Birth, Concession Category, Card Serial, Photograph, Journey History and, where a refund has been paid, Bank Sort Code and Account Number.

    A.2 We publish this handout so that customers can distinguish a genuine approach by the Authority from an approach made by a person seeking to obtain those details dishonestly.

B. WHAT YOU SHOULD DESTROY

    B.1 Destroy by cross-cut shredder, or return to a staffed ticket office for confidential disposal:
        1. expired concession cards, once the replacement has arrived;
        2. paper refund advices, which carry your Card Serial and the last part of your Account Number;
        3. photocopies of identity documents supplied with an application;
        4. printed journey statements, which reveal your regular travel pattern and therefore your movements.

    B.2 Do not place any of the above in domestic recycling intact.

C. WHAT WE WILL NEVER ASK YOU TO SEND BY EMAIL

    C.1 The Authority will not, in any circumstance, ask you to send by email:
        1. your full Card Number or the Security Code printed on it;
        2. your Date of Birth together with your Correspondence Address;
        3. your National Insurance Number;
        4. a scan or photograph of a passport, driving licence or benefit award letter;
        5. your Bank Sort Code or Account Number.

    C.2 Where such information is genuinely required, it is submitted through your online account, at a staffed ticket office, or on the paper form issued by post.

D. RECOGNISING A PRETEXTING CALL

    D.1 A pretexting call is one in which the caller invents a plausible reason — a "system migration", a "duplicate refund", a "final warning before cancellation" — in order to make you disclose details you would not otherwise give.

    D.2 The following features, particularly in combination, indicate that a call is not from the Authority:
        1. the caller supplies the reason for the call and then asks you to confirm the very details that would prove who you are;
        2. you are pressed to act within minutes, or told that a card will be cancelled during the call;
        3. you are asked to stay on the line while you check the number, or to disregard the number printed on the reverse of your card;
        4. you are asked for a Security Code, a password, or a one-time passcode sent to your device;
        5. payment is requested by transfer, voucher, or to an account said to belong to the Authority's "recovery team".

    D.3 End the call. Wait two minutes, or use a different line, then call the number printed on the reverse of your card or shown on the back of this handout.

E. TELLING US

    E.1 Report a suspected approach to any member of ticket office staff, who will pass it to the Station Supervisor.
    E.2 The Station Supervisor refers the matter to the Revenue Protection Lead and, where personal details may have been disclosed, to the Data Protection Officer.
    E.3 Nothing in this handout affects your right to complain to the supervisory authority.
