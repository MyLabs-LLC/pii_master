BILLING OFFICE HANDOUT
SAFEGUARDING PERSONAL INFORMATION — NOTES FOR STAFF AND FOR PATIENTS

I. WHAT WE HOLD

1. To bill an account the office holds a limited set of fields. These are: Patient Name; Date of Birth; Home Address; Home Telephone Number; Social Security Number; Medical Record Number; Guarantor Name; Guarantor Account Number; Insurance Carrier; Insurance Member Identifier; Group Number; Claim Number; Service Date; Charge Description; Balance Due.

2. Payment records hold, in addition: Payment Method; Card Number; Card Expiry Date; Card Verification Code; Bank Routing Number; Bank Account Number; Cheque Number.

3. No member of staff accesses these fields except where a current work assignment requires it. Access is audited.

II. DESTRUCTION

4. Paper leaves this office in one of two ways only: into a locked confidential-destruction bin, or into a sealed internal envelope addressed to a named role.

5. Items for the bin include statements, dunning notices, claim printouts, explanation-of-benefits pages, insurance verification sheets, payment-plan copies, returned mail with its envelope, misprints, and any handwritten note carrying a field listed at clauses 1 and 2.

6. Recycling bins, desk-side waste baskets, and the loading-bay skip are not acceptable disposal routes for any of the above.

7. Bulk destruction is witnessed and logged. Log columns: Date Received; Batch Reference; Department of Origin; Document Type; Identifier Fields Present; Destruction Method; Witnessing Role; Certificate Reference; Retention Basis.

III. ELECTRONIC MAIL AND OTHER TRANSMISSION

8. Ordinary electronic mail is not secure and is not used for identifiers. This applies to the message body, the subject line, and attachments.

9. Card Number, Card Expiry Date, and Card Verification Code are never transmitted by electronic mail, are never written on a message pad, and are never stored after the payment is posted.

10. Approved routes are the secure file transfer service, internal courier in a sealed envelope, and the verified fax line with receipt confirmation.

11. Patients are asked not to send identifiers to the office by electronic mail. Where a patient does so, the message is deleted, the patient is telephoned on the number already held on the account, and the matter is noted.

IV. PRETEXTING

12. Pretexting is a fraud technique. The caller invents an identity — a supervisor, an insurance auditor, a collection agency, a software engineer, a relative — to draw out information.

13. Warning signs: no callback route offered; unusual urgency; flattery or intimidation; a request to "confirm" a Social Security Number or Bank Account Number the caller claims already to hold; a request to send a file "just this once" to an outside address; a request that the call go unlogged.

14. The response is the same in every case. Take no action, take the caller's stated name and organisation, advise that the office will return the call through its own records, and end the call.

V. WHO TO TELL

15. Billing Office Supervisor — day-to-day questions, workload cover, bin collection.
16. Privacy Officer — suspected pretexting, misdirected statements, disclosure to a wrong party.
17. Records Custodian — retention periods, file retrieval, destruction certificates.
18. Information Security Officer — lost devices, suspected account compromise, unfamiliar software.
19. Patient Financial Services Director — complaints from patients about handling of their information.
