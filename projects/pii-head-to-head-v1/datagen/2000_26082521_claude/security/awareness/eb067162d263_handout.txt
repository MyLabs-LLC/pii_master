MEMBER SERVICES DEPARTMENT
GUIDANCE HANDOUT — PROTECTION OF PERSONAL IDENTIFIERS
Issued by: Compliance Officer | Effective: March 2016 | Review cycle: Annual

PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This handout describes the credit union's expectations for the handling, transmission, and destruction of personal identifiers by members and by staff who assist them. It applies to information collected at account opening, during loan origination, and in the course of routine servicing, whether that information is held in paper form at a branch, in imaged form within the document management system, or in transit between the member and the credit union. The identifiers of primary concern are those which, alone or in combination, permit an unauthorised party to authenticate as a member: Social Security Number, Date of Birth, Member Number, Account Number, Card Number and Card Verification Value, Driver's License Number, Mother's Maiden Name, Online Banking Username, and Personal Identification Number.

MATERIALS REQUIRING DESTRUCTION

Any document bearing one or more of the identifiers listed above is to be destroyed by cross-cut shredding when it is no longer required for a business purpose and no retention obligation applies. This category includes, without limitation, expired debit and credit cards, statement copies and courtesy reprints, deposit and withdrawal slips, loan application worksheets and supporting income documentation, credit report printouts, teller balancing tickets bearing account references, pre-approved credit solicitations received at home, and correspondence carrying an embedded Member Number. Strip-cut shredding does not satisfy the standard, as reconstruction of strip-cut output has been demonstrated. Members who do not maintain a shredder at home should not place such material in household recycling; branch lobbies accept material for destruction under the same chain of custody applied to internal records, and quarterly destruction events are scheduled by the Branch Operations Manager.

TRANSMISSION RESTRICTIONS

Standard electronic mail is not an encrypted channel and is not to be used to convey personal identifiers in either direction. Staff are prohibited from soliciting a Social Security Number, Date of Birth, full Account Number, Card Number, PIN, or Online Banking credential by email, and members should treat any such request as illegitimate regardless of the apparent sender. Where documentation must be exchanged electronically, the secure message facility within online banking is the sole approved channel; unsolicited attachments containing identifiers received by ordinary email are to be reported and deleted without forwarding. The same restriction extends to text messaging and to social media messaging services.

RECOGNITION OF PRETEXTING

Pretexting is the use of a fabricated but plausible circumstance to induce disclosure. The characteristic indicators are consistent: the caller asserts urgency, frequently citing a suspended card, a pending wire, or a fraud investigation; the caller already possesses one identifier and requests the remainder, which lends false credibility; the caller discourages a return call to a published number, or supplies an alternative number; and the caller becomes insistent when verification is proposed. Caller identification display is not evidence of origin and can be falsified at negligible cost. The correct response is to terminate the call and re-establish contact through a number obtained independently from a statement or the credit union's website.

INTERNAL POINTS OF CONTACT

Suspected disclosure, loss of documentation, or a pretexting attempt is reported to the Member Services Supervisor at the branch of account, who escalates to the Fraud Investigations Coordinator. Questions concerning retention periods and destruction authorisation are directed to the Records Custodian. Matters bearing on the Identity Theft Prevention Program are referred to the Information Security Officer, who reports quarterly to the Chief Risk Officer.
