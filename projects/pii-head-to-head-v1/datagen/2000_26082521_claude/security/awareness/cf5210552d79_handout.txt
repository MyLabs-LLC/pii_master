LAKEMOOR AREA SCHOOL DISTRICT
Information Protection Handout for Site Staff — 2016-2017 School Year
Issued by the Office of the District Records Custodian

SECTION 1 — WHY THIS MATTERS

1.1 The District holds records that describe children and employees in detail, and the value of those records to an outside party lies precisely in the fields that make them useful to us. An identity thief does not need a complete file; a Name paired with a Date of Birth and a Social Security Number is sufficient to open credit in a child's name years before that child discovers the loss.

1.2 Protection is therefore a matter of routine habit rather than of exceptional vigilance. The clauses below describe the habits expected of every person with access to a records system, a filing cabinet, or a front counter.

SECTION 2 — DESTRUCTION OF PAPER

2.1 Cross-cut shredding is the only approved method of destroying paper that bears a protected field. The protected fields for this purpose are: Social Security Number, Date of Birth, Student Identification Number, State Student Identifier, Employee Identification Number, Home Address, Home Telephone Number, Personal Email Address, Bank Routing Number, Bank Account Number, Payment Card Number, Driver License Number, Medicaid Number, and Health Plan Member Number.

2.2 Documents commonly overlooked include the daily attendance exception report, the nurse's contact log, the field trip permission slip, the substitute folder, the fingerprint clearance receipt, the direct deposit change form, and the fax confirmation sheet that reproduces the first page of what was sent. Each of these is shredded once its operational purpose has ended.

2.3 Shred consoles are emptied only by the contracted destruction vendor under the observation of a District employee. Staff do not remove material from a console once deposited, and do not use console contents as scratch paper.

SECTION 3 — WHAT IS NEVER SENT BY EMAIL

3.1 No protected field listed at clause 2.1 is placed in the body of an email, in an attachment, in a subject line, or in a screenshot pasted into a message. This holds for messages between District accounts as well as for messages leaving the District.

3.2 Where information must move, staff use the secure file transfer service, the encrypted student information system export, or a hand delivery recorded in the transfer log. Staff do not forward records to a personal account in order to work from home.

SECTION 4 — PRETEXTING

4.1 Pretexting is the practice of inventing a plausible reason for a request in order to extract information. The pretexts encountered most often at school sites are: a caller stating that a parent is at the hospital and that a Date of Birth is needed immediately; a caller identifying as an auditor, a benefits carrier, or a state official, who declines to give a department; a caller claiming a system outage requires the recipient to read a record aloud; and a caller who already knows part of an identifier and asks only for confirmation.

4.2 Staff verify nothing over the telephone, confirm nothing, and correct nothing. A caller who is legitimate can submit a written request, which is the ordinary path in any event.

SECTION 5 — WHO TO CONTACT

5.1 Report suspected pretexting to the site Principal and the District Records Custodian on the day it occurs.
5.2 Direct student records questions to the Director of Student Services; personnel and payroll questions to the Director of Human Resources; account, device, and transfer questions to the Chief Technology Officer; and questions of policy interpretation to the Assistant Superintendent for Business Services.
