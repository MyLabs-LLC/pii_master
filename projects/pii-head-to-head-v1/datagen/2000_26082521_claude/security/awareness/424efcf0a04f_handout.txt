GUIDANCE HANDOUT FOR FIELD ASSOCIATES
SUBJECT: PROTECTION OF PERSONAL IDENTIFIERS WHILE ON CLIENT ASSIGNMENT
ISSUING AUTHORITY: THE DIRECTOR OF HUMAN RESOURCES, IN CONSULTATION WITH THE PRIVACY OFFICER
EFFECTIVE: 2021 PLACEMENT YEAR

    A. Preliminary Statement
        Associates placed at a client worksite remain, at all times, subject to the Agency's handling
        standards, and are additionally bound by any security protocol the client may impose. The
        existence of a client instruction shall not be treated as authority to depart from the minimum
        standards set out below.

    B. Categories of Material Requiring Cross-Cut Destruction
        (i)   Onboarding paperwork, in whole or in part, once the corresponding record has been
              entered and verified in the placement system;
        (ii)  Any printout displaying the columns Associate Identifier, Social Security Number,
              Date of Birth, Home Address, or Pay Rate;
        (iii) Assignment rosters, call sheets, and shift confirmation lists;
        (iv)  Benefit enrollment forms, dependent verification worksheets, and beneficiary
              designation forms;
        (v)   Adhesive notes, index cards, and scratch paper on which an identifier was recorded
              in the course of a telephone conversation;
        (vi)  Envelopes and mailing labels bearing an associate's Home Address where the mailing
              was undeliverable or misdirected.

    C. Prohibited Electronic Transmission
        (i)   The following shall never be placed in the body of an electronic mail message, in an
              attachment, in a calendar invitation, or in a chat message: Social Security Number;
              Date of Birth; Driver's License Number; Bank Routing Number; Deposit Account Number;
              Payment Card Number; Background Check Reference Number.
        (ii)  Splitting a prohibited element across two or more messages does not cure the
              prohibition and shall be treated as a violation of equal gravity.
        (iii) Where an associate receives such an element by electronic mail from a third party,
              the associate shall not forward the message. The associate shall delete it, purge the
              deleted items folder, and report the receipt to the Information Security Lead.

    D. Identification of Pretexting Contact
        (i)   A pretexting caller seeks disclosure by fabricating identity or circumstance.
        (ii)  Consider the contact suspect where the caller: asserts an emergency requiring
              immediate action; declines to be called back at a number listed in the internal
              directory; professes familiarity with the branch but cannot name the Branch
              Operations Manager; requests that a Home Address, Direct Deposit Election, or
              Emergency Contact record be altered; or asks the associate to confirm that a
              particular individual is on assignment at a named client site.
        (iii) The correct response is to make no confirmation of any kind, to note the date and time
              of the contact and the substance of what was requested, and to escalate under Part E.

    E. Escalation by Role Title
        (i)   The Branch Operations Manager — first report of any suspicious contact or lost document.
        (ii)  The Privacy Officer — all suspected or confirmed unauthorized disclosure.
        (iii) The Information Security Lead — suspicious messages, credential prompts, malware.
        (iv)  The Records Custodian — retention schedules, secure destruction certificates, file access.
        (v)   The Talent Acquisition Supervisor — candidate-facing questions arising from a suspect contact.

    F. Effect
        This guidance supplements and does not supersede the Information Handling Policy. Associates
        requiring clarification shall seek it before acting, and not afterward.
