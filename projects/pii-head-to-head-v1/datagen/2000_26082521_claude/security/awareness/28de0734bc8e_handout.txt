MERIDIAN COAST TITLE & ESCROW COMPANY
DESK REFERENCE — PRETEXTING AND SOCIAL ENGINEERING
Distributed by the Information Security Officer to all escrow and title staff

A. WHAT PRETEXTING IS
    1. A caller invents a pretext — a role, an emergency, an authority — to
       obtain identifiers we hold in a file.
    2. The caller does not need the whole record. Partial disclosure is the
       objective; the pieces are assembled from several calls to several
       branches.
    3. Under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, obtaining customer information from
       a financial institution by false pretenses is unlawful. Assisting such
       a request, even unknowingly, is a reportable event.

B. WARNING SIGNS DURING A CALL
    1. Identity claims
        a. Claims a title the caller does not use correctly — for example,
           asking for "the file clerk" or "your account department."
        b. Refuses to give a callback number, or gives one that does not match
           the number of record for the lender or brokerage.
        c. Says a colleague "already approved" the release.
    2. Information behaviour
        a. Reads part of a field and asks you to complete it.
           - "I have the first digits of the Deposit Account Number here —
             can you read me the rest?"
        b. Asks you to confirm whether a name is associated with a file.
        c. Requests the Social Security Number, Date of Birth, or Wire
           Beneficiary Account for "verification."
    3. Pressure
        a. Signing is in progress; funds must move today.
        b. Threatens escalation to a manager or to the lender.
        c. Calls near the close of business, or during a known staff absence.

C. REQUIRED RESPONSE — FOLLOW IN ORDER
    1. Do not confirm, deny, or correct any field. Silence on a detail is not
       rude; it is the procedure.
    2. Record what you can: caller name given, company claimed, file or loan
       reference cited, time of call, and the request made.
    3. Say: "I am not able to release that by telephone. I will return the
       call through the number in our file."
    4. End the call. Place the return call only to the number of record —
       from the executed closing instructions or the lender's published
       contact page, never from caller ID or from the caller's statement.
    5. If the party of record has no knowledge of the call, stop and report.

D. THE SAME RULES APPLY TO
    1. Walk-ins presenting a business card and asking to "just look at" a file.
    2. Email requests to change disbursement or beneficiary fields.
    3. Faxes on lender letterhead requesting a Payoff Statement be sent to a
       new destination.
    4. Text messages or voicemail asking for a callback to a mobile number.

E. REPORTING
    1. Same business day, to the Branch Escrow Manager.
    2. Same business day, to the Information Security Officer, whether or not
       anything was disclosed.
    3. If any identifier was disclosed, the Privacy Coordinator opens an
       incident record and determines whether state notification duties apply.
    4. Do not discuss a suspected pretexting attempt with parties outside the
       company, including the party the caller claimed to represent, until the
       Compliance Officer has directed the response.
