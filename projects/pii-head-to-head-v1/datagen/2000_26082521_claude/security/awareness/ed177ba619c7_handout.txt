PROTECTING PERSONAL IDENTIFIERS IN THE STUDENT RECORD
Guidance for Staff of the Office of the Registrar and Affiliated Academic Departments
Issued 2011 · Supersedes the 2007 records handling circular

1. WHY THIS GUIDANCE EXISTS

The student record is a regulated record. The identifiers it contains — Social Security Number, Student Identification Number, Date of Birth, Place of Birth, Legal Name, Former Legal Name, Permanent Address, Local Address, Emergency Contact Name, Immigration Status, and Veteran Status — are the raw material of identity theft, and the university is answerable for each of them under federal law and under the identity theft prevention program adopted by the Board of Trustees. Nothing in this guidance requires judgment about whether a particular request is well intentioned. The procedures apply to every request, including those from colleagues, parents, employers, and persons identifying themselves as law enforcement.

2. DESTRUCTION OF PAPER AND MEDIA

Cross-cut destruction is required for every printed or written item that carries one or more identifiers listed above. In practice this means the daily print queue, the registration override forms once keyed, the residency determination worksheets, the grade change routing slips, the degree audit drafts, the enrollment verification drafts, and any handwritten note on which a staff member has jotted an identifier while on the telephone. Sticky notes are the most common failure in this office and deserve particular attention at the end of each day. Compact discs, thumb drives, and old backup tapes are never shredded in the paper consoles; they go to the Records Custodian for media destruction under the property control procedure, logged by asset tag. Do not take identifier-bearing material home, and do not carry it between buildings unless it is sealed and hand-delivered.

3. TRANSMISSION LIMITS

Electronic mail, instant messaging, and personal web mail are prohibited channels for the Social Security Number, the full Date of Birth, the Driver's License Number, the Bank Account Number, the Bank Routing Number, and any image of a passport, visa, birth certificate, or state identification card. The Student Identification Number may travel by campus mail but is not to be placed in the subject line of any message, printed on the outside of an envelope, or read aloud in a public service area. Facsimile transmission is permitted only to a machine in a locked office confirmed by the receiving department in advance, with the sender remaining at the machine until the transmission completes and the confirmation page prints.

4. RECOGNISING A PRETEXTING CALL

Pretexting is the practice of obtaining personal information by inventing a plausible reason to need it. The caller supplies a story rather than an authorisation. Common markers include: an urgent deadline that will be missed unless the record is released immediately; a claim to be calling on behalf of a busy senior administrator, who is named by title; a request that you confirm rather than provide, so the caller may say the identifier and you need only agree; a refusal to accept a return call to a number already in the record; unusual friendliness followed by irritation when the procedure is stated; and a call placed near closing time or during a known staffing gap. When two or more markers appear, end the substantive discussion, take no action on the account, write down what was asked, and transfer the call to the Associate Registrar for Records.

5. INTERNAL CONTACTS BY ROLE

Day-to-day questions about the release of a record go to the Records Custodian. Interpretation of directory information, consent forms, and third-party release go to the FERPA Compliance Coordinator. Suspected pretexting, a lost laptop, a misdirected message, or a container of unshredded output found unattended goes immediately to the Information Security Officer, with a copy of the report to the University Registrar. Matters involving suspected criminal activity are referred by the University Registrar to the Campus Police Records Unit; staff do not make that referral directly.
