COUNTY DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH — RECORDS PROTECTION UNIT

PROTECTING PERSONAL IDENTIFIERS: STAFF AND VISITOR GUIDANCE

I. WHAT COUNTS AS A PERSONAL IDENTIFIER
    A. Direct identifiers
        1. Full name, or last name with initial
        2. Social Security number, in whole or in part
        3. Medicaid or health plan member number
        4. Clinic chart number or immunization registry number
        5. Driver license or state identification number
    B. Indirect identifiers
        1. Date of birth
        2. Residential street address and unit number
        3. Home, work, or mobile telephone number
        4. Electronic mail address
        5. Any combination of the above that narrows a record to one household

II. WHAT TO SHRED
    A. Shred at the point of use, in the locked consoles marked CONFIDENTIAL DESTRUCTION
        1. Sign-in sheets after the daily roster is entered
        2. Appointment reminder cards returned undeliverable
        3. Draft correspondence bearing a member number
        4. Laboratory slips, worksheets, and hand tallies
        5. Adhesive labels and wristband stock printed in error
        6. Fax cover sheets and transmission confirmations
    B. Do not place in recycling, blue bins, or open trash
    C. Do not remove console contents; only the contracted vendor opens consoles
    D. If a console is full, notify the Office Manager the same working day

III. WHAT NEVER TO SEND BY ELECTRONIC MAIL
    A. Prohibited in the message body, subject line, or attachment
        1. Social Security number
        2. Member, chart, or case number paired with a name
        3. Date of birth paired with a name
        4. Scanned identification documents
        5. Full clinic rosters or line lists
    B. Permitted alternatives
        1. Internal secure file transfer, arranged through the Information Systems Coordinator
        2. Interoffice courier in a sealed envelope marked CONFIDENTIAL
        3. Reference by case number alone, with no name attached
    C. Additional rules
        1. Never forward a chain that contains identifiers further down the thread
        2. Never send departmental records to a personal mail account
        3. Verify the recipient list before sending to any distribution group

IV. RECOGNISING A PRETEXTING CALL
    A. Common approaches
        1. Caller claims to be from a state office and needs a record "right away"
        2. Caller states they already have most of the information and asks you to confirm the rest
        3. Caller applies time pressure, or invokes a supervisor by title
        4. Caller asks you to bypass the usual request form
        5. Caller offers a return number and asks you to disregard the one on file
    B. Required response
        1. Do not confirm or deny that a person is known to the department
        2. Do not read back any identifier, including partial numbers
        3. Take the caller's name, office, and stated purpose
        4. Advise that the request must come through the Records Custodian
        5. Terminate the call politely and report it the same day

V. WHO TO CONTACT
    A. Records Custodian — all requests for records, subpoenas, and release authorisations
    B. Privacy Officer — suspected disclosure, lost paper, misdirected mail or fax
    C. Information Systems Coordinator — secure transfer, encryption, device loss
    D. Office Manager — shredding consoles, storage, visitor access
    E. Health Officer — media inquiries and law enforcement contact

VI. IF YOU ARE UNSURE
    A. Stop before disclosing
    B. Ask the Privacy Officer
    C. A delayed answer is correctable; a disclosure is not
