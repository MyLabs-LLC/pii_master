MEMORANDUM

TO:       Member Contact Staff — Tellers, Member Service Representatives, Call Center
FROM:     Member Services Supervisor
DATE:     November 18, 2004
RE:       Telephone Verification and Pretexting

We have received several calls this quarter in which the caller was not the member. This memo sets out what to do on every call, in order.

STEP ONE — ASSUME NOTHING

The caller who says "I'm sure you can see my account right there" is telling you what to do. Do not follow the caller's lead. You control the order of the call.

STEP TWO — VERIFY BEFORE YOU CONFIRM

Ask for the identifiers on file. Ask the caller to state them; never read them aloud and ask for a yes or no. A caller who has stolen a statement can say yes.

Acceptable verification fields:

     Member Name
     Member Number
     Last four digits of Social Security Number
     Date of Birth
     Mother's Maiden Name
     Current Address of Record
     Recent transaction amount and date

Two matching fields are the minimum for balance inquiry. Four are required for any change to the file, and address changes additionally require written confirmation mailed to both the old and new address.

STEP THREE — LISTEN FOR THE PATTERN

A pretext caller usually does several of these things in one call:

     Creates urgency — a closing, a funeral, a flight, a lawyer waiting
     Becomes friendly, then irritated, when questioned
     Claims to be a relative "calling for" the member who is standing right there
     Claims to be from our own Collections, Audit, or Information Systems
     Asks you to confirm a partial number rather than supply one
     Asks for the information by fax to a number not on the account
     Says a manager already cleared it but cannot say which manager
     Calls back within the hour hoping for a different employee

No employee of this credit union will ever telephone you and ask for a member's Social Security Number, PIN, or password. Neither will a vendor. Neither will an examiner.

STEP FOUR — END IT CLEANLY

If verification fails, say: "I'm not able to release that information over the telephone. I can mail it to the address we have on file, or the member is welcome to come into any branch with photograph identification."

Do not argue. Do not explain which answer was wrong. Do not transfer the caller to another department, which only gives the caller a second attempt.

STEP FIVE — REPORT IT

Complete an incident note the same day. Record: Date of Call, Time of Call, Employee Taking Call, Account Type Requested, Information Requested, Information Released, Caller's Stated Relationship, Verification Fields Attempted. Route the note to the Security Officer with a copy to the Member Services Supervisor.

If information was released before you recognized the pretext, say so. We can place a watch on the account and notify the member the same day, but only if we know. There is no penalty for a report made promptly and in good faith.

Refresher training on this procedure is scheduled quarterly. Attendance is recorded by the Training Coordinator and is not optional for member contact staff.
