BILLING OFFICE STANDARD PRACTICE — IDENTIFIER SAFEGUARDS
     Article Four of the Patient Accounts Manual

     Section 1.  Custody.
          (a)  Each clerk is the custodian of the identifiers appearing on
               the work assigned to that clerk for the duration of the
               shift.
          (b)  Work in progress is secured in a locked drawer at meal
               relief and at the close of business. Terminals are signed
               off, not merely left at the menu screen.

     Section 2.  Destruction of paper and media.
          (a)  The following are shredded rather than discarded:
                    (i)    registration face sheets and admission slips;
                    (ii)   insurance verification and pre-certification
                           worksheets;
                    (iii)  explanation of benefits and remittance advice
                           copies;
                    (iv)   collection notes, skip-trace worksheets, and
                           returned mail bearing a Guarantor Address;
                    (v)    all reports listing Patient Name against
                           Account Number or Social Security Number.
          (b)  A shredder that produces long ribbons is not adequate for
               identifier-bearing paper. Use the cross-cut units or the
               locked consoles serviced under contract.
          (c)  Diskettes and printer ribbons are logged out to the Records
               Custodian. A ribbon carries a legible impression of every
               statement it has printed.

     Section 3.  Prohibited transmission.
          (a)  Electronic mail is not an approved channel for Social
               Security Number, Date of Birth, Account Number, Medical
               Record Number, Subscriber Identification, or Group Number,
               whether in the message body, the subject line, or an
               attached file.
          (b)  The prohibition extends to messages between employees of
               this institution, to messages to a payer, and to messages
               to an attorney or agency acting for the institution.
          (c)  Where a payer requests an extract, the request is routed to
               the Information Systems Security Coordinator, who arranges
               transmission by approved tape or dedicated line.

     Section 4.  Pretexting.
          (a)  Pretexting is the practice of obtaining identifiers by
               impersonation. Callers of this kind are patient, courteous,
               and well rehearsed.
          (b)  Phrases warranting caution include: "I just need to confirm
               the number you already have"; "my supervisor is standing
               here"; "the system is down at our end"; "I am calling from
               the corporate office and this is time sensitive."
          (c)  The prescribed response is uniform: offer to return the
               call to a number obtained from the file or the directory,
               and decline to proceed if that offer is refused.
          (d)  Under no circumstances is an identifier read aloud to an
               unverified caller, and under no circumstances is a caller's
               partial recitation confirmed or corrected.

     Section 5.  Referral.
          (a)  Refused calls and suspected pretexts: report to the Patient
               Accounts Manager the same day.
          (b)  Lost documents, missing reports, or unattended printouts:
               report to the Records Custodian.
          (c)  Any disclosure believed to have occurred: report directly
               to the Corporate Compliance Officer. No employee shall be
               disadvantaged for making such a report in good faith.
          (d)  Questions of interpretation are directed to the Business
               Office Director.
