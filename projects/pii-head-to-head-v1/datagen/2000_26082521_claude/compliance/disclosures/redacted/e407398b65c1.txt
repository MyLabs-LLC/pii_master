Request received at the Records Office counter by hand delivery on 14 March 2011 at 1:52 p.m., accompanied by Form R-27 (rev. 09/2009) bearing what purported to be the subject's authorizing signature. The requesting party asserted entitlement to the subject's complete academic file, including all conduct correspondence and instructor communications, on the basis of a civil matter said to be pending in county court. Intake staff logged the request under the standing procedure governing non-subpoenaed third-party requests and routed it to the Associate Registrar for threshold review the same afternoon. No fee was assessed at intake.

Threshold review completed 18 March 2011. The Associate Registrar found the authorization deficient in two respects: the signature block was undated, and the scope statement did not enumerate the categories of record to be released as required by internal procedure RC-4.2. A deficiency letter issued 21 March 2011 by first-class mail to the address of record, with a thirty-day cure period. No response was received within the cure period. The file was placed in suspense status on 25 April 2011 pending closure.

A corrected Form R-27 was submitted 9 May 2011 with a cover memorandum asserting that the original mailing had not been received. The Associate Registrar reopened the file the same day and referred the corrected authorization to the Office of University Counsel for concurrence, given the volume of conduct material implicated. Counsel returned the referral on 2 June 2011 with concurrence limited to the academic record proper, and with an express exclusion of all law enforcement unit records and all records containing information about other students. That limitation was adopted in full.

Partial release was effected on 15 June 2011. Sixty-three pages were produced; eleven pages were withheld in their entirety and nine pages were produced with the identifying particulars of other students obscured. The subject was notified of the disclosure by letter dated 15 June 2011 in accordance with the office's practice of contemporaneous notice, though such notice is not required where valid consent is on file. Copying fees at $0.25 per page were assessed in the amount of $15.75 and subsequently waived by the Registrar on 21 June 2011 on the grounds of administrative delay attributable to the office.

The requesting party filed no objection to the scope of the release within the fourteen-day window provided. The file was closed 27 June 2011 and transferred to the inactive series, retention schedule GS-5, disposition eligible seven years from closure.
