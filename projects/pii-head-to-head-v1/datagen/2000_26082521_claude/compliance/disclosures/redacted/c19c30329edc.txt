    3. STATEMENT OF GRIEVANCE
        The grievant asserts that for the four-week period beginning 05 July 2004,
        weekly schedules were posted later than the seventy-two hours required
        under Article 9, Section 3, on three of four occasions. The grievant
        further asserts that overtime hours available during the same period were
        assigned to a less senior technician on two dates without the senior
        employee first being offered the hours.

    4. MANAGEMENT POSITION
        Management acknowledges the late posting on the weeks beginning 05 July,
        12 July, and 26 July 2004, and attributes it to the transition to the
        new labor scheduling system during that period. Management disputes the
        overtime allegation, stating that the senior employee was contacted by
        telephone on both dates and declined, and that the declination was noted
        on the shift roster.

    5. INVESTIGATION
        5.1 Shift rosters for the period pulled 16 August 2004.
        5.2 Roster for 08 July 2004 shows a declination notation. Roster for
            21 July 2004 shows no such notation.
        5.3 Scheduling system audit log confirms posting timestamps for all
            four weeks in question.

    6. RESOLUTION
        6.1 Late posting: sustained. Management to issue a written commitment
            to the posting standard, effective 01 September 2004.
        6.2 Overtime for 08 July 2004: denied. Documentation supports the
            management position.
        6.3 Overtime for 21 July 2004: sustained. Make-whole payment of four
            hours at the applicable premium rate, processed in the pay period
            ending 25 September 2004.
        6.4 Precedent: none. Resolution is specific to this matter.
