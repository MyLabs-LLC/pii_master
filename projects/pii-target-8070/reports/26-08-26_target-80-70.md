# 80% precision was a threshold away, and the model was never the problem

## Results

Three arms, one evaluator, the eight sealed `data/2-eval` corpora. **Nothing was
retrained**: the two new arms differ from the incumbent in 61 float comparison
points and in nothing else.

| | baseline `scorecard61` | **`p80r70`** | `p80r90` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **micro F1** *(the ranker)* | 0.7299 | **0.8394** | **0.8474** |
| **micro precision** | 0.6360 | **0.9165** | 0.8844 |
| macro F0.5 | 0.6230 | **0.7093** | 0.7056 |
| macro F2 | **0.6651** | 0.6406 | 0.6564 |
| precision, macro over catalogue | 0.6244 | **0.7616** | 0.7456 |
| recall, macro over catalogue | **0.6773** | 0.5668 | 0.5872 |
| worst measurable priority recall | **0.6267** | 0.4830 | 0.4921 |
| prediction rate | 0.8181 | 0.7656 | 0.7855 |
| document recall / precision | 0.7975 / 0.8956 | *identical* | *identical* |
| one-core p95 | 4.029 ms | 4.029 ms | 4.029 ms |

Document-level metrics are identical across all three because the gate is upstream
of every tag head and no gate parameter moved.

### Per-tag precision ≥ 0.80, on the sealed corpora

| arm | pairs passing | of measurable |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| baseline | 102 | 210 |
| **`p80r70`** | **141** | 180 |
| `p80r90` | 139 | 181 |

The denominator moves because a model that fires less has fewer tag × corpus pairs
with 30+ predictions, and a pair with too few predictions is `NOT_MEASURABLE`
rather than failed. So the honest comparison is the **absolute** count: 141 passes
against 102, an extra 39 tag × corpus pairs conclusively above 80% precision.

### Where the remaining failures are

`p80r70`, per-tag precision:

| corpus | pass | fail |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `20000_pii_holdout` | 47 | 2 |
| `38937_openpii` | 27 | 1 |
| `30000_pii2_eval` | 46 | 7 |
| `10626_ai4privacy` | 20 | 18 |
| `10360_betterdataai` | **1** | **11** |

**29 of the 39 failures are on the two corpora with known labelling defects.** On
the other three, 120 of 130 measurable pairs clear 80% precision. `betterdataai` is
silver-labelled — its labels are themselves model output — and 85.5% of its
name-bearing rows carry `full_name` alone, which is why `middle_name` there tops
out near precision 0.004 at *any* threshold. That is a gold ceiling, not a model
ceiling, and it was measured before this run started.

Recall ≥ 0.70 passes 44 of 55 measurable pairs; 3 of the 11 failures are on
`datax` and `govdocs2`, which carry positive-only tag gold and can measure nothing
else.

### Both arms are formally blocked

`NO FEASIBLE ARM` under both policies. "Across the board" means every measurable
pair, and 141 of 180 is not 180 of 180. Nothing was promoted.

## TL;DR

- **The target was reachable on 54 of 61 tags by threshold alone** — measured on
  the calibration carve *before* anything was built, which is what separated
  "needs a threshold" from "needs a model".
- **Micro F1 0.7299 → 0.8394 and micro precision 0.6360 → 0.9165**, with no
  retraining, no new data, and no change in latency.
- **The model was never the problem.** Tags were shipping at precision the model
  did not require: `gender_and_sex` at 0.2689 when 0.9894 was reachable at the same
  recall, `geolocation` at 0.0173 against 0.9655, `religion` at 0.0215 against
  0.8278. The selector was choosing those points and recording them as successes.
- **The cost is recall**, and it is real: macro recall 0.6773 → 0.5668, worst
  priority recall 0.6267 → 0.4830. In a domain where a missed identifier is a
  reportable incident, that is a concession, and it is the reason both arms were
  built rather than one.
- **Keeping recall at 0.90 costs surprisingly little here**: `p80r90` scores
  *higher* micro F1 (0.8474) than `p80r70` at slightly lower precision. On sealed
  data the stricter arm is not the worse arm.
- **Still blocked**, on two corpora whose gold cannot support the target.
- **Precision had to be made gateable first** — the evaluator emits only recall per
  tag × corpus, so an 80% precision gate would have been `NOT_MEASURABLE`
  everywhere and blocked every arm while measuring none of them.

## Why `p80r90` beats `p80r70` on micro F1

It should not be surprising once stated. Micro F1 pools every document-tag
decision, so it is dominated by high-volume tags, and those are the ones that can
hold 0.90 recall *and* 0.80 precision comfortably. The relaxation to 0.70 buys
sixteen extra tags into the box, but those are rare tags contributing few
decisions, and it simultaneously lets the common tags give up recall they did not
need to give up. The result is better precision (0.9165 vs 0.8844) and worse micro
F1 (0.8394 vs 0.8474).

If the goal is the ranker, `p80r90` is the better arm. If the goal is literally
"80% precision across the board", `p80r70` clears two more precision pairs. They
are answers to different questions and this run declines to collapse them.

## Latency is carried, and that is a claim

Neither arm was re-benchmarked. `predict_cascade` computes `X @ tag_weights.T` for
all 61 heads unconditionally and then compares against the threshold vector; moving
a threshold changes which comparisons come out true, not how many are done. The
gate, the weights, the hashing and the read window are byte-identical to
`cascade_scorecard61`, whose measured one-core p95 is 4.0290 ms. A re-measurement
would be timing the same instruction sequence.

This is recorded on the arms as `latency_source` and `latency_note` rather than
presented as a fresh measurement.

## What is still open

- **`betterdataai` and `ai4privacy` cap the result.** 29 of 39 precision failures
  sit there. Either their tag gold is corrected, or they are declared unable to
  measure the tags they mislabel — a gold decision, not a tuning one. Until then
  "across the board" cannot pass on this suite whatever the model does.
- **Seven tags cannot reach the box at any threshold**: `username` (best precision
  0.7860 at the recall target — just short), `county`, `country_of_origin`,
  `employment_status`, `nationality`, `sexual_identity_and_orientation`,
  `swift_code`. These need a better head or better gold.
- **The recall cost has not been priced against the incident it protects
  against.** This run can say macro recall fell 0.11; it cannot say what that
  costs in missed reportable identifiers, and nobody should infer it from these
  numbers.
- **The two interval estimators** — bootstrap for recall, Wilson for precision —
  live in one policy. Defensible, and worth revisiting if per-tag precision ever
  gets a bootstrap in the evaluator itself.
